10:T523,<p>Judging a source by leaving its page — opening new tabs to ask the wider web what it says of the source, instead of reading the source's own account of itself.</p>
<p>The move came from watching fact-checkers beat PhD historians at judging live websites: the experts read down the page and were fooled by official logos and .org URLs; the checkers barely read it, and went sideways to the neighbors first (<a href="/castle-gate/rooms/the-landing">the-landing</a>). It is the load-bearing move of the castle's credibility wing, and the castle has marked its edges three times. It judges a source by its reputation, so on the true frontier, where the wider web has not yet met the source, there are no neighbors to ask (<a href="/castle-gate/rooms/no-neighbors">no-neighbors</a>). And reputation can be met but met wrongly — a well-reputed finding that will not replicate — where internal auditing of method and evidence does the work lateral reading cannot (<a href="/castle-gate/rooms/auditing-over-reputation">auditing-over-reputation</a>). Even on the populated web it is the weaker of two complements, not the whole tool (<a href="/castle-gate/rooms/leaving-the-page">leaving-the-page</a>).
