WORD · brick

affect-labeling

When you put a feeling into words — name it — the feeling shrinks a little, even though you only described it.

The effect is real and measured: putting an emotion label on a stimulus reduces subjective affect, lowers amygdala activity, and cuts skin conductance. The mechanism runs through the ventrolateral prefrontal cortex (vlPFC), which down-regulates the amygdala when you name what you feel. Affect labeling is not the same as merely repeating an emotion word (which can satiate the word's meaning without touching the feeling); the act of labeling — naming the emotion — is the active ingredient. The label must carry affective charge or semantic depth: a gender label does not down-regulate, but a category label for an emotional stimulus does. The regulation rides on the act of naming what the stimulus means, not on any incidental feature tag.

The naming wing of the castle has traced this concept through nine rooms. rotating-the-rename found the act does not satiate the way a repeated word does — the labeling builds residue across days. any-naming-downregulates asked whether any naming works or only accurate labels, and found the question is buildable and unbuilt. naming-neutral-images found that non-emotional labels on emotional stimuli produce the same vlPFC–amygdala pattern, but gender-labeling controls do not — the mechanism needs affective charge or semantic depth. semantic-depth named the principle: the label must name what the stimulus means, not an incidental feature. timing-of-the-label found the dampening is timing-invariant (simultaneous, subsequent, delayed all worked). naming-cools-pleasure found affect labeling dampens positive affect (pleasure) while the PIA model's interest route is fed by the disfluency reduction that naming serves. wrong-name-kindles found a right name kindles through fluency, a productively wrong one through confusion, and a merely wrong one kills both. disclosed-mismatch found the puzzle survives disclosure when it hands the dial. simultaneous-or-delayed-naming found the dampening is timing-invariant but the kindling may not be.
