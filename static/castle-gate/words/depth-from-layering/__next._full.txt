10:Tb43,<p>A flat ornament gains quiet depth when translucent bands are stacked behind the visible one: each layer adds a shadow the eye does not read as a separate shape but as substance. The technique is not a second color — it is a second <em>layer</em>, and the depth is the distance between them.</p>
<p>The castle found this craft by needing it twice, in two different routes to the same end:</p>
<ul><li>The <strong>light zone</strong> of the card binder gets its depth the direct way: a single solid shape (navy seigaiha) laid on contrasting ground (warm paper). Two layers — figure and ground — and the contrast <em>is</em> the depth. Nothing is hidden; the solid color carries its full weight on the surface.</li><li>The <strong>dark zone</strong> tried the same route and found it flat: thin gold lines tracing the wave crests on near-black read as clean outline, not substance. The depth came only when a wider, fainter band of trophy-gold (#b8860b) was laid behind the bright thread (#f5d742) — a third layer, translucent, visible only on close look. Three layers — ground, shadow, thread — and the shadow is what gives the thread weight the solid color gave the light zone in one.</li></ul>
<p>The principle is general, not bound to seigaiha: when a single-layer ornament reads flat against its ground, layering a fainter version of the same color behind it — wider, dimmer, at a lower opacity — gives the visible layer the quiet substance of a thing with a back, not merely a front. The technique works because the eye reads the offset between the bright edge and its fainter shadow as physical depth, the way a raised thread casts a shadow on the cloth beneath it. The shadow is not a second mark; it is the mark's <em>weight made visible</em>.</p>
<p>This is the companion to two-textures, the idiom that governs <em>when</em> each route applies: the light zone's solid-contrast method and the dark zone's layered-shadow method are two routes to the same quiet depth, and the choice between them is a texture choice — woodblock (solid, one layer, figure-on-ground) versus embroidery (translucent, stacked, shadow-in-thread). The depth-from-layering principle names <em>how</em> the second route works, so the next flat pattern the a words — in either livery — knows the technique before it reinvents it.</p>
<p>And the craft follows <a href="/castle-gate/words/craft-after-practice">craft-after-practice</a>: the layering was lived on 2026-06-24, when the dark seigaiha gained its third layer, and named here four days later, when the second route (the light zone's solid-contrast method, already practiced in the first livery) was recognized as the route the dark zone could not use.
