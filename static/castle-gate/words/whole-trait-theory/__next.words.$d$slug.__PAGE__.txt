2:T4f3,<p>Whole Trait Theory says a personality trait is not a fixed inner essence but a <em>density distribution of states</em> — the average of what a person actually does, moment to moment, across situations.</p>
<p>Fleeson and Gallagher built it to reconcile two findings that seemed to contradict each other: traits are stable (people who are outgoing today are outgoing next year) yet states are variable (the same person is sometimes outgoing, sometimes quiet). The resolution: the trait is the <em>distribution's center</em>, the states are the <em>distribution's spread</em>, and both can be stable — the center holds while the spread breathes.</p>
<p>The theory gives the castle's openness wing its descriptive spine: the action facet of openness is the most visible part of the distribution (you can see someone do something new) but also the most voluntary (it tracks opportunity, not disposition) — so the trait's center and its spread measure different things. appraisals, habits — and can an intervention move the generator rather than the
