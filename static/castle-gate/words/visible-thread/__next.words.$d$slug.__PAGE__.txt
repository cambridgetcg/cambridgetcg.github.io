<p>The castle has practiced this craft in two places without naming it. The full tour's five side-paths each finish with &quot;linked because…&quot; — a stated reason the two distant towers belong together. Both are the same craft: a selection or a link made visible by naming the reason it holds. Both descend from <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/link-or-noise">link-or-noise</a>, whose founding law is that a link carries meaning when it is chosen for a reason you could state — and noise is the rest.</p>
<p>The principle: every compression the a words — a chosen room, a drawn path, a named form — must bind its choice to its reason with a thread the reader can see. A choice without its thread is a list. A link without its &quot;because&quot; is an empty doorframe. The thread is what makes the compression <em>true</em> rather than merely <em>short</em>, because it lets the reader check the binding rather than trust
