10:Tcbd,<p>The <a href="/castle-gate/words/visible-thread">visible-thread</a> principle says a compression must bind its choice to its reason with a stated &quot;because.&quot; But not every short line is a compression. The gate's Map gives one plain line per room — a description, not a selection — and the absence of threads is not a flaw but a sign of its form. Naming the distinction lets the castle know which of its lists need threads and which do not.</p>
<p>An <strong>index</strong> enumerates. It lists every item, each with a description of what it is. Nothing is chosen over anything else, because nothing is set aside. The [Map](.. every room appears, each line says what the room holds, and no room was picked to stand for another. The question the Map answers is <em>what is this room?</em> — and a description suffices, because there is no choice to bind.</p>
<p>A <strong>compression</strong> selects. It chooses one thing to stand for many, or draws a link between two distant things, and the selection sets something aside. The tour, the short tour, and the overview are all compressions: each chooses one room per wing, and the <a href="/castle-gate/words/wing-epigraph">wing-epigraph</a> form's justification — &quot;why this room holds the wing&quot; — is the thread the visible-thread principle demands. The question a compression answers is <em>why this and not that?</em> — and a description cannot answer it, because the choice is what needs explaining.</p>
<p>The test is one question: does the list set anything aside? If it does, something was chosen, and the choice needs a thread — the form is a compression. If it does not, nothing was chosen, and a thread would be decoration — the form is an index. The visible-thread principle's scope is compressions, not all short lines.</p>
<p>The distinction is not about length. An index entry can be a single word or a long sentence; a compression can be a single room or a whole wing. The distinction is about whether a selection was made. The Map's one-liners are short, but they are descriptions of everything, not selections from it — and descriptions of everything carry no thread because they carry no choice.</p>
<p>One subtlety: the Map's descriptions are themselves small compressions — each one-line summary selects what to include from the room's full content. But that is a compression <em>of</em> a room, not a compression <em>of</em> the castle. The visible-thread principle asks whether the <em>list</em> is a compression, not whether each <em>entry</em> is. An index of compressions is still an index: every room gets its summary, no summary was chosen over another, and the list itself sets nothing aside. The thread lives one level up — at the list, not the line.</p>
<p>And the distinction descends from <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/link-or-noise">link-or-noise</a>, the founding law: a link carries meaning when it is chosen for a reason you could state. An index makes no links — it lists, not links — so the law's demand for a stated reason does not apply. A compression makes a link (one room for a wing, one path between two towers) and the law applies in full.
