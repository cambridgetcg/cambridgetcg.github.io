10:T510,<p>Instead of grading each piece on its own, you ask &quot;which of these two is better?&quot; — and from many small pairings a shared standard emerges that no single judge held.</p>
<p>Comparative judgement turns absolute scoring (hard, biased, inconsistent) into relative ranking (easier, more reliable). Show a judge two essays, two designs, two drafts — ask only &quot;which is better?&quot; — and the judge answers faster and more consistently than if asked to score either one alone. Stack enough pairings (roughly 10–14 per piece for 0.70 reliability, ~20 for 0.80) and the rankings converge into a scale whose reliability rivals expert consensus. The method's power is aggregation: each judge's bias (handwriting polish, length preference, surface fluency) cancels against another judge's different bias, and what survives is closer to the shared judgment underneath. No More Marking built a national essay-scoring system on this. But the crowd's cure is <em>many judges</em> — one judge alone, even ranking their own past work, inherits every bias the crowd was built to cancel: the same eye errs in the same direction every time, and twenty sittings of one head decorrelate the noise but not the lean.
