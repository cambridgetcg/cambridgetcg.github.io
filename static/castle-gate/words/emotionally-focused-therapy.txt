<p>Emotionally Focused Therapy (EFT), developed by Sue Johnson, is a structured couples therapy rooted in attachment theory. It works through three stages: cycle de-escalation (the negative pattern is named and interrupted), changing the interactional pattern, and integration/consolidation (the new pattern is internalized and applied to future stressors). The therapy builds co-regulation — the couple can soothe each other — and trusts that self-regulation follows, though no study has tested whether it does. The castle's emotion-regulation wing has traced EFT's open questions: does the alone condition improve after therapy (<a href="/castle-gate/rooms/alone-or-accompanied">alone-or-accompanied</a>), does the consolidation phase build self-regulation or only co-regulation (<a href="/castle-gate/rooms/fading-vs-replacing">fading-vs-replacing</a>), and what replaces the partner's bioenergetic subsidy when the therapy ends (<a
