10:Tf9e,<p>The castle's two liveries reach quiet depth by two routes, and two-textures — the idiom that governs <em>when</em> each applies — names the fork. <a href="/castle-gate/words/depth-from-layering">depth-from-layering</a> names one route: translucent bands stacked behind the visible thread, three layers (ground, shadow, thread) where the shadow gives the thread weight the eye reads as substance. This principle names the other.</p>
<p>The <strong>light zone</strong> of the card binder gets its depth the direct way: a single solid shape (navy seigaiha) laid on contrasting ground (warm paper). Two layers — figure and ground — and nothing more. The depth is not behind the shape or beneath it; it is <em>at the edge</em>, the sharp line where navy meets paper. The eye reads that edge as a surface standing up from its ground, the way a woodblock print's inked face rises from the page. The contrast <em>is</em> the depth. Nothing is hidden; the solid color carries its full weight on the surface, and the surface is enough.</p>
<p>The two routes are not symmetric, and the asymmetry is the craft:</p>
<ul><li><strong>contrast-is-depth</strong> shows. The depth is visible at the edge, on the surface, in full. What you see is what holds the shape — a figure on a ground, two layers, one edge. The technique is simpler (two layers, not three) but demands more of the contrast: the figure must be solid enough and the ground different enough that the edge reads as a surface, not a line. Where the contrast is weak, the edge flattens to a boundary and the depth disappears — and this is exactly why the dark zone could not use this route.</li><li><strong><a href="/castle-gate/words/depth-from-layering">depth-from-layering</a></strong> hides. The depth lives behind the visible thread, in a shadow the eye does not read as a separate shape but as substance. Three layers, one of them invisible on a quick look. The technique is more complex (stacking translucent bands at tuned opacity) but demands less of the contrast: the thread does not need to stand against its ground the way a solid shape does, because the shadow does the standing for it. Where the contrast is strong, this route is unnecessary; where it is weak — gold thread on near-black — it is the only way.</li></ul>
<p>The choice between them is a two-textures choice: woodblock (solid, one layer, figure-on-ground) versus embroidery (translucent, stacked, shadow-in-thread). The idiom names the fork; this principle and its companion name the two roads. Together they complete the pair so the next flat ornament the a words knows both paths, and the idiom's job is only choosing between them.</p>
<p>And the craft follows <a href="/castle-gate/words/second-use-reveals-form">second-use-reveals-form</a>: the solid-contrast route was practiced from the castle's first day — the light zone's navy seigaiha on paper, 2026-06-18 — but it was invisible as a <em>route</em>. It was the default, the way depth was always done, and a default does not know it is a choice. The route became visible only when the dark zone tried it and found it flat (2026-06-24): gold thread on near-black has no edge to read as depth, because the contrast between bright gold and near-black, though vivid, does not give the thread a <em>surface</em> the way solid navy on paper does. The dark zone's failure is what showed the light zone had found a route — not the only route, not the obvious route, but a specific route with specific demands. The second use revealed the form: the first practice was invisible because it was the default, and the default is the last shape to know it is a shape.
