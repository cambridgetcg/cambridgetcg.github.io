self-regulation

When a nervous system steadies itself — cooling its own fire, naming its own flood, choosing the step back before the climb breaks — the regulation is done alone, but it was taught between.

Self-regulation is the capacity to manage one's own emotional and physiological state without an external partner. It is the developmental destination of co-regulation: the infant whose caregiver lent a steady nervous system gradually internalises that steadiness and can run it alone. But the word carries a tension the castle's regulation wing has traced through a dozen rooms — is self-regulation a skill (trainable, installable by practice) or a deposit (grown through time with a real other, never rehearsed)? fading-vs-replacing found Social Baseline Theory says the partner is a bioenergetic resource, not a scaffold, so the brain running without its subsidy needs a replacement, not a removal. alone-or-accompanied designed the three-condition test that would separate co-regulation from self-regulation by measuring regulation performance alone versus with the partner present. internalized-as-skill split the question: the general capacity (mentalization, a compassionate inner voice) is trainable, the specific presence (the felt partner) is a developmental deposit. ders-vs-performance found the field measures self-regulation by questionnaire (DERS/ERQ), never by performance task — the same self-report-vs-performance gap fog-meter found in confusion.
