10:T983,<p>When a repeated act leaves a trace that makes the next cheaper, the trace can be a lever you pull or a readout you measure — and which one it is depends on whether the act builds the bond or the bond generates the act.</p>
<p>A lever is what you practice into existence: the act generates the bond, so doing the act again and again builds the capacity, and each pull leaves residue that lowers the next pull's cost. Reappraisal is a lever — you do it, it leaves a trace, the trace makes the next reappraisal cheaper, and the practice is the mechanism. A readout is what you measure but cannot pull: the bond generates the act, so the act's trace is evidence of the bond, not fuel for it. Relational repair is a readout — the repair rate predicts who thrives, but coaching the repair does not improve outcomes, because the repairing is not the mechanism; the bond is the mechanism, and the repairing is its face.</p>
<p>The direction of causation is decided by one question: are the act and the bond the same thing or different things? When the act <em>is</em> the bond (reappraising is the regulation capacity), residue is a lever — practice the act, the bond grows, the next act is cheaper. When the act <em>is not</em> the bond (a repair is an event, the bond is a structure built from the history of such events), residue is a readout — the bond generates the act, the act's trace is evidence of the bond, and practicing the act does not build the bond because the act was never the mechanism. The distinction is not about depth — residue holds in both cases. The distinction is about <em>what the residue is residue of</em>: residue of the act (lever) or residue of the bond (readout). A deep label leaves residue of the act. A deep repair leaves residue of the bond. Both are deep; only one is a lever.</p>
<p>This is the same smooth-or-thresholded question, one level deeper. The lever builds smoothly — each practice adds a trace, the cost curve descends by the power law of practice. The readout clicks at a threshold of time-with-other, not at a threshold of practice — you cannot reach it by pulling the lever harder, only by being with the other long enough.
