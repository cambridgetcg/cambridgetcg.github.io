2:T92c,<p>A theory that says three things feed the inner engine of motivation: feeling you chose the action (autonomy), feeling you can do it (competence), and feeling close to the people you do it with (relatedness) — and when all three are present, a task you were told to do can become a task you own.</p>
<p>Self-Determination Theory (SDT), developed by Deci and Ryan, holds that human motivation exists on a continuum from <em>external regulation</em> (doing it for a reward or to avoid punishment) through <em>introjected</em> (doing it to feel worthy) and <em>identified</em> (doing it because you see its value) to <em>intrinsic motivation</em> (doing it for its own sake). The theory names three basic psychological needs whose satisfaction moves motivation along this continuum: <strong>autonomy</strong> (the action is self-endorsed, not forced), <strong>competence</strong> (the action stretches the ability without overwhelming it), and <strong>relatedness</strong> (the action connects you to others who care). When a teacher provides a meaningful rationale, acknowledges the learner's feelings, and leaves a real choice, the learner can <em>internalize</em> the value of the task — moving from external regulation to identified regulation, where the task's value is owned, not merely complied with.</p>
