<p>Self-regulation is the capacity to manage one's own emotional and physiological state without an external partner. It is the developmental destination of co-regulation: the infant whose caregiver lent a steady nervous system gradually internalises that steadiness and can run it alone. But the word carries a tension the castle's regulation wing has traced through a dozen rooms — is self-regulation a skill (trainable, installable by practice) or a deposit (grown through time with a real other, never rehearsed)? <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/fading-vs-replacing">fading-vs-replacing</a> found Social Baseline Theory says the partner is a bioenergetic resource, not a scaffold, so the brain running without its subsidy needs a replacement, not a removal. <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/alone-or-accompanied">alone-or-accompanied</a> designed the three-condition test that would separate
