<p>A child who can read simple words easily may yawn; the same child, given a hard word that takes three tries, lights up when it cracks — that light is interest. The PIA model names it the second route to aesthetic liking: where <a href="/castle-gate/words/fluency">fluency</a> gives pleasure for free, interest is earned by the effort of reducing disfluency. The two can pull opposite ways — the easy chord bores the trained ear, the difficult one thrills it — and expertise tilts the weighting toward interest, which is why the trained palate chooses against its own gut.</p>
