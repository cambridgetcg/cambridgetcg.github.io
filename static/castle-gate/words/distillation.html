WORD · brick

distillation

Shrinking a piece of knowledge until it fits the mind meant to hold it — boiling a tacit skill down to one nameable contrast, a low-complexity handle a beginner can carry away.

It is the hinge the teaching rooms turn on. A tacit feature becomes teachable only when someone distills it — the chick-sexer's whole craft reduced to convex versus concave (naming-the-tacit); the distiller was always a third party, and now can be an optimizer whose loss is the learner, which makes distillation a computable score (machine-distillation) — but only as far as that learner is modelable, and a machine student's window is not a human's (two-windows). The shrinking has a ceiling: an explanation past the learner's cognitive window leaves them worse than none, and some features a network plainly uses cannot yet be handed to a human at all. What survives the boiling is what training-the-eye points at, what what-must-travel packs lightest, and what a borrowed standard-without-a-key presses concrete.
