10:T531,<p>The timeline a threat follows — from the first flicker of the amygdala to the body's full flood to the long cooling afterward — and the two points on it where regulation can act: before the flood (prevention) or after (recovery).</p>
<p>A partner's presence works at the front of the arc: the brain, perceiving a trusted other, does not mount the full threat response in the first place — Social Baseline Theory calls this a bioenergetic subsidy, the vigilance offloaded and the metabolic cost shared. A trained inner voice works at the back: compassion-focused therapy activates the soothing system to counter-regulate the threat already felt, and heart rate variability measures the parasympathetic recovery. The question the EFT wing keeps turning on is whether the trained general voice can prevent a threat response the way a partner's presence does — or whether it can only help the body recover from one already underway. The two mechanisms are physiologically distinct, and no study has compared them on the same timeline.</p>
