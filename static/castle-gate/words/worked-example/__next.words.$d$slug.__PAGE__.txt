<p>Cognitive load theory's worked-example effect is one of its best-validated findings: novices learn faster from worked examples than from problem-solving, because the examples reduce extraneous load and provide an expert mental model to follow. But the effect reverses with expertise (the expertise reversal effect): as learners gain knowledge, the examples become redundant and can harm performance. Worked examples help most when paired with self-explanation prompts — the learner generates their own explanation of each step, and passive self-explainers gain little.
