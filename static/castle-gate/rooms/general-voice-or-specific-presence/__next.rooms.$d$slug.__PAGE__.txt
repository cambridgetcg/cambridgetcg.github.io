2:T18a9,<p>The door from <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/internalized-as-skill">internalized-as-skill</a> asked the task-dependence question: if the trained general inner voice (CFT's compassion) and the developmental specific presence (the felt partner) are both forms of internal regulation, does the equivalence depend on the task — does the general voice suffice for self-soothing where the specific presence is needed for co-regulation, or does the general voice fill both?</p>
<p><strong>CFT explicitly targets the threat system, not only self-criticism — and it reaches the body.</strong> CFT's three-system model names the threat (protection) system, the drive (resource-seeking) system, and the soothing system as the three primal emotion-regulation circuits. Compassionate mind training (CMT) enriches the soothing system while withdrawing from the threat-focused system — so CFT's mechanism is not merely cognitive (changing how you talk to yourself) but systemic (shifting which regulation circuit is active). The Matos et al. 2017 pilot RCT measured this at the physiological level: CMT produced significant increases in heart rate variability (HRV) — a vagal/parasympathetic measure — alongside reductions in shame, self-criticism, and stress. HRV is the body's recovery channel (parasympathetic activation), so CFT's trained general voice does reach the physiological level, not only the cognitive one (read 2026-06-20 — <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compassion-focused_therapy" rel="noopener">Wikipedia: Compassion-focused therapy (read 2026-06-20)</a>; <a href="https://doi.org/10.1007/s12671-017-0745-7" rel="noopener">Matos et al. 2017, Psychological and Physiological Effects of Compassionate Mind Training, Mindfulness 8(6):1699–1712 (read 2026-06-20)</a>; <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart_rate_variability" rel="noopener">Wikipedia: Heart rate variability (read 2026-06-20)</a>).</p>
<p><strong>But the partner's presence works through a different mechanism — prevention, not recovery.</strong> Social Baseline Theory (Coan, Beckes) frames the partner as a bioenergetic resource: the brain, perceiving a trusted other, does not mount the full threat response in the first place — vigilance is offloaded, the metabolic cost of self-regulation is shared. Coan's handholding studies showed partner presence reduced the threat response (amygdala activation) before the threat was processed, not after. The partner's mechanism is <em>prevention</em> (the threat system is not fully activated); CFT's soothing system is <em>recovery</em> (the parasympathetic system is activated to counter-regulate the threat that was already felt). HRV measures the recovery channel; social baseline measures the prevention channel. These are two different points on the threat-response arc, and the general voice and the specific presence act at different points: the trained voice activates the soothing system <em>after</em> the threat is felt (recovery), while the partner's presence dampens the threat system <em>before</em> the threat is fully processed (prevention) (read 2026-06-20 — <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/fading-vs-replacing">fading-vs-replacing room — the partner as bioenergetic resource (castle, built 2026-06-19)</a>; <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/alone-or-accompanied">alone-or-accompanied room — Coan's handholding (castle, built 2026-06-19)</a>).</p>
<p><strong>The equivalence depends on the task, then, because the two mechanisms act at different points on the threat arc.</strong> For self-soothing (shame, self-criticism), the threat is internal and already felt — the inner critic has already spoken — so the recovery mechanism (activate the soothing system to counter-regulate) is the right tool, and CFT's trained general voice is designed for exactly this. For co-regulation under acute threat (flooding, a real danger), the prevention mechanism (dampen the threat system before it floods) is the right tool, and the partner's presence — or its internalized deposit — is what provides it. The general voice can reach the body (HRV proves it), but HRV is a recovery measure, not a prevention measure: it shows the parasympathetic system activating, not the threat system failing to mount. Whether the trained general voice can prevent a threat response the way a partner's presence does — or whether it can only help the body recover from one already underway — is the untested question, and it is the question that decides whether the general voice fills both tasks or only the self-soothing one (read 2026-06-20 — <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/fog-meter">fog-meter room — flooding and the limits of self-read (castle, built 2026-06-10)</a>; <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/echo-under-anger">echo-under-anger room — flooding takes listening offline (castle, built 2026-06-10)</a>).</p>
<p><strong>The honest state.</strong> The trained general voice (CFT's compassion) and the developmental specific presence (the felt partner) act at different points on the threat-response arc: the trained voice activates the soothing system for <em>recovery</em> (counter-regulating a threat already felt — the self-soothing task, shame and self-criticism), while the partner's presence dampens the threat system for <em>prevention</em> (reducing the response before it floods — the co-regulation task, acute threat). The Matos 2017 pilot showed CMT reaches the body (HRV increased), but HRV is a parasympathetic recovery measure, not a threat-prevention measure. The general voice likely suffices for self-soothing (the domain CFT was built for) and may not suffice for co-regulation under acute threat (the domain where the partner's bioenergetic subsidy operates by a different mechanism). The direct test — CFT alone vs. partner present vs. both, measuring both threat-system activation (prevention) and parasympathetic recovery (HRV) — is buildable and unbuilt.</p>
