2:T1420,<p>The premise has aged out. Distancing has long since been walked onto live ground: speakers with five minutes' notice who coached themselves by name felt less dread and were rated better by blind judges (read 2026-06-10 — <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24467424/" rel="noopener">Kross et al., JPSP 2014</a>); provoked people who stepped back while still hot showed less anger and less aggression (read 2026-06-10 — <a href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0022103112000601" rel="noopener">Mischkowski, Kross &amp; Bushman 2012</a>); and under a live evaluative stressor the body itself tipped from threat toward challenge — an engaged approach state, not a muted retreat (read 2026-06-10 — <a href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0022103116307764" rel="noopener">Streamer et al. 2017</a>).</p>
<p>And the step costs less than the worry says. Third-person self-talk damped the brain's reactivity marker within a second of an aversive image <em>without</em> raising any marker of cognitive control — the authors call it a relatively effortless form of self-control (read 2026-06-10 — <a href="https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-017-04047-3" rel="noopener">Moser et al., Scientific Reports 2017</a>). Where distance met persistence head-on, it built grip rather than spending it: children at a dull task, an iPad glowing nearby, held out longest as Batman and least as &quot;I&quot; (read 2026-06-10 — <a href="https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/cdev.12695" rel="noopener">White et al. 2017</a>); adults advising themselves as &quot;you&quot; solved more anagrams (read 2026-06-10 — <a href="https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/ejsp.2048" rel="noopener">Dolcos &amp; Albarracín 2014</a>) — though a Japanese-language extension found nothing, the pronouns felt foreign (read 2026-06-10 — <a href="https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0305251" rel="noopener">PLOS One 2024</a>), so the trick may lean on the grammar it was born in.</p>
<p>The real cost sits elsewhere. Distance dims both valences: across 38 studies, the third-person view lowers the intensity of positive feeling along with negative (read 2026-06-10 — <a href="https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2016.01715/full" rel="noopener">Wallace-Hadrill &amp; Kamboj 2016</a>), and distancing's own architects concede that when the goal is to feel more, or to turn feeling into action, it is the wrong tool (read 2026-06-10 — <a href="https://sites.lsa.umich.edu/emotion-selfcontrol-psych/wp-content/uploads/sites/1322/2024/01/Handbook_of_Emotion_Regulation_-_63._Distancing_What_It_Is_How_It_Works_and_Where_to_Go_Next.pdf" rel="noopener">Kross &amp; Ayduk 2023</a>). This is the dimmer's one limit, and the wall holds a second knob: <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/renaming-the-fire">renaming-the-fire</a> renames the heat instead of dimming it, so the fuel can stay lit while only its fear-soaked label changes. The habit has a shadow too: people who recall trauma from the observer's seat <em>by default</em> do worse, not better (read 2026-06-10 — <a href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0005796706002117" rel="noopener">Williams &amp; Moulds 2007</a>). Chosen distance regulates; default distance avoids.</p>
<p>So: the step back holds mid-task and spends no grip — on stress. On live epistemic confusion, mid-impasse, no study exists. The hedge the evidence supports: lean back when feeling is degrading the work — panic, spiral, the urge to quit (<a href="/castle-gate/rooms/flooding-self-read">flooding-self-read</a>'s territory) — and stay in your own eyes while absorption itself is the fuel.</p>
<h2>What stays uncertain</h2>
<p>uncertain: the exact case — a distanced eye on a live problem-solving impasse, where working memory is the bottleneck — is untested; every mid-task win above is stress, provocation, images, or boredom. Moser's &quot;no extra control&quot; is an ERP signature, not a dual-task load: absence of the marker is not proof of no cost. Effects replicate but can run small — a live-provocation athlete study found the predicted drops yet called the differences small (read 2026-06-10 — <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7819956/" rel="noopener">Hauck et al. 2020</a>) — and Kross &amp; Ayduk themselves list &quot;when is distancing suboptimal&quot; as an open question.</p>
