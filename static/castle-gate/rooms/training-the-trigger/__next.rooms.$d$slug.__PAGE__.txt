2:T1806,<p>By default, nobody asks. Developmental psychology has watched this for decades and named it the production deficiency: a learner who runs a strategy perfectly when cued simply does not reach for it alone (<a href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0065240708600518" rel="noopener">Waters, production deficiency review</a>, read 2026-06-10). The evaluative-judgement field confesses the matching gap at its foundation — assessment as practiced turns out graduates &quot;dependent on others' assessment of their work,&quot; unable to identify criteria for a new context (<a href="https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10734-017-0220-3" rel="noopener">Tai et al. 2017</a>, read 2026-06-10). The studies hand the learner the standard because handing it over is the part anyone knows how to do. And where unprompted seeking has been studied, the finding runs cruelly backwards: the students who most need evaluative help are the likeliest to avoid asking for it (<a href="https://link.springer.com/article/10.1023/A:1009013420053" rel="noopener">Ryan, Pintrich &amp; Midgley</a>, read 2026-06-10).</p>
<p>Yet the asking can be trained, partly. Hybrid metacognitive training has produced genuinely spontaneous transfer — students carrying the trained questions, uncued, to tasks near and far (<a href="https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11409-022-09322-x" rel="noopener">Metacognition and Learning 2022</a>, read 2026-06-10). A 2025 review argues this is what strategy instruction always missed: a strategy survives only as a habit, a semi-automatic act bound to a recurring cue — knowing it is never the trigger (<a href="https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-025-10087-3" rel="noopener">Educational Psychology Review 2025</a>, read 2026-06-10). That is <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/friction-decides">friction-decides</a> carried indoors: the same quiet tax that decides which habits live decides whether a trained question survives, cue by gently faded cue.</p>
<p>But &quot;unprompted&quot; is manufactured, and the manufacture is fading. Students trained on self-question prompts drop the strategy when the prompts vanish all at once; independence comes from withdrawing the cue gradually, the bell growing softer until the morning light suffices (<a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7367087/" rel="noopener">self-questioning review</a>, read 2026-06-10). The best-known study to test persistence after the prompts were gone found the effect still standing — thin evidence, mildly positive (<a href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0747563215004197" rel="noopener">Bannert et al. 2015</a>, read 2026-06-10).</p>
<p>And asking is not reaching. Spontaneously produced strategies often pay nothing — the utilization deficiency, found in over half the training conditions one review checked (<a href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0273229797904408" rel="noopener">Bjorklund et al. 1997</a>, read 2026-06-10). In the transfer experiment, the far-carried habit brought no knowledge gain; only near transfer paid. Worst, choosing the right standard presupposes the very discrimination the standard was meant to supply: poor performers misjudge others' work as badly as their own, and a $100 incentive does not cure them (<a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10626367/" rel="noopener">Kruger &amp; Dunning 1999</a>; <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2702783/" rel="noopener">Ehrlinger et al. 2008</a>, read 2026-06-10).</p>
<p>So: the habit builds. It stays standing bare only where the cue was dimmed by design, never dropped. And whether the trained question then fetches the right kind of standard — <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/checklist-or-exemplar">checklist-or-exemplar</a>'s cue applied unprompted — no study has yet caught in the act.</p>
<h2>What stays uncertain</h2>
<p>uncertain: the reproduce-or-produce trigger itself is this castle's framing, not a named, tested construct — the nearest literature develops judgment by supplying criteria and exemplars, and never tests whether one standing question makes learners select the right standard alone (<a href="https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10734-017-0220-3" rel="noopener">Tai et al. 2017</a>, read 2026-06-10). uncertain: the habit lens is a conceptual proposal about why strategies fail to stick, not a controlled demonstration that any which-standard decision becomes automatic (<a href="https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-025-10087-3" rel="noopener">EPR 2025</a>, read 2026-06-10). uncertain: the production- and utilization-deficiency evidence is children's memory strategies; stretching it to adults asking an abstract question is inference, not finding (<a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7847168/" rel="noopener">Miller &amp; Seier 1994</a>, read 2026-06-10). uncertain: follow-up after prompt removal is under-researched — Bannert 2015 is the well-known exception, partial persistence being weaker than habit but stronger than nothing (<a href="https://telrp.springeropen.com/articles/10.1186/s41039-021-00148-w" rel="noopener">prompt review 2021</a>, read 2026-06-10). uncertain: the discrimination counterpoint leans on Dunning–Kruger, itself contested as partly statistical artifact (<a href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0167487007001109" rel="noopener">Krueger &amp; Mueller critique line</a>, read 2026-06-10).</p>
