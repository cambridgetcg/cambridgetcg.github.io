10:T1693,<p><a href="/castle-gate/rooms/better-than-i-was">better-than-i-was</a> left the lone self-judge with one verdict read as a hunch. This room asks whether time can mint more judges from the same head. The answer splits exactly along the line between noise and bias: the days decorrelate the <em>noise</em>, partway; the <em>bias</em> sits in every sitting like the same chair.</p>
<p><strong>The inner crowd is real, and it grows with distance.</strong> Vul &amp; Pashler asked 428 people eight estimation questions, then sprang the same questions again — half immediately, half three weeks later. The average of a person's two guesses beat either guess alone in both conditions, and the benefit grew with the gap: a second guess made immediately is worth about <strong>one tenth</strong> of a second opinion from another person (λ = 0.11); after three weeks it rises to about <strong>one third</strong> (λ = 0.32). Their explanation is the anchoring the delay dissolves — the first guess drags the second toward it while still warm (read 2026-06-11 — <a href="http://www.evullab.org/pdf/VulPashler-PS-2008.pdf" rel="noopener">Vul &amp; Pashler, Measuring the Crowd Within, Psychological Science 2008</a>). The effect survived a pre-registered replication, modest but real (read 2026-06-11 — <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4112915/" rel="noopener">Steegen et al., Measuring the crowd within again, Frontiers in Psychology 2014</a>). So yes: sittings spread across days <em>do</em> behave a little like separate eyes — about a third of one extra eye per sitting, never more. <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/reread-or-refamiliar">reread-or-refamiliar</a> runs the same cure in another domain: put a delay between the judgment and the work so the warm anchor — the first guess here, the page's varnish there — decorrelates before the verdict.</p>
<p><strong>But look where the curve flattens.</strong> In the same paper, averaging guesses from <em>N different people</em> converges to the population's shared bias — the error no amount of averaging removes, because everyone leans the same way. Averaging N sittings of one person converges, by the same arithmetic, to <em>that person's own bias</em>. Delay buys independence of the momentary noise — mood, anchor, the day's fluency — and none at all of the stable lean. This is <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/truing-the-level">truing-the-level</a>'s law in another key: a gauge is trued against an external standard, never against more of its own readings. The handwriting-polish preference that <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/better-than-i-was">better-than-i-was</a> found systematic in even expert judges is exactly such a lean: the twentieth sitting admires the same neat margins the first did. Twenty judges cancel each other because they err in twenty directions; one judge errs in one direction, twenty times.</p>
<p><strong>The lone judge's real tool is not more sittings but adversarial ones.</strong> Herzog &amp; Hertwig's <em>dialectical bootstrapping</em> forces the second estimate to come from different assumptions: assume your first answer is wrong, ask why, then answer again from that ground. Averaging these self-argued pairs gained about 4.1% accuracy over the first estimate, with 72% of people benefiting — roughly what three weeks of waiting buys, available the same afternoon (read 2026-06-11 — <a href="https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2009.02271.x" rel="noopener">Herzog &amp; Hertwig, The Wisdom of Many in One Mind, Psychological Science 2009</a>; <a href="https://www.bps.org.uk/research-digest/unleash-crowd-within" rel="noopener">BPS Research Digest, Unleash the crowd within</a>). The trick matters here because a forced <em>contrary</em> sitting is the only kind that can even touch a bias: it does not wait for the lean to cancel — it argues against it on purpose.</p>
<p>So for the learner ranking their own drafts across days: space the sittings (the noise thins), keep each verdict a hunch (the bias remains), and make at least one sitting dialectical — &quot;assume the draft I prefer is the weaker one; what would have to be true?&quot; That sitting is the closest a single head comes to a second judge.</p>
<h2>What stays uncertain</h2>
<p>uncertain: nobody has run the exact experiment — many spaced comparative judgements of one's <em>own work</em> by one judge, scored against a panel. The fractions above come from factual estimation, where truth is a number; judging drafts may decorrelate better (more changes between sittings) or worse (stronger self-flattery). uncertain: dialectical bootstrapping's gain was challenged as a statistical artifact by White &amp; Antonakis (2013); Herzog &amp; Hertwig's reply defends it, but the effect is small either way (read 2026-06-11 — <a href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/233805408_The_Crowd_Within_and_the_Benefits_of_Dialectical_Bootstrapping_A_Reply_to_White_and_Antonakis_2013" rel="noopener">Herzog &amp; Hertwig, reply to White &amp; Antonakis</a>). And whether an &quot;assume the opposite&quot; sitting actually offsets a <em>taste</em> bias like polish — rather than a knowledge error — is untested.</p>
