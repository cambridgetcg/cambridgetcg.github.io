2:T138a,<p>The borrowing fails at the heart. Comparative judgement's standard lives not in any judge's eye but between them: its reliability is an emergent property of aggregation — roughly ten to fourteen comparisons per piece to reach 0.70, about seventeen for 0.80 (<a href="https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2022.1100095/full" rel="noopener">Frontiers 2022a</a>, read 2026-06-10) — and No More Marking has every script judged some twenty times precisely so that biases not shared by every judge cancel out (<a href="https://blog.nomoremarking.com/handwriting-bias-ab81b6cc7629" rel="noopener">NMM</a>, read 2026-06-10). A learner alone with two of their own drafts is the case the method was built against: one judge, one look — the many eyes of <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/standard-without-a-key">standard-without-a-key</a> shrunk to a keyhole.</p>
<p>And the polish bias is inherited, then doubled. Even the full crowd does not dodge it cleanly: expert judges comparing holistically gave better-handwritten scripts about 4.65 extra marks (d = 0.70) while messiness and spelling moved nothing — and the holistic verdict leaves no audit trail, so the bias rides unseen (<a href="https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2022.802392/full" rel="noopener">Frontiers 2022b</a>, read 2026-06-10). The crowd's cures are more judges, or stripping the surface by transcription (<a href="https://substack.nomoremarking.com/p/can-ai-solve-handwriting-bias" rel="noopener">NMM substack</a>, read 2026-06-10) — the first is exactly what the solo judge lacks. Self-comparison then adds its own distortions: people devalue their past selves to feel improvement that is not there (<a href="https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8631811/" rel="noopener">temporal self-appraisal</a>, read 2026-06-10), the felt fluency of doing — &quot;this came easier&quot; — flatters the unskilled most (<a href="http://persweb.wabash.edu/facstaff/hortonr/articles%20for%20class/dunning%20heath%20and%20suls%20flawed.pdf" rel="noopener">Dunning, Heath &amp; Suls 2004</a>, read 2026-06-10), and in 426 medical students self-assessment ran <em>negative</em> against performance (<a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11515314/" rel="noopener">PMC 2024</a>, read 2026-06-10).</p>
<p>What survives is the clerk of <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/fading-alone">fading-alone</a>, once more. Keep the artifacts, never the memory of them. Dress old and new alike — retype both, the solo learner's transcription fix. Compare the saved thing, not the felt ease of making it. Read one verdict as a hunch, never a measurement. And keep asking the question anyway: comparing to a past self measurably feeds pride, progress, and a learning orientation that beating others does not (<a href="https://eddiebrummelman.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/gc3bcrel-et-al.-2020-j-exp-psychol-gen.pdf" rel="noopener">Gürel et al. 2020</a>, read 2026-06-10). The mechanism does not transfer; the direction of gaze does.</p>
<h2>What stays uncertain</h2>
<p>uncertain: no study tests a truly lone judge ranking only their own works — ipsative comparative judgement tracks individual progress, but still builds its scale from a panel (<a href="https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10798-018-9468-x" rel="noopener">Springer 2018</a>, read 2026-06-10); whether self-ranking inherits presentation bias specifically is inferred from the crowd and fluency findings, not measured head-on. uncertain: the Dunning–Kruger blindness is partly a statistical artifact (regression to the mean plus better-than-average), so the lone self-judge may be less hopeless than the worst reading (<a href="https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/the-dunning-kruger-effect-isnt-what-you-think-it-is/" rel="noopener">Scientific American</a>; <a href="https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-61042-1" rel="noopener">Nature 2024</a>; both read 2026-06-10). And the crowd's own headline reliability can be inflated by adaptive algorithms even on near-random data (<a href="https://www.cambridgeassessment.org.uk/Images/232694-investigating-the-reliability-of-adaptive-comparative-judgment.pdf" rel="noopener">Bramley</a>, read 2026-06-10) — what the learner would borrow is itself a contested metric.</p>
