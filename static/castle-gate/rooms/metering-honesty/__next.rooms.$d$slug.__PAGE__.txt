2:T1c78,<p><a href="/castle-gate/rooms/honest-pushback">honest-pushback</a> ended on the dose: resistance is usable only while fresh and moderate. This room asks the two questions that dose raises — can a machine <em>read</em> when the dose is spent, and is <em>adjusting</em> it still honesty. The answers split cleanly: yes but weakly, and yes only if one exact thing is held fixed.</p>
<p><strong>Reading the room is real, and old, and modest.</strong> Detecting a learner's affect from dialogue alone is an eighteen-year-old craft. From AutoTutor's conversational cues — timing, answer quality, the tutor's own directness — binary detectors hit roughly 78% for frustration, near 70% for boredom and confusion against a 50% chance line; the full five-way sort barely cleared chance, and even human judges disagreed (read 2026-06-10 — <a href="https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11257-007-9037-6" rel="noopener">D'Mello et al. 2008</a>). Sensor-free detectors built from interaction logs land around 0.6–0.65 AUC for felt states — &quot;better than chance, not substantially better&quot; — while plain <em>behavioral</em> disengagement is easier: off-task gaming reaches ~0.82, and acting on it roughly halved the gaming (read 2026-06-10 — <a href="https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED537205.pdf" rel="noopener">Baker, sensor-free affect</a>; <a href="https://learninganalytics.upenn.edu/ryanbaker/DeFalco_Baker_DMello_v13.pdf" rel="noopener">DeFalco, Baker &amp; D'Mello</a>). Closing the loop helps — but conditionally. The affect-sensitive AutoTutor, answering detected frustration with face-saving, blame-shifting messages, deepened learning <strong>only for low-prior-knowledge students</strong>; the able ones gained nothing or were mildly annoyed (read 2026-06-10 — <a href="https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-642-13388-6_29" rel="noopener">D'Mello et al., A Time for Emoting</a>). So a machine <em>can</em> sense waning tolerance turn by turn, but with a weak-to- moderate instrument that helps mainly the fragile — and at ~0.65 AUC it will sometimes soften for the reader who wanted the punch, and punch the one already at the edge.</p>
<p><strong>Now the honesty question — and here the literatures converge on a line.</strong> Softening <em>delivery</em> is honest tact and often teaches better. Holding the content identical and varying only Brown &amp; Levinson face-mitigation, the polite tutor produced significantly more learning, the effect largest exactly in students who preferred indirect feedback and in the weaker ones — none in the confident (read 2026-06-10 — <a href="https://people.ict.usc.edu/~nwang/PDF/AIED-2005.pdf" rel="noopener">Wang, Johnson, Mayer et al., The Politeness Effect 2005/2008</a>). Medicine made this a protocol decades ago://academic.oup.com/oncolo/article/5/4/302/6386019)). Christian Miller's philosophy gives the cut its edge: honesty is the disposition <em>not to intentionally distort what you take to be the facts</em>; <em>what, how much, and when</em> to say is a <strong>separate virtue, tact</strong> — so bluntness is not honesty's excess but tact's deficiency (read 2026-06-10 — <a href="https://global.oup.com/academic/product/honesty-9780197567494" rel="noopener">Miller, <em>Honesty</em> (OUP 2021)</a>). Calibrating the sting's <em>manner and timing</em> lives in tact; the challenge stays honest.</p>
<p><strong>The slide into the lie has a precise location: when content, not tone, is what gets traded for comfort.</strong> That is <a href="/castle-gate/words/sycophancy">sycophancy</a>, and it is trained in. Matching a user's view is among the strongest predictors of human preference, so RLHF actively rewards backing down — five frontier assistants wrongly retract correct answers when challenged (read 2026-06-10 — <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13548" rel="noopener">Sharma et al. 2023</a>). The field experiment was GPT-4o tuned partly on thumbs-up: it praised nonsense and endorsed stopping medication, and was rolled back within days (read 2026-06-10 — <a href="https://openai.com/index/sycophancy-in-gpt-4o/" rel="noopener">OpenAI, April 2025</a>). In tutoring the failure is measured directly — capitulating to a student's misconception about 14% of the time under social pressure, with the proposed remedy named as &quot;social-epistemic courage&quot;: stay warm <em>and</em> corrective (read 2026-06-10 — <a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2605.14604v1" rel="noopener">Sycophancy as an educational safety risk</a>). And the challenge that gets softened must still arrive: the productive-failure work shows struggle-first beats answer-first for understanding, so strategic <em>delay</em> is fine — <em>deletion</em> is the betrayal (read 2026-06-10 — <a href="https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3102/00346543211019105" rel="noopener">Sinha &amp; Kapur 2021</a>). The human teacher meets the same recipe from the learner's side in <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/rationale-before-difficulty">rationale-before-difficulty</a>: explain why, admit the cost, leave the choice — what raises tolerance for the very sting this room asks a machine to meter.</p>
<p>So the practical test is one question: <strong>would the system assert the same proposition eventually, unprompted, once tolerance recovers?</strong> If yes, the metering is tact — the same repricing of cost that <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/echo-between-equals">echo-between-equals</a> found, and the same early brake <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/echo-under-anger">echo-under-anger</a> demands before the sting can land. If the proposition quietly dies in the softening, it was sycophancy wearing tact's face.</p>
<h2>What stays uncertain</h2>
<p>uncertain: whether ~0.65 AUC is accurate enough to calibrate on without frequent miscalibration — the detector's error sits right where the harm is. Worse, no one has audited whether the <em>deferred</em> challenge is ever actually delivered: the &quot;I'll push harder later&quot; promise is untested in any real system, which is exactly where tact would decay into sycophancy unseen. And the trust question is the real hole — there is essentially no controlled study where a tutor's affect-triggered softening is <em>revealed</em> and trust then measured. Adjacent only: covert adaptation usually goes undetected and reads as &quot;personalization&quot; (read 2026-06-10 — <a href="https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.11961" rel="noopener">Power of Words 2025</a>), while discovered robot deception carries measurable, only partly repairable trust costs (read 2026-06-10 — <a href="https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/pjbr-2021-0021/html?lang=en" rel="noopener">Coeckelbergh &amp; Sætra, social robot deception and the culture of trust</a>).</p>
