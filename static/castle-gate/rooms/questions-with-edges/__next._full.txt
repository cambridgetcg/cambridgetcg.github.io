10:Tf92,<p><a href="/castle-gate/rooms/spaced-understanding">spaced-understanding</a> ended here: spaced recall deepens ideas only where it exercises them. So what does such a question look like? Three working shapes, and one law beneath them.</p>
<p>The widest shape is no question at all: a blank page. <em>Write everything you understand about this, without looking.</em> Students who did that with a science text beat careful concept-mappers a week later on inference questions they had never practiced — questions that required connecting ideas — and had predicted the opposite ranking themselves (read 2026-06-10 — <a href="https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.1199327" rel="noopener">Karpicke &amp; Blunt, Science 2011</a>).</p>
<p>The sharpest shape is a why. <em>Why would this be true of this thing and not some other?</em> The answer cannot be a stored string; it has to be built, on the spot, from what you already know (read 2026-06-10 — <a href="https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1529100612453266" rel="noopener">Dunlosky et al., 2013</a> — rated moderate: firm for memory at short delays, thinner for deep comprehension).</p>
<p>The most practical shape is many small questions tracing the edges of one idea — because an idea does not fit on one card. For chicken stock, not &quot;what is stock?&quot; but: how does it differ from broth? why do restaurants cook with it instead of water? name an example you have never given before (read 2026-06-10 — <a href="https://andymatuschak.org/prompts/" rel="noopener">Matuschak, How to write good prompts</a>).</p>
<p>The law beneath all three: you get the understanding you exercise. Fact quizzes improved later fact tests and nothing more; higher-order quizzes the reverse; a mix lifted both (read 2026-06-10 — <a href="https://pdf.poojaagarwal.com/Agarwal_2019_JEdPsych.pdf" rel="noopener">Agarwal, J. Ed. Psych. 2019</a>). Across 192 effects, transfer from recall practice is real but modest (d ≈ 0.40) and leans on broad retrieval and elaborated feedback — a bare fact card, alone, often transfers nothing (read 2026-06-10 — <a href="https://pdf.retrievalpractice.org/transfer/Pan_Rickard_2018.pdf" rel="noopener">Pan &amp; Rickard, 2018</a>).</p>
<p>And a test you can apply from inside: if the answer arrives by recognition, smooth and instant, the question was exercising a fact. If it must be rebuilt — edges found, reasons re-derived — that effort <em>is</em> the understanding, walked again, as <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/remembering">remembering</a> said a path is kept (read 2026-06-10 — <a href="https://carlhendrick.substack.com/p/making-retrieval-practice-actually" rel="noopener">Hendrick</a>, a secondary source, but grounded in the studies above).</p>
<h2>What stays uncertain</h2>
<p>uncertain: whether recall practice holds up as material grows complex is a live dispute — Van Gog &amp; Sweller (2015) saw the effect fade with &quot;element interactivity&quot;; Karpicke &amp; Aue (2015) answered that the measure was never defined and counter-evidence was left out. The rebuttal stands stronger, but the boundary is unmapped. Matuschak names his own failure modes: a question's shape can be memorized and answered by pattern instead of thought, and his most idea-like prompts (creative ones) are craft informed by evidence, not themselves tested. And even good transfer mostly arrives in roughly the form practiced — ask in many shapes, or stay shaped by one.</p>
