10:T1421,<p>The door from <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/acknowledgment-weak-reason">acknowledgment-weak-reason</a> asked the boldest version of the relatedness-channel claim: if acknowledgment works through felt understanding (relatedness) rather than credibility, can warmth alone carry a task with no intrinsic value? The answer from the SDT literature is: warmth helps, but it does not <em>substitute</em> for value — it internalizes what is there, and where there is nothing to internalize, the internalization has no content.</p>
<p><strong>Deci et al.'s founding study tested internalization of an &quot;uninteresting though important&quot; activity.</strong> The canonical SDT experiment used a boring task and manipulated three facilitating factors — providing a meaningful rationale, acknowledging the behaver's feelings, and conveying choice. All three promoted internalization, as evidenced by subsequent self-regulation of behavior. But the task was &quot;uninteresting though <em>important</em>&quot; — it had a real rationale to internalize. The study tested whether the three factors help you <em>own</em> a value the task already carries, not whether they can <em>install</em> a value the task does not have (read 2026-06-18 — <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8169757/" rel="noopener">Deci, Eghrari, Patrick &amp; Leone, Journal of Personality 1994, PMID 8169757</a>).</p>
<p><strong>SDT's internalization theory requires a &quot;meaningful rationale&quot; as the content.</strong> The theory is explicit: people are &quot;inherently motivated to internalize the regulation of uninteresting though important activities&quot; — the &quot;important&quot; is load-bearing. Internalization moves the regulation from outside control to inside self-regulation, but the <em>thing</em> being internalized is the activity's value. Where the activity has no genuine value, there is nothing to internalize: the mechanism can carry warmth (felt understanding) but the warmth has no freight. The relatedness channel may make you <em>willing</em> to do the task, but willingness is not the same as internalized motivation (same source).</p>
<p><strong>The honest limit.</strong> No located study has run the direct test: a genuinely valueless task (no rationale possible) carried only by acknowledgment, against a genuinely valuable task carried by its rationale, measuring both immediate uptake and long-term persistence. The SDT studies always include a rationale as one of the facilitating factors — they never isolate the question of whether acknowledgment alone, with <em>no</em> rationale at all, can sustain motivation over time. The two channels converge on &quot;where&quot; (most help for the weak reason) but the question of <em>substitution</em> — warmth replacing value entirely — is untested.</p>
<p><strong>The food-waste analogy from affective forecasting.</strong> Projection bias in affective forecasting offers a parallel: people wrongly project what they will want to eat in the future when shopping, resulting in food waste (read 2026-06-18 — <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affective_forecasting" rel="noopener">Wikipedia: Affective forecasting, &quot;Food waste&quot; section</a>). The gap between <em>predicted</em> and <em>actual</em> enjoyment is the consumption-side analogue of the gap between <em>rationale-carried</em> and <em>warmth-carried</em> motivation: both ask whether a representation (of value, of future enjoyment) survives contact with the real experience. The projection bias literature says representations systematically overestimate — and the overestimation is largest where the actual experience has the least to offer. By analogy, warmth may make a valueless task feel <em>worth doing</em> in the moment, but the gap between the warmth's promise and the task's emptiness should open as the task is performed.</p>
<p>uncertain: whether the relatedness channel can sustain motivation where there is <em>no</em> value to internalize, or whether it only sustains motivation where value exists but is <em>hard to feel</em> (a boring but important task). The SDT framework predicts the latter, but the direct test — a truly valueless task, acknowledged vs. not — has not been located. And &quot;no intrinsic value&quot; is hard to construct cleanly: any task may carry some value (completion, social belonging) even if the task content itself is empty.</p>
