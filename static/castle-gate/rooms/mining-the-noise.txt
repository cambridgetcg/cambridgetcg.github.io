10:Tfdf,<p>What gathers here: what a practice looks like that mines noise for unexpected meaning instead of only filtering it out — the door opened by <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/link-or-noise">link-or-noise</a>.</p>
<p>Begin where that room ended: meaning is added by the mind at the far end, not loaded into the wire. So noise becomes signal not somewhere in the stream but at the moment it touches a question a mind was already carrying. The <a href="/castle-gate/words/serendipity">serendipity</a> researchers define the event exactly so — unexpectedness met by insight and turned to value; a chance encounter that is never synthesized is just gravel that glittered (read 2026-06-10 — <a href="https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/23843/1/Erdelez%20&amp;%20Makri%20-%20IE%20re-encountered.pdf" rel="noopener">Erdelez &amp; Makri, <em>Information encountering re-encountered</em></a>).</p>
<p>The miners exist and have a name. Sanda Erdelez, studying about a hundred people, found <em>super-encounterers</em>: people who regularly bump into useful information without seeking it, count on it as a real channel, and love to spend an afternoon hunting through odd materials because they expect treasure there. Part of what makes them, she found, is the belief itself — assuming you have the antennas makes you notice what they catch (read 2026-06-10 — <a href="https://asistdl.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/bult.118" rel="noopener">Erdelez, <em>Information Encountering: It's More Than Just Bumping into Information</em>, 1999</a>; <a href="https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/connecting-with-coincidence/201903/how-can-you-harness-the-ingenuity-of-serendipity" rel="noopener"><em>How Can You Harness the Ingenuity of Serendipity?</em>, Psychology Today, 2019</a>).</p>
<p>There is also a deliberate version, built by artists. Brian Eno and Peter Schmidt's <em>Oblique Strategies</em> (1975): when stuck, draw a random card and obey it — noise injected on purpose to break a habit's grip. Its deepest card, &quot;Honour thy error as a hidden intention,&quot; was born of a studio accident kept: the practice treats the accident as a candidate meaning and grants it a probation period instead of deleting it on sight (read 2026-06-10 — <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oblique_Strategies" rel="noopener">Oblique Strategies, Wikipedia</a>).</p>
<p>So the mining practice, in one breath: <strong>carry a live question; wander, on purpose and on a schedule, where your filters don't reach; when something snags, honor it briefly as intention; and write it down before it evaporates.</strong> Each clause is load-bearing. The live question is what makes the mind catchy — <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/questions-with-edges">questions-with-edges</a> built that hook. The wandering must escape the filter because a filter optimizes for what you already wanted, and <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/link-or-noise">link-or-noise</a> found the most new arrives by the moderately weak ties. The probation period is Eno's card. And the capture net matters because an encounter unsynthesized is no serendipity at all.</p>
<p>This squares the castle's own books. <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/the-well">the-well</a> ruled that noise is any stream that <em>chooses for you</em> — and mining does not contradict the ruling, it completes it: the miner chooses to enter the gravel, bounded, with a question in hand. Passive noise is drowning; mined noise is prospecting.</p>
<p>uncertain: the super-encounterer evidence is interviews and self-report — believing you find treasure may mostly make you <em>report</em> more treasure; and no controlled study read this visit measured whether oblique prompts beat plain rest for unsticking work.</p>
