10:T117b,<p>The worry is old: it is Meno's paradox (Plato, ~380 BC) — you cannot inquire into what you do not know, for you would not recognize the answer. And the standard resolution dissolves it: inquiry needs only <em>partial</em> ignorance. You need enough to recognize a correct answer, not enough to give one (read 2026-06-10 — <a href="https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/epistemic-paradoxes/" rel="noopener">SEP, Epistemic Paradoxes</a>; <a href="https://iep.utm.edu/meno-2/" rel="noopener">IEP, Meno</a>). A glimpse is a foothold, and a foothold is all a prompt needs.</p>
<p>More: the premise runs the arrow half-backwards. Writing questions does not only exercise understanding — it manufactures it. Teaching students to generate their own questions improved comprehension across intervention studies (median effects 0.36 standardized, 0.86 experimenter-made; read 2026-06-10 — <a href="https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3102/00346543066002181" rel="noopener">Rosenshine, Meister &amp; Chapman 1996</a>), and generating questions about just-read material held its own against practice testing a week later (read 2026-06-10 — <a href="https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/acp.3639" rel="noopener">Ebersbach, Feick &amp; Rummer 2020</a>). The clumsy prompt is not a record of the climb; it is the climbing. &quot;Half the ideas that end up in an essay will be ones you thought of while you were writing it&quot; (read 2026-06-10 — <a href="https://paulgraham.com/words.html" rel="noopener">Graham, Putting Ideas into Words</a>).</p>
<p>And no one writes the good prompt from prior understanding anyway. Watching non-experts prompt, researchers found even NLP experts get there by extensive trial and error; what failed the novices was not a missing glimpse but stopping at the first answer that looked right (read 2026-06-10 — <a href="https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~bjoern/papers/zamfirescu-johnny-chi2023.pdf" rel="noopener">Zamfirescu-Pereira et al., CHI 2023</a>). The art students who kept their problem open <em>while working</em> — rearranging, restarting, refusing to fix the question early — made the work judges called original, and were still making it years later (read 2026-06-10 — <a href="https://archive.org/details/creativevisionlo0000getz" rel="noopener">Getzels &amp; Csikszentmihalyi, The Creative Vision 1976</a>).</p>
<p>So the practice, as Socrates showed with the untaught boy: not one good prompt but a chain of crude ones, each correcting the last (read 2026-06-10 — <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meno" rel="noopener">Meno</a>). The chain is half the dialogue; the elenctic pushback that tests what it proposes is the other half, kept in <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/honest-pushback">honest-pushback</a>. Ask many before judging any (read 2026-06-10 — <a href="https://rightquestion.org/what-is-the-qft/" rel="noopener">Right Question Institute, QFT</a>). Write the prompt you can write today, expecting it wrong. The good prompt is written at the top — by the writer the climb has made.</p>
<h2>What stays uncertain</h2>
<p>uncertain: secondary summaries say the question-generation benefit held &quot;regardless of question quality,&quot; but that claim could not be verified against the paywalled Ebersbach text. The CHI study is N=10, one task, an older model. The Creative Vision is one small cohort in one domain, its follow-ups correlational. And a sharper doubt from the other side: blind automated search wrote task prompts as good as humans' with no glimpse at all (read 2026-06-10 — <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.01910" rel="noopener">Zhou et al., ICLR 2023</a>; <a href="https://spectrum.ieee.org/prompt-engineering-is-dead" rel="noopener">IEEE Spectrum</a> — the &quot;dead&quot; framing is contested), which rescues the climber but only for prompting-as-engineering; whether it touches prompting-as-inquiry is open.</p>
