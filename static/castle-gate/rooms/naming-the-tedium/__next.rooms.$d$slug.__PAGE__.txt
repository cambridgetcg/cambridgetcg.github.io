2:T132a,<p><a href="/castle-gate/rooms/rationale-before-difficulty">rationale-before-difficulty</a> closed on a conditional recipe — explain why + admit the cost + leave a real choice + let the payoff be felt. This door asks the hard question of any recipe: which ingredient does the work? The honest answer is that the three were never cleanly dismantled, and what isolated evidence exists ranks them — unevenly.</p>
<p>Start with the founding study. Deci, Eghrari, Patrick &amp; Leone (1994) is where these ingredients were named, but it never separated them: it varied all three at once, then sorted people by <em>how many</em> were present, not <em>which</em> (read 2026-06-11 — <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8169757/" rel="noopener">Deci et al. 1994</a>). More ingredients meant more internalization — exactly the confound this question intuits. The component-isolation study it asks for does not exist.</p>
<p>What <em>has</em> been isolated points one way. <strong>The rationale carries its own weight.</strong> Reeve, Jang, Hardré &amp; Omura (2002) varied rationale against no-rationale and the reason alone raised the value learners placed on a dull task, which drove effort (read 2026-06-11 — <a href="https://link.springer.com/article/10.1023/A:1021711629417" rel="noopener">Reeve et al. 2002</a>). <strong>Choice carries its own weight too</strong> — Patall, Cooper &amp; Robinson's meta-analysis of 41 experiments found choice alone lifts motivation (~d 0.30), so it is a real, separable lever (read 2026-06-11 — <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18298272/" rel="noopener">Patall et al. 2008</a>). But the lever is shakier than its billing: the effect shrinks for <em>consequential, instructionally-relevant</em> choices — precisely the &quot;real choice&quot; the recipe wants — and can reverse when the choice is effortful.</p>
<p><strong>Naming the tedium is the weak ingredient.</strong> It has essentially never been isolated; later frameworks merge &quot;acknowledge feelings&quot; with perspective-taking rather than testing it apart (read 2026-06-11 — <a href="https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-010-9142-7" rel="noopener">Su &amp; Reeve 2011</a>). And the one meta-analysis that tried — Steingut, Patall &amp; Trimble (2017) — found rationale <em>alone</em> (d .34) numerically beat rationale plus the extras; the additions did not measurably improve on it (read 2026-06-11 — <a href="https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/2017_SteingutPatallTrimble_MotivSci.pdf" rel="noopener">Steingut et al. 2017</a>).</p>
<p>So: rationale is load-bearing, choice is a real but qualified lever, and naming the tedium is closer to <em>unmeasured</em> than to <em>proven decoration</em>.</p>
<h2>What stays uncertain</h2>
<p>uncertain: the clean dismantling study is missing, and the field admits it — Steingut et al. found engagement &quot;the only outcome&quot; with enough data to even test rationale-plus-acknowledgment. So &quot;naming the tedium is decoration&quot; is not shown; it is <em>under-tested</em>. Against the decoration reading, Su &amp; Reeve's training meta-analysis (g ~0.63) found programs teaching <em>more</em> autonomy-support elements worked better — synergy, but correlational across studies, not per-ingredient (read 2026-06-11 — <a href="https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-010-9142-7" rel="noopener">Su &amp; Reeve 2011</a>). And the recipe's threads may be a chain, not a sum: Reeve &amp; Jang's rationale only worked when <em>already</em> delivered with acknowledgment and non-pressuring language, suggesting admitting the cost is part of what lets the reason land, not a flourish bolted on (read 2026-06-11 — <a href="https://link.springer.com/article/10.1023/A:1021711629417" rel="noopener">Reeve &amp; Jang 2002</a>). The defensible stance: treat all three as load-bearing until a study proves otherwise.</p>
