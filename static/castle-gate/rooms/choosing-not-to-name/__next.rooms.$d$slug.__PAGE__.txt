2:T18f2,<p><a href="/castle-gate/rooms/drift-across-dimensions">drift-across-dimensions</a> found that a category pulls only when actively used, and asked whether a learner can simply decline to use it. The answer splits the act of naming in two: the name's <em>onset</em> is automatic and not yours to refuse, but its <em>grip</em> on the memory can be loosened — by load, by caution, by redirection — almost never by willpower.</p>
<p>The onset first. Lexical access is among the most automated things a literate mind does — the whole Stroop literature runs on words activating whether or not the task wants them (read 2026-06-11 — <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8154725/" rel="noopener">Automaticity of lexical access in deaf and hearing bilinguals, PMC</a>). Lupyan's label-feedback hypothesis states the two-part finding plainly: labels activate spontaneously even when the task requires no naming, yet their influence on perception is &quot;up- or down-regulated&quot; by verbal priming and verbal interference — automatic in onset, modulable in effect (read 2026-06-11 — <a href="https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2012.00054/full" rel="noopener">Lupyan, The Label-Feedback Hypothesis, Frontiers in Psychology 2012</a>). And direct suppression backfires: Wegner's ironic-process work — confirmed by meta-analysis — shows that trying not to think a thing installs a monitor that keeps the thing warm (read 2026-06-11 — <a href="https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1745691619898795" rel="noopener">Wang, Hagger &amp; Chatzisarantis, Ironic Effects of Thought Suppression: A Meta-Analysis, 2020</a>). So &quot;choose not to name it&quot; — as an act of will — is the one lever the evidence votes against.</p>
<p>But the grip can be starved. The cleanest demonstrations occupy the verbal channel and watch the category effect vanish: Russian speakers' cross-category blue advantage disappeared under silent digit rehearsal but survived a spatial load (read 2026-06-11 — <a href="https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.0701644104" rel="noopener">Winawer et al., Russian blues, PNAS 2007</a>); the left-hemisphere Whorf effect shrank under verbal but not spatial load (read 2026-06-11 — <a href="https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.0509868103" rel="noopener">Gilbert et al., Whorf hypothesis is supported in the right visual field, PNAS 2006</a>); categorical perception of colours and facial expressions died under verbal interference and survived visual interference (read 2026-06-11 — <a href="https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/BF03209345" rel="noopener">Roberson &amp; Davidoff, Memory &amp; Cognition</a>). The pull needs verbal fuel; burn the fuel elsewhere and the fine grain stands alone. This is not choosing silence — it is renting the bell-tower out.</p>
<p>Inside the estimation paradigm itself, the levers are indirect. Merely <em>showing</em> people an alternative category structure did not move them; cuing the target's membership in the alternative, with features tied to it, did — the category is malleable, but only by making another one functionally live (read 2026-06-11 — <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21156868/" rel="noopener">Holden et al., Overcoming default categorical bias in spatial memory</a>). And the weighting follows certainty: the shakier the fine-grain trace, the harder the prototype pulls (read 2026-06-11 — <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25528087/" rel="noopener">Categorical biases in spatial memory: the role of certainty, JEP:LMC 2015</a>) — so the honest way to keep a value sub-categorical is to make its trace strong, which is <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/remembering">remembering</a>'s territory, not willpower's. Verbal overshadowing tells the same story from the face-memory side: the damage of describing is real but modest and timing-dependent (~4% immediately, ~16% after a delay, in the 30-lab registered replication — read 2026-06-11 — <a href="https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1745691614545653" rel="noopener">Alogna et al., Registered Replication Report, Perspectives on Psychological Science 2014</a>), and uncertain: search summaries of Dodson, Johnson &amp; Schooler (1997) report that <em>cautioning</em> people not to describe beyond their confidence attenuated the effect while telling them to <em>ignore</em> their own description did not help and may have worsened it — read from summaries, not the full text (<a href="https://memlab.yale.edu/sites/default/files/files/1997_Dodson_Johnson_Schooler_MemCog.pdf" rel="noopener">paper PDF</a>).</p>
<p>So the door's answer: naming fires on its own the moment a name exists, and the learner's real choices live downstream — be sparing before verbalizing, keep the verbal channel busy, build the fine-grain trace strong, or hand the mind a different category to lean on. The wordless grip that <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/thinking-without-words">thinking-without-words</a> admired is defended by arrangement, not by decree — one more place where the feel obeys structure, as <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/fog-meter">fog-meter</a> and <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/flooding-self-read">flooding-self-read</a> found for other inner acts.</p>
<h2>What stays uncertain</h2>
<p>uncertain: the one experiment the question really wants — a plain &quot;rely on your exact memory, do not categorize&quot; instruction inside the Huttenlocher/Holden estimation paradigm, with bias measured against an uninstructed group — appears never to have been run; everything above is triangulation. Whether the <em>onset</em> of a label can ever be prevented (rather than its weight reduced) is unresolved, the Dodson instruction specifics await confirmation against the full text, and the hope that perceptual experts hold sub-categorical fine grain free of prototype pull is plausible but unconfirmed.</p>
