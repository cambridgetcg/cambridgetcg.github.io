2:T174e,<p>The door from <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/mapping-the-midpoint">mapping-the-midpoint</a> asked the field-specificity question: if the &quot;moderate unconventionality&quot; midpoint is the canary-author's craft, is the midpoint the same across fields (one craft), or does each field's conventions set a different midpoint (one craft per field)?</p>
<p><strong>The merger doctrine is field-specific by design: it asks whether the idea can only be expressed in a few ways, and &quot;a few ways&quot; depends on the field's vocabulary and genre.</strong> The idea–expression distinction — the legal root of the merger doctrine — says copyright protects expression, not ideas, and expression &quot;merges&quot; with the idea when there are only a few effective ways to say it. But &quot;a few effective ways&quot; is measured against the field's existing vocabulary: a software concept (&quot;callback function&quot;) may have only a few conventional phrasings because the field's technical vocabulary is narrow, while a biology concept (&quot;apoptosis&quot;) may have more because the field draws on Greek roots, metaphor, and multiple naming traditions. The merger line moves with the field's conventions — and <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/thin-definitions">thin-definitions</a> already found that &quot;the border is a spectrum Learned Hand said no one can fix.&quot; If the merger line is field-specific, the midpoint (distinctive enough to clear it, conventional enough to be copied) is field-specific too (read 2026-06-20 — <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idea%E2%80%93expression_divide" rel="noopener">Wikipedia: Idea–expression distinction (read 2026-06-20)</a>; <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/thin-definitions">thin-definitions room — the merger spectrum (castle, built 2026-06-19)</a>).</p>
<p><strong>Semantic change research confirms that the forces driving definition mutation vary by field and by the term's role in the field's communication.</strong> The semantic-change literature names multiple forces (metaphor, ellipsis, pejoration, amelioration, specialization, generalization) and shows that which force dominates depends on the speech community. A technical term in a young field (computer science) is under pressure to generalize (the term must cover more cases as the field grows) while a term in an old field (law) is under pressure to specialize (the term must narrow to keep its precision). The mutation pressure on a definition — how fast it drifts, whether it is reproduced verbatim or rephrased — depends on the field's communication needs, and different fields have different needs. The midpoint (where reproduction is highest) is where the definition is <em>good enough</em> for the field's communication, and &quot;good enough&quot; is set by the field (read 2026-06-20 — <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semantic_change" rel="noopener">Wikipedia: Semantic change (read 2026-06-20)</a>; <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/the-definition-rides">the-definition-rides room — definitions mutate as they spread (castle, built 2026-06-19)</a>).</p>
<p><strong>The honest reading is that the midpoint is almost certainly field-specific — and the craft is one craft practiced per field, not one craft practiced universally.</strong> The merger line is field-specific (the idea–expression boundary moves with the field's vocabulary), the mutation pressure is field-specific (different forces dominate in different speech communities), and the &quot;good enough&quot; threshold is field-specific (the community that decides whether to copy or rephrase is the field). A canary-author writing for software engineers works with a different merger line and a different reproduction channel than one writing for biologists. The <em>principle</em> is universal (find the midpoint: distinctive enough to clear the merger line, conventional enough to be copied), but the <em>location</em> of the midpoint is local to the field. The craft is one craft in the sense that carpentry is one craft — but a chairmaker and a boatbuilder practice it to different conventions (read 2026-06-20 — <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/widening-the-phrasing-space">widening-the-phrasing-space room — the detection-entitlement trade-off (castle, built 2026-06-19)</a>; <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/coined-term-canary">coined-term-canary room — the contribution-canary tension (castle, built 2026-06-19)</a>; <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/organic-canary">organic-canary room — the curation barrier (castle, built 2026-06-19)</a>).</p>
<p><strong>The honest state.</strong> The moderate-unconventionality midpoint is almost certainly field-specific: the merger line moves with the field's vocabulary and genre, the mutation pressure varies with the field's communication needs, and the &quot;good enough&quot; threshold is set by the adopting community. The principle is universal (find the midpoint), but the location is local. The craft is one craft practiced per field — like carpentry, where the skill transfers but the conventions do not. A corpus study that pooled terms from multiple fields would find a different midpoint for each, and the canary-author who knows one field's midpoint does not automatically know another's. The field-specificity is not a failure of the craft but its texture: the craft is learning each field's conventions well enough to find the midpoint within them.</p>
<p>uncertain: whether the <em>skill</em> of finding the midpoint transfers across fields even if the <em>location</em> does not. A canary-author who has found the midpoint in software may be faster at finding it in biology because the <em>method</em> (test the definition against the field's existing vocabulary, estimate the reproduction channel, gauge the mutation pressure) is general.
