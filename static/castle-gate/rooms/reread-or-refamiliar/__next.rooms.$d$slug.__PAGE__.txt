2:T1474,<p>The feeling itself cannot tell. Asked how well they understood what they read, readers in general barely know: across 115 studies the match between felt and tested comprehension averages a correlation of .178 (<a href="https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.3102/00346543221094083" rel="noopener">Yang, Zhao, Yuan, Luo &amp; Shanks 2023</a>, read 2026-06-10). And the second pass tilts that weak signal the wrong way: rereading raises perceptual fluency — the words glide — and the glide is read as knowing, the <a href="/castle-gate/words/fluency">fluency</a> trap of <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/simple-explanations">simple-explanations</a> collected on the return trip (<a href="https://www.structural-learning.com/post/fluency-illusions-students-think-they-know" rel="noopener">Structural Learning on fluency illusions</a>, read 2026-06-10).</p>
<p>But the two impostors live in different houses, and that is the test. Refamiliarized fluency lives in surface memory of the words — it answers only while the page is near. Repaired comprehension lives in the situation model, the gist that the words built — it travels with you. So take the judgment away from the page: wait a little, then produce from nothing — keywords, a summary, one <em>why</em> in your own words — and judge only by what came back. The instrument is the blank page of <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/questions-with-edges">questions-with-edges</a>, hired for a second job: built there to deepen, it judges here. When readers wrote summaries after a delay, their judgment of what they understood reached +.57; written immediately, with the words still echoing, only +.26 (<a href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S000169180700128X" rel="noopener">Anderson &amp; Thiede 2008</a>; <a href="https://scholarworks.boisestate.edu/cifs_facpubs/8/" rel="noopener">Thiede, Anderson &amp; Therriault 2003</a>; both read 2026-06-10). The same logic powers <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/productive-confusion">productive-confusion</a>'s cheap retrieval check and the third witness of <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/pretty-or-well-made">pretty-or-well-made</a>: re-meet it where the varnish can't follow. Retrieval likewise deflates the rereader's overconfidence (<a href="https://www.learningscientists.org/blog/2026/3/26-1" rel="noopener">Learning Scientists 2026</a>, read 2026-06-10).</p>
<p>And one repair to the question's own premise: the second pass is not only a fluency machine. Read <em>for snags</em>, it is a diagnostic instrument — rereaders judged their comprehension more accurately than single-pass readers, apparently because the second pass exposes the deep places that still resist while the easy places flow (<a href="https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/BF03209348" rel="noopener">Rawson, Dunlosky &amp; Thiede 2000</a>, read 2026-06-10). The gain came only when the return was prompt — a week later it vanished (<a href="https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1207/s15326950dp4001_2" rel="noopener">Dunlosky &amp; Rawson 2005</a>, read 2026-06-10) — which fits <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/invited-back">invited-back</a>'s timing lesson from the other side: spaced returns repair more but judge worse; prompt returns judge better.</p>
<p>So the re-reader's discipline, in two moves: during the return, trust only the snags, never the glide; after the return, step away, wait, and ask the blank page. Smoothness cannot follow you there. <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/spaced-understanding">spaced-understanding</a> caught the same fluency illusion in massed study — the same Kornell &amp; Bjork finding that 71% trusted the worse method — and reached for the very same delayed blank-page cure.</p>
<h2>What stays uncertain</h2>
<p>uncertain: all these accuracy gains are <em>relative</em> — telling your better-understood passages from your worse — while the overall <em>level</em> of confidence can stay inflated even as the ranking sharpens; the meta-analysis measures ordering, not calibration (<a href="https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.3102/00346543221094083" rel="noopener">Yang et al. 2023</a>) — what trues the level itself, against a key as fine as the answer's parts, is <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/truing-the-level">truing-the-level</a>, leaning on the same .178 finding from the far side. uncertain: the .57 ceiling still leaves much unexplained — even the best-tested check is a lantern, not daylight. And the lab's delays and generation tasks were assigned; whether a solo reader applies them honestly, unwatched, is the <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/fog-meter">fog-meter</a> worry again.</p>
<h2>Doors</h2>
<ul><li>Every accuracy gain measured is relative — ranking what you understand better and worse — while the level of confidence can stay inflated. What repairs absolute calibration, not just the ordering?</li><li>The blank-page check needs a delay, and delays cost momentum — what is the smallest step-away (a minute? one closed paragraph?) that still strips the varnish from the judgment?</li><li>A return read for snags judges well; a return read for flow feels well.
