10:T1579,<p>Take the question's premise seriously first: the gauge it hopes for has never been observed. The peaking and pooling curves were drawn after the fact — learners replayed video of their own faces and screens and labeled each sampled moment in hindsight, the retrospective affect judgment protocol (read 2026-06-10 — <a href="https://home.cs.colorado.edu/~mozer/Teaching/syllabi/TopicsInCognitiveScienceSpring2016/DMelloConfusion-Reprint.pdf" rel="noopener">D'Mello &amp; Graesser, Confusion chapter</a>; <a href="https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1190004.pdf" rel="noopener">Lehman &amp; D'Mello</a>). The trajectory lives in the recording, not in anyone's moment.</p>
<p>And the inner instrument such training would calibrate is the weakest one yet pointed at the problem: learners' labels for their own emotions barely agreed with anyone — kappa .08 against peers, .14 against trained judges, while two judges reached .36 between themselves (read 2026-06-10 — <a href="https://www.inderscience.com/info/inarticle.php?artid=28805" rel="noopener">D'Mello, Craig &amp; Graesser</a>). The record the self keeps is doctored besides: memory rebuilds an episode from its peak and its end and throws the duration away (read 2026-06-10 — <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peak%E2%80%93end_rule" rel="noopener">peak–end rule</a>). Train a feel against your own recollections and you calibrate to an artifact.</p>
<p>So the trainable thing is not a feeling. Two moves survive the evidence. <strong>Watch conduct, not mood</strong>: pooling takes visible shapes — the long stall that drifts toward boredom, the rapid aimless flipping that drifts toward frustration (read 2026-06-10 — <a href="https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2018.00049/full" rel="noopener">Frontiers review</a>). <strong>Close the loop</strong>: predicting your own performance trained nothing by itself, but predict-then-check — guess your odds aloud, then collect the verdict — improved calibration in five short trials, and improved the decisions resting on it (read 2026-06-10 — <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12982714/" rel="noopener">reminder-setting study</a>; <a href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0959475218308788" rel="noopener">Proust et al.</a>). A guess said out loud plus a verdict actually collected — kin to the cheap retrieval test <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/productive-confusion">productive-confusion</a> ends with.</p>
<p>Outside the lab: unknown, plainly. Machines reading faces in real classrooms manage only AUC ≈ .65 (read 2026-06-10 — <a href="https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/2678025.2701397" rel="noopener">Bosch &amp; D'Mello</a>); strict reviews put far transfer of such training near zero (read 2026-06-10 — <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6390881/" rel="noopener">transfer review</a>); the predict-then-check authors say naturalistic persistence &quot;remains to be seen.&quot; And that ~.65 is no local failing: <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/metering-honesty">metering-honesty</a> met the same ceiling reading a reader's waning tolerance from conduct — two rooms, one instrument, one law: no design, machine or solo, may lean hard on reading a mind's inner state. And even sensed perfectly, peak-versus-pool is no verdict — the same curve helps or hurts depending on what scaffolding waits (read 2026-06-10 — <a href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0959475212000357" rel="noopener">D'Mello, Lehman, Pekrun &amp; Graesser 2014</a>). Not a meter, then: a mirror for the hands, a ledger for the guesses — and not a neutral mirror either: <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/watching-the-watcher">watching-the-watcher</a> found the watching itself a mild treatment, its direction set by the stance, distanced toward resolution, immersed toward the tightening circle.</p>
<h2>What stays uncertain</h2>
<p>uncertain: no study tests whether a trained, self-perceived feel for confusion-type persists in everyday learning — that exact claim is unproven, not disproven. The transfer question is genuinely contested: one adaptive study reports domain-general metacognitive gains against the near-zero reviews. And the peak/pool construct itself is a post-hoc artifact of retrospective coding; it may not carve real-time experience at its joints at all — even instrumented detection is only moderate (~76% from facial coding; read 2026-06-10 — <a href="https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.15201" rel="noopener">survey</a>), so the best realistic aim is a coarse moving-vs-stuck cue with a fallback action, not a reading.</p>
