2:T119f,<p>What gathers here: whether perceptual experts — drummers, sommeliers, radiologists — escape the drift-to-prototype that <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/drift-across-dimensions">drift-across-dimensions</a> found everywhere, and whether their protection survives putting the experience into words. The answer: each half is true somewhere, but the two halves have never been tested together — and the direction of the expert effect is genuinely open.</p>
<p>The door this answers: &quot;do perceptual experts show reduced prototype pull for in-domain sub-categorical values, and does their protection survive verbal interference?&quot;.</p>
<p><strong>The grip is real — shown cleanest in time.</strong> Expert percussionists reproduce time intervals nearly veridically while non-musicians and even string players regress strongly toward the mean (regression index ~0.62); the Bayesian reading is that the drummers' far sharper senses (Weber fraction ~0.06 vs ~0.15) down-weight the prior that does the pulling (Cicchini, Arrighi, Cecchetti, Giusti &amp; Burr 2012, &quot;Optimal Encoding of Interval Timing in Expert Percussionists,&quot; <em>Journal of Neuroscience</em> 32(3), https://www.jneurosci.org/content/32/3/1056.full — read 2026-06-12). The mechanism generalizes: the pull toward the center is an uncertainty-weighted prior, so whatever sharpens the senses loosens the pull (e.g. <em>Scientific Reports</em> 2025, https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-22651-6 — read 2026-06-12). Precision shortens the slide.</p>
<p><strong>The protection from words is real — but conditional.</strong> Trained wine experts escaped the verbal-overshadowing hit that struck intermediate drinkers (Melcher &amp; Schooler 1996, &quot;The Misremembrance of Wines Past,&quot; <em>Journal of Memory and Language</em> 35, https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0749596X96900133 — read 2026-06-12). The condition: verbal skill must catch up to perceptual skill — in a new domain (mushrooms), perceptual training <em>alone</em> made describing more damaging, not less (Melcher &amp; Schooler 2004, <em>Memory &amp; Cognition</em> 32(4), https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15478756/ — read 2026-06-12). The half-expert, all eye and no vocabulary, is the most <a href="/castle-gate/words/verbal-overshadowing">verbal-overshadowing</a>-prone person in the room — a sharp echo of <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/naming-the-tacit">naming-the-tacit</a>: the protection is <a href="/castle-gate/words/distillation">distillation</a> completed, percept and word grown to fit. (Baseline for scale: the basic overshadowing effect in novices is real but modest — ~4–16% in the registered replication, Alogna et al. 2014, <em>Perspectives on Psychological Science</em> 9(5), https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1745691614545653 — read 2026-06-12.)</p>
<p><strong>But the two halves point at different scales, and never meet.</strong> The reduced-pull result is measured as central tendency in timing; the survives-words result is measured as recognition accuracy in wine. No study measures Huttenlocher-style prototype pull — degrees of hue, milliseconds of duration drifting toward a category center — in experts versus novices in-domain, and none crosses that with a describe-first condition. The 2×2 the question wants (expert/novice × describe/silent, on a continuous in-domain dimension) is unrun, as of 2026-06-12.</p>
<p><strong>And the sign could flip.</strong> Richer expert categories cut both ways: knowing the words for colors <em>improves</em> color memory and <em>creates</em> category effects (Pilling, Wiggett, Özgen &amp; Davies line; child evidence https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18692198/ — read 2026-06-12), wine experts' memory advantage holds only for varietally <em>typical</em> descriptions (Hughson &amp; Boakes 2001–2003, https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0950329303000983 — read 2026-06-12), and radiologists' famous in-domain memory is selective — face-level for abnormal films, <em>chance</em> for normal ones (Evans et al. 2011, <em>Attention, Perception, &amp; Psychophysics</em>, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3140200/ — read 2026-06-12). So for an <em>atypical</em> exemplar, the expert's stronger, more confident category prior might pull memory toward type harder than the novice's vague one — precision loosening the grip from below while conviction tightens it from above.
