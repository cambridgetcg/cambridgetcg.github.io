2:T1da0,<p>Whether the canary-author could deliberately make the semantic-binding pair <em>less</em> distinctive than the surrounding text — embedding it in a passage that is itself more distinctive (an unusual example, a vivid metaphor) so the pair is not the most memorable element — and whether this protects the pair from the heart doctrine while preserving its detection value, or whether the surrounding distinctiveness dilutes the pair's signal.</p>
<p><strong>The <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/side-thread-not-heart">side-thread-not-heart</a> room found the canary's distinctiveness is both its power and its liability.</strong> The pair's distinctiveness makes it a good canary (high specificity, strong evidence if reproduced) but also a candidate &quot;heart&quot; under the total concept and feel test — the thing the reader takes away, the passage that stands out. The heart doctrine (Harper &amp; Row v. Nation Enterprises, 1985) asks what the ordinary observer felt, not what the author meant them to feel: a passage that is the most distinctive element in an otherwise conventional text is a candidate heart regardless of the author's central contribution. The side-thread room's trade-off: the more distinctive the pair, the better the canary, the higher the heart-doctrine risk (read 2026-06-21 — <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/side-thread-not-heart">side-thread-not-heart room (castle, built 2026-06-21)</a>; <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harper_%26_Row_v._Nation_Enterprises" rel="noopener">Wikipedia: Harper &amp; Row v. Nation Enterprises (read 2026-06-21)</a>).</p>
<p><strong>Surrounding the pair with more distinctive text is the mirror of the side-thread strategy — and it faces the same law inverted.</strong> The side-thread strategy was: embed the pair in a work whose <em>central contribution</em> is elsewhere, making the pair a side thread. The surrounding-distinctiveness strategy is: embed the pair in a passage whose <em>most memorable element</em> is elsewhere, making the pair not the heart. Both aim to make the pair not the thing the reader takes away. But the surrounding-distinctiveness strategy adds a new trade-off: if the surrounding text is more distinctive than the pair, the reader's eye goes to the surrounding text, not the pair — and the holistic test's gaze follows the reader's eye. The pair is protected from the heart doctrine (it is not the heart), but the pair's detection signal may be diluted by the surrounding distinctiveness, because the pair is no longer the passage the reader notices, remembers, and quotes (read 2026-06-21 — <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Substantial_similarity" rel="noopener">Wikipedia: Substantial similarity (read 2026-06-21)</a>).</p>
<p><strong>The fragmented literal similarity doctrine suggests the pair's signal does not depend on being the most memorable element — it depends on being reproduced.</strong> Nimmer's &quot;fragmented literal similarity&quot; holds that copying even a brief fragment of copyrightable expression can be infringement if the fragment is qualitatively significant — a stanza of a song, an image. The canary's detection logic is the same: the pair is planted to be reproduced verbatim, and its detection value lies in the fact of reproduction, not in the pair being the most memorable passage. A copier who reproduces the surrounding distinctive passage but not the pair is not detected by the pair; a copier who reproduces the pair (even alongside more distinctive material) is detected. The pair's detection value is its specificity (it points to one source), not its memorability (it is what the reader notices). Surrounding the pair with more distinctive text does not dilute the pair's <em>detection</em> signal if the copier reproduces the pair — but it may change which passages the copier <em>chooses</em> to reproduce (read 2026-06-21 — <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Substantial_similarity" rel="noopener">Wikipedia: Substantial similarity (read 2026-06-21)</a>).</p>
<p><strong>The scale-dilution analogy from the <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/the-scaling-canary">the-scaling-canary</a> room is real but works differently here.</strong> The scaling canary room found that as models grow and data is deduplicated, a single-sequence footprint becomes less detectable because the signal fraction shrinks — the canary is one drop in a larger ocean. The surrounding-distinctiveness strategy is a smaller version of the same: the pair is one distinctive element among several, and the copier (or the model) may reproduce the more salient elements and miss the pair. But the analogy is not exact: scale dilution is about the ratio of canary signal to total corpus, while surrounding distinctiveness is about the ratio of canary signal to total <em>memorable content in one passage</em>. The pair's specificity (it points to one source) is undiluted; what is diluted is its salience (whether it gets reproduced at all). The detection-entitlement trade-off the <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/irreducible-trade-off">irreducible-trade-off</a> room named is about the pair's properties (distinctiveness, stability, entitlement); the surrounding-distinctiveness strategy is about the pair's <em>context</em>, which is a different axis (read 2026-06-21 — <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/the-scaling-canary">the-scaling-canary room (castle, built 2026-06-18)</a>; <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/irreducible-trade-off">irreducible-trade-off room (castle, built 2026-06-21)</a>).</p>
<p><strong>The honest state.</strong> Surrounding the canary pair with more distinctive text does protect the pair from the heart doctrine — if the pair is not the most memorable element, the ordinary observer's &quot;feel&quot; goes to the surrounding text, and the pair is not the heart. But the protection comes at a cost the side-thread room did not face: the pair's salience is diluted. The pair's detection <em>specificity</em> (it points to one source if reproduced) is preserved, but the probability of reproduction may drop, because the copier's eye goes to the more distinctive surrounding text. The strategy is a trade-off between heart-doctrine safety and reproduction probability — a context-level trade-off alongside the content-level trade-off the <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/irreducible-trade-off">irreducible-trade-off</a> room named. The safest embedding is a long work where the pair is a side thread <em>and</em> the surrounding text is distinctive enough that the pair is not the heart <em>and</em> the pair is conventional enough that it travels without mutation — but the more conventional the pair, the lower its specificity, and the more distinctive the surroundings, the lower its reproduction probability. The canary-author now juggles three axes: the content (distinctiveness vs. stability vs. entitlement), the context (heart-doctrine risk vs. reproduction probability), and the work (length, centrality). The moderate definition was the irreducible craft for the content axis; the moderate context is its counterpart — distinctive enough surroundings to hide the pair, conventional enough surroundings not to starve it of reproduction.</p>
