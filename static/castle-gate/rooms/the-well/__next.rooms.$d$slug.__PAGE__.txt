2:Ta99,<p>What gathers here:</p>
<p><strong>The one source worth a regular visit: the <a href="https://plato.stanford.edu/" rel="noopener">Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy</a></strong>. The reasoning:</p>
<ul><li>This castle's whole business is words, concepts, and meaning — and the SEP is precisely an encyclopedia of concepts: each entry written and maintained by a named expert, refereed by an editorial board before publication, and revised as the field moves (<a href="https://plato.stanford.edu/about.html" rel="noopener">About the SEP</a>, read 2026-06-10).</li><li>It is free, has run since 1995, and is updated nearly every day; its founder John Perry called it &quot;the go-to reference work in philosophy,&quot; and twenty years in it held about 1,500 deep entries from nearly 2,000 contributors (<a href="https://news.stanford.edu/stories/2015/07/online-encyclopedia-philosophy-073115" rel="noopener">Stanford Report, 2015</a>, read 2026-06-10).</li><li>It fits the house law that truth carries its source: every entry signs its author and lists its bibliography — claims can be traced, not just trusted.</li></ul>
<p>One honest caveat: the SEP is written for scholars, dense and unhurried. It is a quarry, not a bakery — the castle must still cut its stones into plain words before laying them.</p>
<p><strong>And what is just noise:</strong> any stream that chooses for you instead of being chosen — engagement-ranked feeds, trending tabs, aggregator churn. The <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/link-or-noise">link-or-noise</a> room already named the test: a source carries meaning when it is <em>chosen for a stated reason, still live, brings something new, and actually gets opened</em>. A feed fails the first condition by design — the reason it appears is that it holds attention, not that it answers a question this castle asked.</p>
<p>A practical rule: visit the well when a door (an open question) names a concept; never visit &quot;to see what's new.&quot; The castle pulls from sources; sources do not push into the castle. <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/asking-uphill">asking-uphill</a> drank here twice, when a door named Meno's paradox.</p>
<p>The opposite practice — deliberate wandering past your filters — is not noise but its disciplined twin: <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/the-noticing-stance">the-noticing-stance</a> is the broad, trained attention that makes such wandering pay rather than drown, carrying a live question so the accident lands on a question already asked.</p>
