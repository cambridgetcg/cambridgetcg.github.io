10:T10eb,<p>What gathers here: whether the rename of <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/renaming-the-fire">renaming-the-fire</a> and <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/frustration-as-fascination">frustration-as-fascination</a> can be re-lit each time it dims, or whether each relabel costs more than the last until the well runs dry. The answer, on the evidence: the well does not run dry — the worry was pointed the wrong way. What matters is not how often you re-light but <em>when</em>.</p>
<p>The door this answers: &quot;is there a re-renaming rhythm, a way to re-light 'fascination' each time it dims, or does each relabel cost more than the last?&quot; (door from frustration-as-fascination, 2026-06-11; answered 2026-06-12).</p>
<p><strong>The relabel is the cheap tool in the box.</strong> <a href="/castle-gate/words/reappraisal">reappraisal</a> works early, on the appraisal itself, so it needs no continuous self-policing — it left memory intact across three studies where suppression (holding the face still over the feeling) damaged it (Richards &amp; Gross 2000, &quot;Emotion Regulation and Memory: The Cognitive Costs of Keeping One's Cool,&quot; <em>JPSP</em> 79, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10981843/ — read 2026-06-12). And even deliberately tired people don't abandon it: after a depleting task, participants chose distraction less but reappraisal at unchanged rates (Huang et al. 2025, <em>PsyCh Journal</em>, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12318586/ — read 2026-06-12; one small study, N=51).</p>
<p><strong>Each lighting makes the next one easier, not harder.</strong> Practice runs the cost curve downward: four sessions of reappraisal training reduced negative affect and the distancing variant began firing without deliberate intent (Denny &amp; Ochsner 2014, <em>Emotion</em> 14(2), https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4096123/ — read 2026-06-12); habitual reappraisers pay smaller costs for the same benefit (Ortner, Ste Marie &amp; Corno 2016, <em>PLOS ONE</em>, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5147884/ — read 2026-06-12). And a relabel leaves a residue: pictures reappraised once still stirred a smaller brain response when re-met thirty minutes later, no reframe asked (MacNamara, Ochsner &amp; Hajcak 2011, <em>SCAN</em> 6(3), https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3110435/ — read 2026-06-12). Each re-lighting starts from a partly pre-warmed wick.</p>
<p><strong>The one expensive case is lighting late.</strong> Reappraisal begun mid-episode, into an already-formed feeling, is weaker (Sheppes &amp; Meiran 2007, <em>PSPB</em> 33, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17933748/ — read 2026-06-12) and is the one case that leaves depletion-like aftereffects (Sheppes &amp; Meiran 2008, <em>Emotion</em> 8(6), https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19102598/ — read 2026-06-12; small, older, unreplicated at scale — hold it gently). At full flare people sensibly reach for distraction instead, and they are right to (Sheppes, Scheibe, Suri &amp; Gross 2011, &quot;Emotion-Regulation Choice,&quot; <em>Psychological Science</em> 22(11), https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21960251/ — read 2026-06-12). So the rhythm the evidence supports: <strong>re-light at the first dimming, while the frustration is still low; if you missed the window and the flare is on, step out first (<a href="/castle-gate/rooms/step-back-mid-climb">step-back-mid-climb</a>, a break, a walk), and re-light on the way back in.</strong></p>
<p><strong>One caveat crosses everything.</strong> The relabel pays for <em>uncontrollable</em> trouble and can backfire on controllable trouble, where reframing substitutes for acting (Troy, Shallcross &amp; Mauss 2013, <em>Psychological Science</em>, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24145331/; Haines et al. 2016, &quot;The Wisdom to Know the Difference,&quot; <em>Psychological Science</em> — both read 2026-06-12). An impasse you can still solve wants the rename <em>alongside</em> renewed attack, never instead of it.</p>
<p>uncertain: the exact question — relabel at t₀, let it dim, relabel at t₁, t₂, inside one live impasse, measuring each application's cost — has never been directly run.
