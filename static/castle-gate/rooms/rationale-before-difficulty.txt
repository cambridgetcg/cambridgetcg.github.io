10:T145d,<p>consent-to-the-sting left this door open: the honest placebo works because the disclosure carries a true rationale, not just a name. Does the rationale carry the same freight in teaching? The honest answer splits cleanly: <strong>yes, it raises uptake; no, it does not, on its own, secure persistence.</strong></p>
<p>The premise holds on the placebo side. A controlled study found open-label placebos given <em>with</em> a rationale beat those given without one, and the rationale is an active ingredient, not framing (read 2026-06-11 — <a href="https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/pain-research/articles/10.3389/fpain.2021.734882/full" rel="noopener">Wei et al., Frontiers in Pain Research 2021</a>). So &quot;why&quot; measurably matters.</p>
<p>Carried into teaching, the cleanest direct test is sobering. Tell learners <em>why</em> repeated retrieval (<a href="/castle-gate/words/retrieval-practice">retrieval-practice</a>) helps, before they practice, and they use the effortful strategy more during learning — but the lift vanishes once the prompts are removed; they do not keep using it (read 2026-06-11 — <a href="https://pdf.retrievalpractice.org/metacognition/11_Ariel_Karpicke_2018.pdf" rel="noopener">Ariel &amp; Karpicke 2018</a>). Rationale boosts immediate tolerance far more reliably than it sustains persistence. The leading review says it plainly: sharing the science is &quot;essential to lay the basis for willingness,&quot; but &quot;simply having the information is not sufficient to elicit behavioral change&quot; — the learner must also <em>experience</em> the difficulty pay off (read 2026-06-11 — <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10858853/" rel="noopener">Biwer et al., review 2024</a>).</p>
<p>Why so weak alone? Because the rationale fights the fluency illusion that <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/productive-confusion">productive-confusion</a> named: effortful methods <em>feel</em> ineffective, and learners rate them worse even after learning more from them (read 2026-06-11 — <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9397154/" rel="noopener">Biwer et al., Study Smart 2020</a>). Words cannot debunk a feeling the body keeps re-supplying. What does is words plus firsthand proof — and proof the learner articulates as their own, since self-generated utility-value beats top-down lectures (read 2026-06-11 — <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6168083/" rel="noopener">Hulleman et al. 2017</a>). The self-determination recipe sharpens it: rationale + admitting &quot;yes, this is tedious&quot; + a real choice internalizes motivation, and internalized motivation is what persists (read 2026-06-11 — <a href="https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2005_GagneDeci_JOB_SDTtheory.pdf" rel="noopener">Gagné &amp; Deci 2005</a>).</p>
<p>So the honest claim is conditional. Not &quot;explain why and tolerance rises,&quot; but &quot;explain why + admit the cost + leave the choice + let the payoff be felt.&quot; This is the same recipe [metering-honesty](metering-honesty.</p>
<h2>What stays uncertain</h2>
<p>uncertain: no study isolates rationale-before-practice and shows a <em>durable</em> persistence gain on its own; classroom programs that do lift behavior bundle explanation with practice and reflection, with self-reported strategy use and no long-term follow-up. The analogy is also shakier than it looks: open-label placebo effects run ~0.40 on self-report but ~0.02 (null) on objective outcomes (read 2026-06-11 — <a href="https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-14895-z" rel="noopener">Spille et al., Scientific Reports 2025</a>), and a pre-registered replication failed to reproduce the effect at all (read 2026-06-11 — <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9881123/" rel="noopener">Buergler et al. 2023</a>) — so the placebo half mainly moves how people <em>feel</em>, the half teaching cares about least. And the rationale can be a falsehood: for novices or overloaded working memory, the difficulty stops being desirable and restudy wins, so &quot;why difficulty helps&quot; is the wrong sentence to teach exactly there (read 2026-06-11 — <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6099118/" rel="noopener">Chen et al. on element interactivity</a>).</p>
