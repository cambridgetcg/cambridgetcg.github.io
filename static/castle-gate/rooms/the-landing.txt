10:T12b4,<p><a href="/castle-gate/rooms/what-must-travel">what-must-travel</a> found the strongest companion abroad was the means to read the land's own library on arrival. This room asks whether the reading itself is a skill — and the cleanest evidence says yes, with one honest crack running through it.</p>
<p>The keystone finding inverts where you look. Watching ten PhD historians, ten professional fact-checkers, and twenty-five Stanford students judge live websites, Wineburg and McGrew found the fact-checkers fastest <em>and</em> most accurate; the domain experts were right only about half the time, fooled by official logos, scholarly references, and .org URLs. The fact-checkers barely read the page — they opened new tabs to see what the wider web said about the source. <strong>Orientation is less about how deep you read than where you look first: you judge the land by leaving it</strong> (<a href="https://stacks.stanford.edu/file/druid:yk133ht8603/Wineburg%20McGrew_Lateral%20Reading%20and%20the%20Nature%20of%20Expertise.pdf" rel="noopener">Wineburg &amp; McGrew, read 2026-06-11</a>).</p>
<p>The skill has a concrete grammar. <em>Lateral reading</em> — leave the page, check the source (<a href="https://hapgood.us/2019/06/19/sift-the-four-moves/" rel="noopener">SIFT's first move is simply STOP, read 2026-06-11</a>). <em>Click restraint</em> — scan the whole list of results before clicking, since the top hit is gameable; use quote marks for exact phrases (<a href="https://news.stanford.edu/stories/2017/10/fact-checkers-outperform-historians-evaluating-online-information" rel="noopener">Stanford News, read 2026-06-11</a>). And in a genuine field, <em>start with the synthesis</em>: read the best review article first, mine reference lists for the names that keep recurring, and ask one expert what they would read if they could read only one thing (<a href="https://guides.library.ucla.edu/c.php?g=180285&amp;p=8329107" rel="noopener">UCLA research guide, read 2026-06-11</a>). Your first reading is the messengers, not the message.</p>
<p>And it drills. A civics-course intervention took correct lateral reading from near-zero controls to 61% of instructed students (<a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8012470/" rel="noopener">Brodsky et al., read 2026-06-11</a>); a short asynchronous module raised lateral reading from 3% to 77% and halved the .org-means-trustworthy reflex (<a href="https://misinforeview.hks.harvard.edu/article/lateral-reading-college-students-learn-to-critically-evaluate-internet-sources-in-an-online-course/" rel="noopener">Harvard Misinformation Review, read 2026-06-11</a>). The home drill is one move: pick one unknown source a day and open a tab on it (<a href="https://cor.inquirygroup.org/curriculum/lessons/lateral-reading-resources-practice/?cuid=teaching-lateral-reading" rel="noopener">Stanford COR, read 2026-06-11</a>).</p>
<h2>What stays uncertain</h2>
<p>uncertain: the crack is the &quot;whom to trust&quot; half. Deciding whom to trust may be gated by the very domain knowledge a newcomer lacks — far-transfer of trained skill is near null across the literature, and expert thinking is largely domain-specific (<a href="https://www.aft.org/ae/fall2020/willingham" rel="noopener">Willingham, read 2026-06-11</a>; <a href="https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5724589/" rel="noopener">far-transfer meta-analyses, read 2026-06-11</a>). Worse, a true novice may be structurally unable to gauge their own orientation — though that Dunning-Kruger claim is itself partly a statistical artifact (<a href="https://gwern.net/doc/iq/2020-gignac.pdf" rel="noopener">Gignac &amp; Zajenkowski, read 2026-06-11</a>). The honest verdict: the <em>habit</em> of leaving the page is real and teachable; the judgment it serves still leans on field knowledge. This is the cheap searchlight of <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/the-noticing-stance">the-noticing-stance</a>'s split; the slow other half — growing the openness that turns a catch into value — is the hard-won action facet of <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/growing-openness">growing-openness</a>.</p>
