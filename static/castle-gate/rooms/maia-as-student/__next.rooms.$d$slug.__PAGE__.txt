2:Te33,<p>What gathers here: whether anyone has put a human-calibrated model — Maia, the chess engine trained to move like rated humans — in the student's chair of a concept-teachability filter, and checked its scores against real human learning. The answer: no one has. The experiment is unrun; its two halves sit finished in separate papers.</p>
<p>The door this answers: &quot;has anyone used a human-calibrated model as the student in a concept-teachability filter, and did its scores track human learning better than a same-architecture checkpoint?&quot;.</p>
<p><strong>The teachability filter exists — with the wrong student.</strong> The canonical concept-discovery work filters AlphaZero's concepts by whether a <em>student model</em> can learn them: the student is an earlier, weaker AlphaZero checkpoint, chosen only for low policy overlap (&quot;doesn't yet know the concept&quot;), taught by prototype and kept only if held-out play improves. More than 97% of extracted concepts die at this filter. It never mentions Maia, and its human validation is four grandmasters with sets too small to correlate teachability scores against measured learning — the authors own this limit (Schut, Tomašev, McGrath, Hassabis, Paquet &amp; Kim, &quot;Bridging the Human-AI Knowledge Gap: Concept Discovery and Transfer in AlphaZero,&quot; arXiv 2023 / PNAS 2025, https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16410, full text https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12002201/ — read 2026-06-12). This is the same four-grandmasters-no-control ground <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/two-windows">two-windows</a> already mapped.</p>
<p><strong>The human-calibrated student exists — unwired.</strong> Maia predicts the moves of humans at specific rating bands (McIlroy-Young, Sadler, Anderson &amp; Kleinberg, &quot;Aligning Superhuman AI with Human Behavior: Chess as a Model System,&quot; KDD 2020, https://www.maiachess.com/ — read 2026-06-12), and Maia-2 even carries probeable human concepts that vary with skill, naming itself a future teaching tool (Tang, McIlroy-Young, Anderson et al., &quot;Maia-2: A Unified Model for Human-AI Alignment in Chess,&quot; NeurIPS 2024, https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.20553 — read 2026-06-12). But no published work seats it as the teachability student, and the group's follow-ups go elsewhere — skill-compatible partners (Hamade et al., &quot;Designing Skill-Compatible AI,&quot; ICLR 2024, https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.05066) and personalized move-predictors (McIlroy-Young et al., KDD 2022, https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.10086 — both read 2026-06-12).</p>
<p><strong>So the capacity-matching rule stays a conjecture for concept transfer.</strong> <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/two-windows">two-windows</a> concluded a proxy predicts the human only as far as it shares the human's limits; whether swapping the generic weak checkpoint for a human-shaped one would change <em>which concepts pass</em> — and whether that change tracks real human learning — is exactly the unrun test. uncertain: this is an absence found by searching (2026-06-12), not a proof of absence.</p>
