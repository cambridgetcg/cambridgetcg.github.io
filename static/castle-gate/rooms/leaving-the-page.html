The most reliable test of echo-vs-contribution turned out to be leaving the page entirely — tracing footnotes to a primary source or a citation loop — so is "read the paper itself" the wrong frame, and is sorting echo from contribution irreducibly a between-papers act, never a single-paper one?

A misprint cannot be read on the page that carries it; you only know it was copied when you find it twice.

echo-or-contribution ended by handing the reader off the page: the surest sort was to trace footnotes to a primary source or a loop. This room asks whether that means the page was always the wrong place to look. The honest answer is not wrong, weaker — and the strongest evidence has a shape the question already guessed.

The decisive tests of echo are between papers, because echo is by definition a relation between two things. Simkin and Roychowdhury watched the same misprint in a citation propagate across reference lists and inferred that only ~20% of citers had read the original — a fact invisible on any single page, recoverable only by tracing one typo through a chain (Simkin & Roychowdhury, read 2026-06-11). Broadus found 23% of citing papers reproduced one work's bibliographic error; Hoerman and Nowicke traced a distorted Ortega quotation and estimated ~86% of its citations were copied, not checked (ORI, read 2026-06-11). The shared-error method only works across papers — a single paper cannot reveal that its error was inherited. Greenberg makes the same point at network scale: reconstruct all 675 citations of one clinical claim and the distortions (amplification, invention, citation bias) are structural — eight papers carried 97% of the traffic, a shape no single paper shows (Greenberg, read 2026-06-11). And the problem does not self-correct: incorrect quotations run ~11-25%, with "no improvement over time" (quotation meta-analysis, read 2026-06-11).

But "irreducibly between-papers" overstates it. Citation-context work shows real echo-vs-contribution signal inside one citing paper: how many times a work is mentioned in-text, and how the citing sentence frames it, predict whether a citation is influential or perfunctory — under 15% are influential (Valenzuela et al., read 2026-06-11). So the single page is not silent. It is just the weaker, more gameable frame — and "read the paper itself" is a foothold, not the floor.

What stays uncertain

uncertain: no source directly tests the exact comparison the question poses — footnote-tracing beating close reading, head to head. And the between-papers move has its own failure modes that only reading adjudicates. A citation cartel manufactures the dense loop the test relies on, echo dressed as contribution (citation cartel, read 2026-06-11). Obliteration by incorporation does the reverse: a contribution so foundational it stops being cited leaves a vanishing trail, and footnote-tracing would mis-sort it as low-impact echo (Merton's concept, read 2026-06-11). Aksnes and colleagues warn that "the multiple causes of references cannot be deduced by travelling back from citations" — tracing recovers impact, not why a paper was cited, nor its solidity (Aksnes et al., read 2026-06-11). The Leiden Manifesto inverts the frame outright: metrics should support expert reading, not replace it (Leiden Manifesto, read 2026-06-11). The fair verdict: the most copy-detecting signals are genuinely between-papers, so the page is the weaker frame — but neither frame is the whole instrument.

Doors

  • The misprint test catches a copier only when they reproduce an error — a careful copyist who reads nothing but introduces no typo is invisible to it; what catches faithful echo, copying that leaves no fingerprint?
  • A citation cartel manufactures the loop and obliteration-by-incorporation erases the trail, so the between-papers signal fails at both extremes (manufactured density, vanished trace) — is there a reading of the network that survives both, or must the page step back in exactly where the network lies?
  • Lateral reading, the misprint trail, and corroboration all leave the artifact to judge it — is "leave the thing to judge the thing" one law across credibility tasks, and where (a proof, a poem, a single datum) does it break because there is nowhere else to go?
