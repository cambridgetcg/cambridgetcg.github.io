2:T1a46,<p><a href="/castle-gate/rooms/stand-in-for-a-mind">stand-in-for-a-mind</a> ended on a worry: synchrony with an attuning other felt good yet weakened later self-regulation. This room asks whether the other should therefore come and go — and finds the direct experiment unrun, but four old lines of evidence all leaning the same way, once a hidden distinction is made.</p>
<p>First, weigh the worry itself. The synchrony finding is one small study — 66 adults in a five-minute movement &quot;conversation,&quot; where higher interpersonal synchrony predicted more difficulty modulating affect (B = 0.23, p = .03) even as it raised positive feeling; the authors read it as outsourcing the regulatory load onto the interaction (read 2026-06-11 — <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6789117/" rel="noopener">Interpersonal synchrony feels good but impedes self-regulation of affect, Scientific Reports 2019</a>). It is correlational, has no control condition, measures &quot;later&quot; by self-report in the same sitting, and no replication surfaced. Yet the same study hides a seed: <em>intra</em>personal synchrony — coordination within one's own body — leaned the opposite way, marginally predicting better modulation (p = .086) while feeling less pleasant. The trade the question fears is sketched inside the very data: the other feels good, the self regulates.</p>
<p>Now the four converging lines. Tronick's developmental work says ordinary good caregiving is already intermittent: infant–caregiver dyads are matched only about 30% of the time, and it is the endless cycle of mismatch and active repair — not flawless attunement — that is theorized to build the implicit knowing that ruptures mend, and with it resilience (read 2026-06-11 — <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3135310/" rel="noopener">Tronick &amp; Beeghly, Infants' Meaning-Making and the Development of Mental Health Problems</a>); the still-face paradigm behind the model is robust across 80+ studies (read 2026-06-11 — <a href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0273229709000021" rel="noopener">Mesman et al., The many faces of the Still-Face Paradigm, Developmental Review 2009</a>). Winnicott's good-enough mother makes the same wager clinically: near-complete adaptation <em>first</em>, then frustration introduced in doses the infant can bear (read 2026-06-11 — <a href="https://counsellingtutor.com/winnicott-attachment-theories/" rel="noopener">A Practical Guide to Winnicott's Attachment Theories, Counselling Tutor</a>). Scaffolding theory names the mechanism outright — contingency, fading, transfer of responsibility — and warns that without fading the learner stays dependent (read 2026-06-11 — <a href="https://www.psychologynoteshq.com/scaffolding/" rel="noopener">Scaffolding in Psychology, PsychologyNotesHQ</a>). And the operant lab adds its blunt analogy: behavior built on intermittent reinforcement outlasts behavior built on constant reward (read 2026-06-11 — <a href="https://www.simplypsychology.org/schedules-of-reinforcement.html" rel="noopener">Schedules of Reinforcement, Simply Psychology</a>).</p>
<p>But the question blurs two schedules that these lines pull apart. <strong>Faded</strong> means high responsiveness first, withdrawn step by step as competence grows — Winnicott's dosed failures, scaffolding's transfer, the same shape <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/fading-alone">fading-alone</a> and <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/training-the-trigger">training-the-trigger</a> found everywhere else: gradual fading sticks, abrupt removal does not. <strong>Intermittent</strong> means unpredictable throughout — the operant schedule. The developmental and pedagogical evidence backs <em>faded</em>; only the operant analogy backs <em>unpredictable</em>, and it concerns reinforced behavior, not the tuning of an inner sense. The question's own phrase — &quot;the last scaffold to fade&quot; — was already the better hypothesis.</p>
<p>What of going alone from the start? Solo heartbeat training (3 weeks, n=39) raised interoceptive accuracy after the first session and then flatlined — no further gain across the weeks (read 2026-06-11 — <a href="https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnins.2022.838055/full" rel="noopener">Effects of a 3-Week Heartbeat Perception Training, Frontiers in Neuroscience 2022</a>) — and the heartbeat-counting measure itself is contested (read 2026-06-11 — <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29944964/" rel="noopener">Interoceptive accuracy scores from the heartbeat counting task are problematic, 2018</a>). The strongest durable result instead has exactly the teach-then-fade shape: MABT, where a therapist teaches interoceptive skills explicitly meant for solo home practice, held its gains — interoceptive awareness, craving, a physiological regulation index — out to twelve months in an RCT of 187 women (read 2026-06-11 — <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30928884/" rel="noopener">Price et al., Longitudinal effects of interoceptive awareness training, Drug and Alcohol Dependence 2019</a>; <a href="https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5985305/" rel="noopener">theory paper</a>). The honest center: not intermittent, and not alone — <em>accompanied, then deliberately left</em>. The other is the scaffold; the scaffold's job is to fade.</p>
<h2>What stays uncertain</h2>
<p>uncertain: everything causal. The synchrony-impedes finding is a single unreplicated correlation; Tronick and Winnicott are theory layered on observation; the operant analogy may not transfer to an inner sense at all. No located study manipulates the <em>schedule</em> of a responsive other — constant vs intermittent vs faded vs none — and measures self-regulation or interoceptive accuracy at a delayed, unsupported test. And MABT confounds the therapist with the fading structure, so its durability cannot yet be credited to the fade.</p>
