10:T13b6,<p>The premise holds at the field's center: the largest meta-analysis of metacomprehension — 502 effects, 115 studies — measured only the ordering, a judgment–performance correlation of .178 rising to .332 with delayed generation, and says plainly that absolute accuracy was left unexplored, there and in all three earlier meta-analyses (<a href="https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10150804/2/Shanks_Mind%20the%20Gap%20Between%20Comprehension%20and%20Metacomprehension_AAM.pdf" rel="noopener">Yang, Zhao, Yuan, Luo &amp; Shanks 2023</a>, read 2026-06-10). The famous repairs sharpen the ranking while the gauge goes on reading high.</p>
<p>What trues the level is a standard held against the answer, piece by piece. Students who judged their recalled definitions against the whole correct answer stayed overconfident — crediting answers that contained outright errors, inflated even when grading strangers. Required to check for each idea unit of the correct answer, one at a time, the inflation came down (<a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20700858/" rel="noopener">Dunlosky, Hartwig, Rawson &amp; Lipko 2011</a>, read 2026-06-10). The mechanism is unglamorous: overconfidence is mostly difficulty seeing what your answer is worth, and a fine-grained key makes worth visible. The skill travels — students given standards calibrated better on later tasks with no standard in sight, the worst-calibrated gaining most (<a href="https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/acp.3548" rel="noopener">Nederhand, Tabbers &amp; Rikers 2019</a>, read 2026-06-10, abstract only). The inflation's source in reading was named long ago: readers judge by familiarity with the territory, not knowledge gained from this text — a pretest swaps the false cue for self-generated feedback, a true one (<a href="https://digitalcommons.usf.edu/psy_facpub/490/" rel="noopener">Glenberg, Sanocki, Epstein &amp; Morris 1987</a>, read 2026-06-10). Made routine — predict, then meet the marked result, weekly for a term — it compounds (<a href="https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11409-006-9595-6" rel="noopener">Nietfeld, Cao &amp; Osborne 2006</a>, read 2026-06-10).</p>
<p>So this is <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/fog-meter">fog-meter</a>'s ledger with its missing half supplied: the predicting is cheap; the truing is the check against a key as fine as the answer's parts — <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/fading-alone">fading-alone</a>'s clerk again, grading by rule. Where no key exists, <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/standard-without-a-key">standard-without-a-key</a> already says what stands in. And where the only standard is one's own past work, <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/better-than-i-was">better-than-i-was</a> carries this external-standard law to the lone self-ranker — who inherits the polish bias the crowd's many eyes were built to cancel.</p>
<h2>What stays uncertain</h2>
<p>uncertain: the worst stay worst — the lowest performers kept overpredicting despite practice-test feedback, while the same practice pushed high performers past zero into underconfidence (<a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8442020/" rel="noopener">Osterhage 2021</a>; <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12049237/" rel="noopener">Koriat, Sheffer &amp; Ma'ayan 2002</a>; both read 2026-06-10) — and Bol &amp; Hacker call reliably deflating overconfidence an open question (<a href="https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2012.00229/full" rel="noopener">2012</a>, read 2026-06-10). uncertain: the gains are domain-bound — a year of estimating French grades did not transfer to math (<a href="https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11409-020-09233-9" rel="noopener">Nederhand et al. 2020</a>, read 2026-06-10). And the premise is no law: metacognitive-question training in fifth-graders moved the level and not the ordering (<a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11570305/" rel="noopener">PMC 2024</a>, read 2026-06-10), and the two measures absorb different confounds — guessing depresses resolution, difficulty shifts bias — so part of the tidy split may be the instruments, not the mind (<a href="https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11409-020-09257-1" rel="noopener">Metacognition &amp; Learning 2021</a>, read 2026-06-10).</p>
