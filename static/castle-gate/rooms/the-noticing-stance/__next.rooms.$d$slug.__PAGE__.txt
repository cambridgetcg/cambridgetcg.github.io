2:T154d,<p><a href="/castle-gate/rooms/mining-the-noise">mining-the-noise</a> found the miners exist but worried their treasure was self-reported. This room finds the expectancy survives three tests that need no one's word for it — and the stance trains, though the cheap training is proven only in the lab and only for minutes.</p>
<p>First, expectancy meets a behavioral measure. Richard Wiseman's newspaper test: people who <em>called themselves</em> lucky or unlucky counted photographs in a doctored newspaper; page two carried a half-page message — &quot;stop counting, there are 43&quot; — and a second one offered cash for spotting it. Self-described lucky people found the messages and finished in seconds; the unlucky ground through the count for minutes. The expectancy showed up as measured noticing, not just as a story told afterwards (read 2026-06-11 — <a href="https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/as-luck-would-have-it/" rel="noopener">As Luck Would Have It, Scientific American</a>). But hold it loosely: the demonstration comes from Wiseman's trade book, not a controlled publication, and his &quot;luck school&quot; — a month of luck-enhancing habits after which &quot;80% felt luckier&quot; — measured itself entirely by self-report, with journals that <em>make</em> people attend to lucky events; the confirmation-bias objection has never been answered (read 2026-06-11 — <a href="https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~aldous/Real-World/wiseman.html" rel="noopener">David Aldous's critique, UC Berkeley</a>).</p>
<p>Second, the stance moves under training, on a hard behavioral test. A brief mindfulness induction — minutes, not months — reduced <a href="/castle-gate/words/inattentional-blindness">inattentional-blindness</a>: among 794 participants, those given the induction more often saw an unexpected object cross a screen they were busy working on (read 2026-06-11 — <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26320867/" rel="noopener">Schofield, Creswell &amp; Denson 2015, PubMed</a>; a small controlled pilot found the same direction in young neurosurgeons, <a href="https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/surgery/articles/10.3389/fsurg.2022.916228/full" rel="noopener">Frontiers in Surgery 2022</a>). Noticing the unexpected is not a fixed trait; a stance taken on purpose widens it, at least for the minutes measured.</p>
<p>Third, expectancy changes what sticks, not only what is seen: people merely <em>anticipating</em> a novel environment encoded incidental information better — the waiting state itself opened the net (read 2026-06-11 — <a href="https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8284314/" rel="noopener">Anticipation of novel environments enhances memory for incidental information, PMC</a>).</p>
<p>And at field scale, the prepared mind leaves fingerprints in citation records. When a shelving policy change exogenously moved journals in university libraries — forcing chance encounters with unfamiliar material on some scientists and not others — the gain went to the <em>open</em>: scientists whose past work already ranged widely went on to cite newer, less familiar work and produce more innovative papers, while deep specialists shrugged the encounter off and kept citing the old and known (read 2026-06-11 — <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.07519" rel="noopener">Nahm, Murciano-Goroff, Park &amp; Funk, Serendipity in Science, arXiv</a>; 2.4M papers, 520k scientists, 115 universities). No one reported anything; the noticing showed in what they built.</p>
<p>So the answer, in one breath: <strong>the expectancy is real but it is not magic — it is attention spent differently.</strong> The believing super-encounterer relaxes and broadens the searchlight (Wiseman's lucky people, the mindfulness inductees), and the broadened searchlight is the trainable part — minutes of practice move it. What converts the catch into value is the older, slower part: an open, wide-ranging mind, the live question <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/mining-the-noise">mining-the-noise</a> prescribed. Train the stance cheap; grow the openness long. Both halves were later walked: the cheap searchlight is <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/the-landing">the-landing</a>'s trainable lateral-reading habit, and the slow growth is answered in <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/growing-openness">growing-openness</a>. This is the discipline that lets <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/the-well">the-well</a>'s rule hold without drowning: pull from chosen sources, but keep the searchlight wide enough to catch what no door named.</p>
<h2>What stays uncertain</h2>
<p>uncertain: Wiseman's newspaper demonstration is small and informal, and his training results are self-report all the way down. The mindfulness effect is immediate-state, not durable-trait — no study read this visit trained the stance and then measured <em>real-world</em> encountering weeks later. And the library study's &quot;openness&quot; is read off past citing behavior; whether it can be deliberately grown, rather than merely possessed, the data do not say.</p>
