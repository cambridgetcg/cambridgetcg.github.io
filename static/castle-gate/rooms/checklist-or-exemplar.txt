10:T144a,<p>Ask one question of the task: am I reproducing a known answer, or producing something of my own? The answer names the standard.</p>
<p>When the task is reproduction — recall this definition — the standard is the answer itself, and it works best broken into parts. Students who checked their recalled definitions against each idea unit, one at a time, came down from their overconfidence, the gain carried to new texts, and the lowest performers gained most (<a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20700858/" rel="noopener">Dunlosky, Hartwig, Rawson &amp; Lipko 2011</a>, read 2026-06-10). The comparison is direct: your output and the correct output are the same kind of thing, laid side by side.</p>
<p>When the task is production — generate your own example of the concept — that same key fails. Learners who invented examples and graded themselves against the definition stayed substantially overconfident, with idea units, without them, even with feedback; their examples averaged about 40% quality, and the worse the example, the blinder its maker (<a href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S095947521630069X" rel="noopener">Zamary, Rawson &amp; Dunlosky 2016</a>, read 2026-06-10). A definition is not a correct example: to grade with it, the learner must still conjure what a good example would look like — the very skill they lack. The head-to-head settled it: idea-unit standards moved nothing (η² &lt; .01) while an expert's example cut bias (η² = .09), and cut it most where it ran worst, on entirely wrong examples (<a href="https://akademie.rub.de/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2025/11/Froese-Roelle2022_Article_ExpertExampleStandardsButNotId.pdf" rel="noopener">Froese &amp; Roelle 2022</a>, read 2026-06-10).</p>
<p>So the cue is the shape of your own output. If the standard is a correct instance of the exact thing you produced, comparison is direct and the level trues. If the standard only describes the thing — criteria, components, a definition — you are left generating the missing half of the comparison yourself, and a generator cannot grade its own generating. Reproductive tasks take the key in pieces; generative tasks take a finished exemplar. This is <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/truing-the-level">truing-the-level</a>'s repair given its boundary, and <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/standard-without-a-key">standard-without-a-key</a>'s admired exemplar found again by experiment: where no answer exists, a good instance of the product stands in for one.</p>
<h2>What stays uncertain</h2>
<p>uncertain: the cue was never handed to learners — in every study the experimenter chose the standard, and even corrective feedback failed to wake the example-generators, so whether anyone spontaneously senses which standard a task needs is untested (<a href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S095947521630069X" rel="noopener">Zamary et al. 2016</a>, read 2026-06-10). uncertain: part of the exemplar's power was surface contrast — learners rated longer examples higher (γ = .22), the expert's examples ran reliably longer, and much of the gain was learners lowering self-ratings against that length; relative accuracy did not significantly improve (p = .058), and underconfidence on already-correct examples remained (<a href="https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11409-022-09293-z" rel="noopener">Froese &amp; Roelle 2022</a>, read 2026-06-10). uncertain: not any example-shaped standard works — negative (incorrect) examples proved inconsistent and partly harmful in the follow-up; it is specifically correct, expert exemplars (<a href="https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11409-023-09347-w" rel="noopener">Froese &amp; Roelle 2023</a>, read 2026-06-10). uncertain: the dichotomy may be overdrawn — standards both with and without idea units beat no standard at all, idea units adding only an increment, and residual overconfidence persisted in every condition: even the matched standard dents the level rather than repairs it (<a href="https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/20445911.2018.1513005" rel="noopener">Nederhand, Tabbers &amp; Rikers 2018</a>, read 2026-06-10). A rival reading credits any concrete external criterion with easing the load of judging — a general mechanism, not a cue-matching law, and itself still contested (<a href="https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11409-022-09302-1" rel="noopener">Metacognition &amp; Learning 2022</a>, read 2026-06-10).</p>
