2:T188b,<p>Begin with the honest blow: the eye is not free. The skill that produces good answers and the skill that recognizes them are largely one skill — which is why the worst performers in Kruger &amp; Dunning's studies misjudged not only their own work but everyone else's (read 2026-06-10 — <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10626367/" rel="noopener">Kruger &amp; Dunning 1999</a>). Even a machine that generates candidates freely may be unable to check them: GPT-4's self-critique collapsed on puzzles an external verifier handled (read 2026-06-10 — <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.08115" rel="noopener">Stechly, Valmeekam &amp; Kambhampati 2024</a>). So the checking that <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/text-answers-back">text-answers-back</a> demands of every machine answer falls to the reader's eye — the eye this room trains. The foothold <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/asking-uphill">asking-uphill</a> leaned on — <em>enough to recognize an answer</em> — is itself the output of earlier learning, as the SEP's own example concedes: a spelling dictionary is useless to a six-year-old (read 2026-06-10 — <a href="https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/epistemic-paradoxes/" rel="noopener">SEP, Epistemic Paradoxes</a>).</p>
<p>The good news: that earlier learning has shortcuts. The eye trains faster than the hand, by three moves. <strong>Contrast</strong> — set two examples side by side and guess the difference before any explanation. Students who analyzed contrasting datasets and then heard the lecture out-predicted students given a summary of the distinctions plus the same lecture (read 2026-06-10 — <a href="http://aaalab.stanford.edu/assets/papers/earlier/A_time_for_telling.pdf" rel="noopener">Schwartz &amp; Bransford 1998</a>). And shuffle: learners shown twelve painters interleaved recognized new paintings better than learners who binged one painter at a time — while most swore the binge had worked better (read 2026-06-10 — <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3978334/" rel="noopener">Kornell &amp; Bjork 2008, replicated by Verkoeijen &amp; Bouwmeester 2014</a>). <strong>Pointing</strong> — one written page locating the single diagnostic contrast let novices sex day-old chicks near the level of professionals whose eye took years to wear in (read 2026-06-10 — <a href="https://psycnet.apa.org/record/1988-03299-001" rel="noopener">Biederman &amp; Shiffrar 1987</a>); five and a half minutes on rare fingerprint features improved novices <em>and</em> examiners with thirteen years behind them (read 2026-06-10 — <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9288576/" rel="noopener">Growns et al. 2022</a>). Unpointed, the novice eye sees only surface — springs and inclined planes where the expert sees conservation of energy (read 2026-06-10 — <a href="https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1207/s15516709cog0502_2" rel="noopener">Chi, Feltovich &amp; Glaser 1981</a>). <strong>Feedback</strong> — many quick classification attempts, each answered right or wrong at once, reweight attention toward the features that matter (read 2026-06-10 — <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6124488/" rel="noopener">Kellman, Massey &amp; Son</a>); even Kruger &amp; Dunning found a little training in the skill improved the judging of it.</p>
<p>One boundary, one rule. The eye learns only where the world answers back — stable regularities, prompt honest feedback; in low-validity fields no decades of exposure make the feeling trustworthy (read 2026-06-10 — <a href="https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Conditions-for-intuitive-expertise:-a-failure-to-Kahneman-Klein/f1a5fb0c4b9703b3213bc3bd2dfe1f79ee35d511" rel="noopener">Kahneman &amp; Klein 2009</a>). And until yours is trained, never grade by looks-right: borrow calibration — a key, a verdict, a measured outcome — for the feeling is the last thing to calibrate. How the borrowed key actually trues the gauge — piece by piece, deflating the level and not just the ranking — is <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/truing-the-level">truing-the-level</a>'s whole story. When that borrowed calibration is ready to come down, <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/when-the-trade-flips">when-the-trade-flips</a> holds the signs.</p>
<h2>What stays uncertain</h2>
<p>uncertain: the size of the Kruger-Dunning deficit is contested — reanalyses call much of it regression to the mean (read 2026-06-10 — <a href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0160289620300271" rel="noopener">Gignac &amp; Zajenkowski 2020</a>), though the training result is an experimental manipulation, not a correlation. The chick-sexing percentages could not be verified against the paywalled primary text; the dramatic perceptual-module numbers come largely from one lab; the contrasting-cases classrooms held only ~20-36 students — and in that same study, recognizing true statements did <em>not</em> predict the discernment that transferred, so &quot;recognition&quot; may name two different depths. Practitioner testimony pulls the other way: Ira Glass's taste gap says the eye arrives <em>before</em> the hand in creative work (read 2026-06-10 — <a href="https://www.themarginalian.org/2014/01/29/ira-glass-success-daniel-sax/" rel="noopener">Glass, via The Marginalian</a>) — but that eye was likely fed by years of heavy consumption: contrast and feedback by another name, and testimony, not a study.</p>
