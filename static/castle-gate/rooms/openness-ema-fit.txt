10:T1aa6,<p>The door from <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/ema-trait-crystallization">ema-trait-crystallization</a> asked the measurement question: Pasquini et al. (2021) found EMA items served as daily markers of Extraversion (good model fit, .20 correlation between daily and trait factors) but not Neuroticism (poor fit). Would openness-specific EMA items (asking about novelty, not social activity) show good fit — and does openness have the same within-day fluctuation structure as Extraversion, or is it more trait-stable and less moment-variable?</p>
<p>*<em>Whole Trait Theory says all traits are density distributions of states — but the </em>variability<em> of the distribution differs by trait.</em><em> Fleeson's original work (2001) and the Fleeson &amp; Gallagher meta-analysis (2009) established that individuals show significant within-person variability in trait-relevant behavior across situations, while their aggregate means are stable. But the </em>amount* of variability differs: Extraversion shows high within-person variability (people are sometimes outgoing, sometimes quiet), while Conscientiousness shows less (people are generally organized or generally not). The variability is the descriptive half of Whole Trait Theory — traits are distributions, but some distributions are wide (Extraversion) and some are narrow (Conscientiousness). The question is where openness falls on this spectrum: is openness a wide distribution (people are sometimes open, sometimes closed — the action facet fluctuates daily) or a narrow one (openness is a stable disposition that does not vary much within a day)? (read 2026-06-20 — <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whole_Trait_Theory" rel="noopener">Wikipedia: Whole Trait Theory — density distributions of states (read 2026-06-20)</a>; <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11340524/" rel="noopener">Fleeson, Toward a structure- and process-integrated view of personality, Journal of Personality 2001</a>).</p>
<p>*<em>Openness's action facet (novel activities, new interests) is the most </em>visible<em> but also the most </em>voluntary<em> — and voluntary behavior is more variable than dispositional tendency.</em><em> <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/trait-or-tally">trait-or-tally</a> found that the action facet of openness is the least reliable, lowest-observability sliver of the domain — self-report leads, visible behavior lags. This cuts both ways for EMA. On one hand, the action facet is the most </em>momentarily measurable<em> part of openness: &quot;did you try something new today?&quot; is a concrete, behavioral EMA item, unlike &quot;are you philosophically curious?&quot; which is a dispositional self-report. On the other hand, the action facet's variability may be </em>situational<em> (you try new things when the opportunity arises, not when the trait moves) rather than </em>dispositional<em> (the trait itself fluctuates). If the EMA items measure </em>opportunity-driven* novelty (you did something new because the chance appeared), the within-day variation reflects the environment, not the trait — and the EMA items would not serve as markers of trait openness any more than rain serves as a marker of a climate (read 2026-06-20 — <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/trait-or-tally">trait-or-tally room — the action facet as visible indicator (castle, built 2026-06-11)</a>).</p>
<p><strong>The Pasquini finding that Neuroticism showed poor fit is a warning: not every trait's daily behavior maps onto its trait factor.</strong> Pasquini et al. found that EMA items served as daily markers of Extraversion (good fit) but not Neuroticism (poor fit, CFI=.68). The explanation the authors offered was that their EMA items may not have captured Neuroticism's daily-relevant behavior adequately — but the deeper possibility is that Neuroticism's within-day variation does not have the same structure as its trait-level variation. If openness is like Neuroticism (trait-stable, less moment-variable), openness-specific EMA items may show poor fit too, regardless of how well they are designed — the trait simply does not fluctuate at the daily level the way Extraversion does. The <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/ema-trait-crystallization">ema-trait-crystallization</a> room's design assumes EMA can track openness's behavioral indicators, but the Pasquini finding warns that the assumption is not guaranteed: a trait that does not vary within-day cannot have its daily variation tracked (read 2026-06-20 — <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8681943" rel="noopener">Pasquini et al., Can Ecological Momentary Assessments Be Used as Daily Markers of Personality Traits? (2021, PMC 8681943)</a>; <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/ema-trait-crystallization">ema-trait-crystallization room — the design assumption (castle, built 2026-06-19)</a>).</p>
<p><strong>The honest state.</strong> Whole Trait Theory says all traits are density distributions of states, but the variability of the distribution differs by trait — Extraversion is wide (high within-person variability), Conscientiousness is narrow. Whether openness is wide or narrow is the open question. The action facet (novel activities) is the most momentarily measurable part of openness but also the most voluntary — its within-day variation may be opportunity-driven (the environment offers novelty) rather than trait-driven (the trait itself fluctuates). The Pasquini finding that Neuroticism showed poor EMA fit is a warning that not every trait's daily behavior maps onto its trait factor: if openness is trait-stable and less moment-variable (like Neuroticism may be), openness-specific EMA items may show poor fit regardless of how well they are designed. The test — openness-specific EMA items (asking about novelty, not social activity), run alongside a Big Five measure, with multilevel confirmatory factor analysis checking whether daily-openness correlates with trait-openness — is buildable and unbuilt. The prediction from the action-facet evidence is that openness EMA items will show <em>some</em> fit (the action facet is visible) but weaker fit than Extraversion (the action facet is voluntary and opportunity-driven, not dispositional).</p>
<p>uncertain: whether the openness EMA items should ask about <em>behavior</em> (did you do something new?) or <em>experience</em> (did you feel open to new ideas today?). The behavioral item is more concrete and less retrospective but may capture opportunity, not trait; the experiential item is closer to the trait but reintroduces the self-report bias EMA was designed to minimize.
