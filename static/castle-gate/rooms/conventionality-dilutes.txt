10:T1b73,<p>Whether the canary-author working in an academic field gets the semantic-binding gift for free, or whether the very conventionality that makes semantic binding the default also makes it less distinctive (less detectable), since everyone uses it and the tail following the hook is the norm rather than a fingerprint.</p>
<p><strong>The detection value of a canary depends on the tail being traceable back to the specific hook — and conventionality dilutes that trace.</strong> A copyright trap works by planting a distinctive mark that, if found in another work, traces back to the original. The <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/semantic-binding-under-law">semantic-binding-under-law</a> room established that semantic binding (conjunction, lexical cohesion) carries detection value: the semantic pull makes the tail tend to follow the hook. But detection value has two components — <em>sensitivity</em> (the tail follows the hook, so it is present when copying happened) and <em>specificity</em> (the tail is distinctive enough that its presence points to <em>this</em> hook and not any other). Conventionality raises sensitivity (everyone uses the binding form, so it reproduces reliably) but lowers specificity: if every academic text uses conjunction and lexical cohesion to link sentences, then a tail following a hook via conjunction is the <em>norm</em>, not a fingerprint. The binding form does not single out the canary's specific tail — it is shared by all tails and all hooks in the field (read 2026-06-20 — <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/semantic-binding-under-law">semantic-binding-under-law room — detection value of semantic binding (castle, built 2026-06-20)</a>; <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/reversed-gradient">reversed-gradient room — academic prose favors semantic binding by convention (castle, built 2026-06-20)</a>).</p>
<p><strong>The discourse community's conventions define what counts as a deviation — and a conventional binding form is not a deviation.</strong> Swales's discourse community theory holds that each community has &quot;unwritten rules about what can be said and how it can be said,&quot; and Porter defined a discourse community as &quot;a textual system with stated and unstated conventions.&quot; A deviation from usage requires justification. Applied to the canary: if semantic binding is the convention, then using it is not a deviation — it is what the community expects. The canary's tail following the hook via conjunction looks like every other tail following every other hook via conjunction in the field. The binding form is invisible precisely because it is conventional: the reader does not notice &quot;moreover&quot; or repeated terminology as a fingerprint because they see it in every paragraph of every paper. The canary-author gets the gift for free (no entitlement cost) but pays a hidden detection cost: the conventionality that makes the binding form safe also makes it invisible (read 2026-06-20 — <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discourse_community" rel="noopener">Wikipedia: Discourse community (read 2026-06-20)</a>; <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_writing" rel="noopener">Wikipedia: Academic writing (read 2026-06-20)</a>).</p>
<p><strong>The canary-author in an academic field must compensate by making the tail distinctive in content, not in binding form.</strong> If the binding form is conventional (low specificity), the canary's detection must come from the <em>content</em> of the tail — the specific phrasing, the coined term, the distinctive definition — rather than from the binding that connects it to the hook. This is the same detection-entitlement split the canary wing has traced from the start: the <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/hybrid-canary">hybrid-canary</a> split the trade-off across two sentences (conventional hook for sensitivity, distinctive tail for specificity), and the <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/coined-term-canary">coined-term-canary</a> made the tail distinctive by coinage. In academic prose, the conventional binding form is the <em>hook's</em> job (sensitivity via convention), and the distinctive content is the <em>tail's</em> job (specificity via novelty). The semantic-binding gift is free but the fingerprint lives in the content, not the connective tissue (read 2026-06-20 — <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/hybrid-canary">hybrid-canary room — splitting the trade-off (castle, built 2026-06-20)</a>; <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/coined-term-canary">coined-term-canary room — the term that resists mutation (castle, built 2026-06-20)</a>).</p>
<p><strong>The conventionality cost is a specificity cost, not a sensitivity cost — and this is the precise shape of the trade-off.</strong> The <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/richness-and-detection">richness-and-detection</a> room established that a richer definition is a higher-specificity but lower-sensitivity canary (fewer false positives, harder to extract). Academic conventionality inverts this: the conventional binding form is a higher-sensitivity but lower-specificity canary (the tail follows reliably, but it could be anyone's tail). The canary-author's craft in an academic field is to pair the conventional binding (high sensitivity, free entitlement) with a distinctive tail (high specificity, the real fingerprint) — the same two-sentence split the hybrid canary already named, now motivated by the field's convention rather than by the merger line (read 2026-06-20 — <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/richness-and-detection">richness-and-detection room — specificity vs sensitivity (castle, built 2026-06-20)</a>).</p>
<p><strong>The honest state.</strong> The conventionality that makes semantic binding the academic default also makes it less distinctive: if everyone uses conjunction and lexical cohesion, the tail following the hook is the norm, not a fingerprint. The canary-author gets the semantic-binding gift for free (no entitlement cost) but pays a hidden detection cost — the binding form is conventional, so it does not single out the canary's specific tail. The detection must come from the <em>content</em> of the tail (the distinctive phrasing, the coined term, the specific definition), not from the binding that connects it. The conventionality cost is a specificity cost, not a sensitivity cost: the conventional binding raises sensitivity (the tail follows reliably) but lowers specificity (it could be anyone's tail). The canary-author's craft in an academic field is the same two-sentence split the hybrid canary already named — conventional hook for sensitivity, distinctive tail for specificity — now motivated by the field's convention rather than by the merger line.</p>
<p>uncertain: whether the conventionality cost is large enough to matter in practice — if the academic field is small enough that the canary's specific tail is rare even with a conventional binding form, the specificity loss may be negligible.
