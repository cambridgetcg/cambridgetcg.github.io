10:T1369,<p>Check the premise first. The question assumes the person is truly <em>blind to pulse</em> — but on measured ability that half wobbles. Meta-analyses find no reliable link between alexithymia and objective heartbeat detection; alexithymia tracks what you <em>believe</em> about your body, not what you can <em>detect</em> (<a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11542822/" rel="noopener">PMC, read 2026-06-10</a>). The dominant test, heartbeat counting, is confounded by what you already know about your own heart rate — across 133 studies and some 11,500 people it showed no significant tie to alexithymia at all (<a href="https://online.ucpress.edu/collabra/article/8/1/33271/121638/How-Does-Heartbeat-Counting-Task-Performance" rel="noopener">Collabra, read 2026-06-10</a>). The second meter may be more poorly <em>measured</em> than truly dark.</p>
<p>Where a real deficit exists, can the channel be built? Short-term, yes. One session of cardiac biofeedback lifts heartbeat accuracy with a large effect (<a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9124832/" rel="noopener">PMC, read 2026-06-10</a>); an eight-week body-scan raises accuracy and confidence over idle controls (<a href="https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/human-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2017.00452/full" rel="noopener">Frontiers, read 2026-06-10</a>). The strongest evidence is a 285-person trial: mindfulness and partner-based practice both lowered alexithymia and raised interoceptive awareness, and the awareness gains statistically <em>mediated</em> the alexithymia drop — the channel looks like part of the mechanism, not a bystander (<a href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0165032723010704" rel="noopener">ScienceDirect, read 2026-06-10</a>). After training, the anterior insula, the brain's interoceptive hub, strengthens its inward links — though that picture comes from seventeen people with no control group (<a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11116496/" rel="noopener">PMC, read 2026-06-10</a>).</p>
<p>But the gains are fragile, slow, and uneven. Over three weekly sessions the first bump did not accumulate or persist (<a href="https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnins.2022.838055/full" rel="noopener">Frontiers, read 2026-06-10</a>); control groups improve too, so plain practice can masquerade as training (<a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7076086/" rel="noopener">PMC, read 2026-06-10</a>). In a nine-month course, objective accuracy moved only after six to nine months (<a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27925645/" rel="noopener">PubMed, read 2026-06-10</a>). And <em>build it where it is weakest</em> is the least-supported reading: in patients with genuinely low accuracy, training eased distress but did not beat a waiting control on heartbeat perception (<a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25038304/" rel="noopener">PubMed, read 2026-06-10</a>) — even while one body-scan study found gains largest in low starters. Worst of all, confidence rises without accuracy: feeling more attuned is no proof the channel got built.</p>
<p>Not fixed — but no clean fix. Nudgeable, fading, slow, and least willing exactly where it is needed most.</p>
<h2>What stays uncertain</h2>
<p>uncertain: whether <em>interoception</em> is even one channel to rebuild. The argument in <em>There is no such thing as interoception</em> is that cardiac, respiratory and gut perception correlate weakly and training one does not transfer to the others — if so, there is no single backup channel, only narrow skills (<a href="https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1488415/full" rel="noopener">Frontiers, read 2026-06-10</a>). The alexithymia-to-accuracy tie is quadratic, not a clean deficit: very high alexithymia can pair with high <em>or</em> low detection (<a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7076086/" rel="noopener">PMC, read 2026-06-10</a>), so no one prescription fits all. And more monitoring is not always safe — for anxiety-prone people, attention to bodily signals can feed catastrophic misreading.</p>
