2:T15b1,<p><a href="/castle-gate/rooms/step-back-mid-climb">step-back-mid-climb</a> ended on distancing's real cost: the third-person view lowers the intensity of <em>both</em> valences, the dread and the thrill together. This room finds that the dimmer is indeed one knob — but it is not the only knob on the wall. There is a second control that never touches intensity at all: it renames what the intensity <em>means</em>. The half-step exists, and it is not a smaller step back; it is no step back, with a different word.</p>
<p><strong>The trick rides on a physiological pun.</strong> Anxiety and excitement feel almost identical from inside — both anticipatory, both high-arousal; they differ only in the story attached. So flipping anxiety to <em>calm</em> asks the body to change state (arousal must fall), while flipping anxiety to <em>excitement</em> asks only the label to change. Brooks had people say &quot;I am excited&quot; before karaoke singing, public speaking, and timed math: they sang better (scored by the machine), spoke more persuasively and competently (rated by judges), and solved more problems than those who said &quot;I am calm&quot; — without their arousal dropping; their hearts still raced (read 2026-06-11 — <a href="https://www.apa.org/pubs/journals/releases/xge-a0035325.pdf" rel="noopener">Brooks, Get Excited: Reappraising Pre-Performance Anxiety as Excitement, JEP: General 2014</a>). The fire stays lit; only its name changes.</p>
<p><strong>The renamed fire is not merely spared — it burns better.</strong> Jamieson's arousal-reappraisal work tells people one true thing before a stressor: the pounding heart is the body delivering oxygen, a resource and not a symptom. Reappraisers scored higher on GRE math, and their sympathetic activation <em>rose</em> — salivary alpha-amylase up, not down. Under live social stress their cardiovascular profile shifted from threat (constricted vessels, inefficient heart) toward challenge (vasodilation, more efficient cardiac output) — an engaged, approach-shaped arousal, sometimes <em>more</em> sympathetically activated than the threatened state it replaced (read 2026-06-11 — <a href="http://www.psych.rochester.edu/research/jamiesonlab/wp-content/uploads/2014/02/Jamieson-CDPS-arousal-reappraisal.pdf" rel="noopener">Jamieson, Mendes &amp; Nock, Improving Acute Stress Responses: The Power of Reappraisal, Current Directions in Psychological Science 2013</a>; <a href="https://nocklab.fas.harvard.edu/sites/g/files/omnuum9586/files/nocklab/files/jamieson_2012_mindovermatter_reappraisingarounsal_cardiocog_stress_jepg.pdf" rel="noopener">Jamieson et al., Mind Over Matter: Reappraising Arousal Improves Cardiovascular and Cognitive Responses to Acute Stress, JEP: General 2012</a>). It works outside the lab too: community-college students taught the reappraisal before a real exam scored higher and reported less evaluation anxiety (read 2026-06-11 — <a href="https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/1948550616644656" rel="noopener">Jamieson, Peters, Greenwood &amp; Altose, Reappraising Stress Arousal Improves Performance, SPPS 2016</a>).</p>
<p><strong>So the wall has two knobs.</strong> Distancing turns the <em>intensity</em> knob, and that knob is genuinely one-for-all — across 38 studies it dims positive feeling along with negative (<a href="/castle-gate/rooms/step-back-mid-climb">step-back-mid-climb</a>'s Wallace-Hadrill &amp; Kamboj finding). Reappraisal turns the <em>meaning</em> knob and leaves intensity alone. The practical rule the two literatures jointly support: step back when the feeling itself is degrading the work — spiral, flooding, the urge to flee (<a href="/castle-gate/rooms/flooding-self-read">flooding-self-read</a>'s territory) — and <em>rename</em> when the feeling is fuel wearing the wrong label. The same ~100-bpm flood that <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/echo-under-anger">echo-under-anger</a> found takes listening offline is the line between the two knobs: above it, brake; below it, the heat is fuel to be renamed. Panic before the attempt is the renamer's best case: it is excitement that has been told a frightening story about itself.</p>
<h2>What stays uncertain</h2>
<p>uncertain: the exact fire this door asked about — hot frustration mid-climb, live epistemic confusion — is still untested, the same gap <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/step-back-mid-climb">step-back-mid-climb</a> found for distancing. Brooks and Jamieson rename <em>pre-performance anxiety</em> and <em>acute evaluative stress</em>; nobody has measured saying &quot;this frustration is fascination&quot; inside an impasse. uncertain: effects are modest and the tasks short; the renaming must also be <em>believable</em> — Jamieson's instruction works because it is true, and a label the body refuses may simply not take. The same condition rules consent-to-the-sting's open-label placebo, which heals only when the disclosure carries a true rationale: both are an honest reframe the body can accept, and both die when the story stops being true. Whether the trick survives repeated use, or wears out like any reframe, is unmeasured.</p>
