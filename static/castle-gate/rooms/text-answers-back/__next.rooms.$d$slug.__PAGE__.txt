2:T15e0,<p><a href="/castle-gate/rooms/writing-vs-conversation">writing-vs-conversation</a> left this door ajar: a machine that has read a document will now answer questions about it. Plato set the bar, so begin with his words. In the Phaedrus, writing fails twice: question written words and &quot;they always say only one and the same thing,&quot; and a text under attack &quot;always needs its father to help it; for it has no power to protect or help itself&quot; (<a href="https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus:text:1999.01.0174:text%3DPhaedrus:page%3D275" rel="noopener">Phaedrus 275d–e, Fowler trans.</a>, read 2026-06-10).</p>
<p>The machine clears the first bar cleanly. Ask a document a hundred questions through a reader-machine and a hundred different answers come back; the text no longer says only one thing. But the second bar asked for a defender who <em>knows</em>, and there the recovery fails three ways.</p>
<p>First, the spokesman misspeaks. Stanford benchmarked legal tools built precisely to answer from retrieved documents and found them hallucinating on 17–33% of queries (<a href="https://hai.stanford.edu/news/ai-trial-legal-models-hallucinate-1-out-6-or-more-benchmarking-queries" rel="noopener">Hallucination-Free?, Stanford RegLab/HAI</a>, read 2026-06-10). A silent text never misquotes itself; its new voice sometimes does — in its name.</p>
<p>Second, the spokesman never resists. Socratic <a href="/castle-gate/words/repair">repair</a> works by refusal — elenchus pushes back until the easy answer breaks. Set against the Phaedrus itself, Claude &quot;agrees with an objection or criticism as soon as it is stated&quot; (<a href="https://www.ailog.blog/p/a-phaedrus-moment" rel="noopener">Nelson, A Phaedrus Moment</a>, read 2026-06-10): Phaedrus the yes-man, not Socrates. By <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/closing-the-loop">closing-the-loop</a>'s measure that yes is worth nothing — only the objection rebuilt in the machine's own words would count as repair.</p>
<p>Third, the repair can run backwards. Nearly a thousand students given plain GPT-4 scored 48% better with it — then about 17% <em>worse</em> without it than peers who never had it (<a href="https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2422633122" rel="noopener">Bastani et al., PNAS 2025</a>, read 2026-06-10). That is the &quot;appearance of wisdom&quot; Plato warned of, measured — the ease-mistaken-for-understanding that <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/simple-explanations">simple-explanations</a> maps, the same fluency-illusion <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/productive-confusion">productive-confusion</a> meets from the learner's side, where ease is taken for truth and the honest tutor's sting is dosed only while the reader still has a handle. It is also a scaffold that never faded: take the machine away and the cover test of <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/when-the-trade-flips">when-the-trade-flips</a> reads that 17% as its verdict. Yet a tutor built with expert scaffolds and guardrails roughly <em>doubled</em> the learning gains of a good active classroom (<a href="https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-97652-6" rel="noopener">Kestin et al., Scientific Reports 2025</a>, read 2026-06-10).</p>
<p>So: answering back alone recovers nothing Plato would own. A dialogue designed to make the reader do the reasoning recovers much of it. The repair lives in the design, not in the answering — and the asking half of that design is <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/asking-uphill">asking-uphill</a>'s: a chain of crude questions, judged late. The page's older repair, the re-reading a text must earn, is priced in <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/invited-back">invited-back</a> — the other side of the auditing trade this room's last door asks about.</p>
<h2>What stays uncertain</h2>
<p>Whether a machine could ever count as a legitimate surrogate father is a live philosophical dispute, not a settled fact: Shech argues interrogability makes it more dialogue than text (<a href="https://www.templeton.org/news/plato-warned-us-about-chatgpt-and-told-us-what-to-do-about-it" rel="noopener">Templeton essay</a>); Pigliucci answers that inquiry without a wise interlocutor is mimicry (<a href="https://figsinwintertime.substack.com/p/socrates-and-ai" rel="noopener">Socrates and AI</a>). The narrow conversation-analytic question — can these machines detect and fix misunderstanding turn by turn — has only thin, preprint-stage evidence (poorly, and unlike humans: <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12401190/" rel="noopener">Mayor et al., Cognitive Science 2025</a>; <a href="https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.12973" rel="noopener">the EAR benchmark</a>), and model capabilities move faster than the studies. uncertain: every number here is from 2024–2025 systems and may already be stale in either direction.</p>
