2:T1dcf,<p><a href="/castle-gate/rooms/training-the-eye">training-the-eye</a> leaned on a pointer who could name the diagnostic feature. This room asks what happens when no one can — and finds the bottleneck is rarely that the feature is unsayable, only that no one has yet <em>said it out</em>.</p>
<p>Start with the very case that made &quot;tacit&quot; famous. Chick-sexing is told as proof that some skill lives only in the trained eye. But Biederman &amp; Shiffrar did an expert-systems analysis — interviewing sexers of eighteen to thirty-six years' standing — and isolated a single nameable contrast: the genital bead is <strong>convex in males, concave or flat in females</strong>. A roughly one-minute instruction sheet naming that contrast lifted novices about forty points, to near-expert on the lab images (read 2026-06-10 — <a href="https://www.sciepub.com/reference/17257" rel="noopener">Biederman &amp; Shiffrar 1987</a>). The <a href="/castle-gate/words/tacit-knowledge">tacit knowledge</a> was not locked; it was <strong>un-analyzed</strong>. This naming-nudge is the same law <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/words-shape-thought">words-shape-thought</a> found in the Russian-blue studies, read from a second literature — each room the other's strongest evidence. The same move works on statistics no eye weights on its own: a five-and-a-half-minute module on which fingerprint minutiae are <em>rare</em> — and so diagnostic — improved 551 people including fifty-two professional examiners (read 2026-06-10 — <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9288576/" rel="noopener">Growns et al. 2022</a>).</p>
<p>How much stays un-analyzed is the real question. The knowledge-elicitation trade works from a rule of thumb that experts run most decisions on automatic, unverbalized knowledge and omit much of what a novice needs when they narrate their own task — a robust direction, a folklore-grade number (read 2026-06-10 — <a href="https://www.globalcognition.org/cognitive-task-analysis/" rel="noopener">Global Cognition, Cognitive Task Analysis</a>; review, <a href="https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8903544/" rel="noopener">PMC8903544</a>). So the analyst recovers a meaningful slice, never the whole.</p>
<p>When no analyst can reach the feature, two routes remain. <strong>Contrast and feedback alone</strong> build the discrimination without ever naming it: Kellman's perceptual-learning modules raise accuracy <em>and</em> speed across histology, dermatology, radiology by many feedback-driven classification trials (read 2026-06-10 — <a href="https://kellmanlab.psych.ucla.edu/research-perceptual-and-adaptive-learning-in-medicine.php" rel="noopener">Kellman lab</a>), and feedback by itself — with no bottom-up exposure driving it — can install a discrimination (read 2026-06-10 — <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3352973/" rel="noopener">Liu, Lu &amp; Dosher 2012</a>). This is <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/training-the-eye">training-the-eye</a>'s engine, running with the pointer switched off. <strong>Machines naming the feature back</strong> is the newer route and has two real successes. DeepMind mined AlphaZero's latent space, filtered its concepts by <em>teachability</em> and <em>novelty</em>, and taught the survivors to four grandmasters, every one of whom improved on unseen puzzles — one going from none solved to five (read 2026-06-10 — <a href="https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2406675122" rel="noopener">Schut et al., PNAS 2025</a>). And the fingerprint features above were, in effect, the discrimination analyzed out of the data and handed back.</p>
<p>But the machine route mostly disappoints, and the reason sharpens the whole answer. The common explainer — the saliency map, the heat-blob over the image — fails to lift human performance about as often as it helps, across sixty-eight user studies (read 2026-06-10 — <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16042" rel="noopener">Müller 2024</a>). Worse, a machine explanation that exceeds the learner's &quot;cognitive window&quot; — too complex to hold — leaves them performing <em>below</em> no explanation at all (read 2026-06-10 — <a href="https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10994-020-05941-0" rel="noopener">Ai, Muggleton et al. 2021</a>). And the explanatory concept is often <em>harder</em> to learn than the thing it explains (read 2026-06-10 — <a href="https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.09615" rel="noopener">Ramaswamy et al. 2022</a>). The decisive variable is not contrast versus machine. It is <strong>distillation</strong>: a discrimination becomes teachable only when someone — a human analyst, or an ML method constrained to be teachable — reduces it to a low-complexity, human-legible contrast. That is exactly what Biederman did by hand for the chick. Where the feature cannot be so reduced — a network reads age and sex and cardiac risk off a retinal photograph that no clinician can interpret (read 2026-06-10 — <a href="https://www.nature.com/articles/s41551-018-0195-0" rel="noopener">Poplin et al. 2018</a>) — the machine plainly <em>uses</em> a real feature it cannot yet hand to a human.</p>
<p>So: contrast and feedback always work and need no pointer; a named feature, when one can be distilled, works faster and even re-trains experts; and a machine helps only when its answer survives the same shrinking the human analyst performs. The eye <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/training-the-eye">training-the-eye</a> trains is the floor that never fails; the named feature is the elevator, and it runs only when someone fits it into the cognitive window. And the elevator has a floor of its own: <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/precise-across-time">precise-across-time</a> holds the other half of the law — name a feature finer than its category and the naming erases the very precision it meant to keep — so together the two rooms bound when to name and when to stay silent. And the silence is not willed: <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/choosing-not-to-name">choosing-not-to-name</a> finds the name fires on its own the moment one exists, so staying silent means starving the label, never ordering it quiet.</p>
<h2>What stays uncertain</h2>
<p>uncertain: there is no clean horse-race — no rigorous trial pitting pure contrast-plus-feedback against feature instruction on the <em>same</em> perceptual task with transfer and retention measured, so their relative efficiency is essentially unknown. The concept-based explainers in dermatology and pathology demonstrate the model <em>can</em> articulate human-legible concepts but almost never test whether a learner's own unaided skill improves after studying them. The AlphaZero transfer is four elite players, no control group — whether machine-found concepts teach ordinary learners, or transfer out of closed-world games into noisy medical perception, is untested. And whether the truly un-articulable features (retinal sex, some histopathology gestalts) are <em>in principle</em> distillable or permanently locked in the network is open.</p>
