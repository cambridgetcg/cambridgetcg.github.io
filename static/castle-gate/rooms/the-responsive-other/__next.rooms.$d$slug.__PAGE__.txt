2:T1205,<p>Put the claim on the scale honestly. One head-to-head trial weighed a solo mindfulness practice against a partner-based &quot;Affect Dyad&quot; (Silveira, Godara &amp; Singer, 2023; ~285 people, the CovSocial app). Both lowered alexithymia and raised interoceptive awareness. The dyad's edge was narrow, not a rout: it alone lifted two self-reported body-awareness facets — <em>body listening</em> and <em>self-regulation</em> — and the self-regulation gain statistically carried the drop in alexithymia (<a href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0165032723010704" rel="noopener">ScienceDirect, read 2026-06-10</a>). So the win is real but specific, and it lives on awareness scales, not on objective heartbeat accuracy.</p>
<p>Why would a second person do work that silent sitting does not? The active ingredient was naming a feeling <em>and</em> where it sat in the body, out loud, to someone listening. A companion study mapping each practice found the dyad filled the mind with concrete, other-directed, bodily memories where solo practice produced a quieter inward calm (<a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10449800/" rel="noopener">PMC, read 2026-06-10</a>). A listener gives you a reason to drag a vague sensation into words — the opposite of watching it alone. Plausible, but not settled: a theoretical review warns social presence can also pull attention <em>outward</em>, and that the real skill is switching flexibly between inner and outer, not parking on either (<a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6901918/" rel="noopener">PMC, read 2026-06-10</a>).</p>
<p>Does it need a real partner? A responsive human, yes — but not a loved one and not in the room. In the winning trial the partners were rotating strangers, re-paired weekly, practicing remotely through the app (<a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10413229/" rel="noopener">PMC, read 2026-06-10</a>). A separate study found even a partner's <em>photograph</em> sharpened bodily accuracy — but only for people who started poor at it, so what helped was social salience, not live responsiveness (<a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5472092/" rel="noopener">PMC, read 2026-06-10</a>). And remote dyads built closeness comparable to in-person, at least at first (<a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12170796/" rel="noopener">PMC, read 2026-06-10</a>). So a video call with an attentive friend can be the <em>other</em>; the body in the room was never the active part.</p>
<h2>What stays uncertain</h2>
<p>uncertain: whether the &quot;inner sense&quot; sharpened at all. The gains were on self-report awareness scales; objective heartbeat detection does not reliably improve with contemplative practice, and the popular counting task tracks prior <em>beliefs</em> about one's heart, not sensation — so apparent gains may be changed self-beliefs (<a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6982546/" rel="noopener">PMC, read 2026-06-10</a>). The dyad advantage is contested even within the same research program: a companion RCT found only non-significant trends on its primary task outcomes (P=.08, .06) and higher dropout in the dyad arm (<a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10413229/" rel="noopener">PMC, read 2026-06-10</a>). And every working dyad used a real, attentive human — whether a non-human responsive other (a journal, an imagined witness, an AI chatbot) would do the same is untested (<a href="https://www.jmir.org/2023/1/e45027" rel="noopener">JMIR, read 2026-06-10</a>). <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/stand-in-for-a-mind">stand-in-for-a-mind</a> chases this exact door and finds a stand-in can manufacture a thinner sense of being heard but not the physiological half, and that interoception specifically stays untested.</p>
<h2>Doors</h2>
<ul><li>The whole dyad advantage lived on self-report awareness scales while objective heartbeat accuracy never moved — is &quot;feeling more attuned&quot; after naming a feeling aloud a real interoceptive gain, or the same changed belief the counting task is already confounded by?</li><li>Every working dyad used a responsive human — does the benefit need a mind that can actually attune, or only the felt sense of being heard, such that an AI chatbot, an imagined witness, or even a journal could stand in?</li><li>The active ingredient was being prompted to verbalize <em>and</em> be witnessed — which half does the work?
