2:T1a87,<p><a href="/castle-gate/rooms/machine-distillation">machine-distillation</a> found teachability made a computed score — but scored against a student AI. This room asks whether that stand-in student's window opens onto the same garden as a human's, and finds the correlation never measured, the divergences well mapped, and one clean rule: a proxy predicts the human only as far as it shares the human's limits.</p>
<p>Start with what the AlphaZero pipeline actually established. The student was a partially-trained AlphaZero checkpoint — same architecture, same training world as the teacher — and the teachability filter it ran discarded 97.6% of candidate concepts. The human validation was four grandmasters, each shown a <em>different</em> concept set of 36–48 puzzles, improving on average by less than one puzzle (0.85, SE 0.12) — significant, but an existence proof, not a calibration. No concept that failed the AI filter was ever shown to a human, so there is no negative control; and the authors themselves allow that the gains may partly reflect priming to hunt for counterintuitive moves rather than the concepts themselves (read 2026-06-11 — <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12002201/" rel="noopener">Schut et al., Bridging the human–AI knowledge gap, PNAS 2025, full text</a>). So the question's premise sharpens: the AI student's score has never been <em>correlated</em> with human learnability across concepts, anywhere.</p>
<p>What evidence exists points to one governing rule. In the cleanest direct test, optimal teaching sets computed from a <em>limited-capacity</em> model of human category learning lifted real learners from ~79% to ~92% accuracy — and the unlimited-capacity version of the same model predicted human performance poorly (read 2026-06-11 — <a href="https://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~jerryzhu/career/pub/nips14OptTeachGCM.pdf" rel="noopener">Patil, Zhu, Kopeć &amp; Love, Optimal Teaching for Limited-Capacity Human Learners, NeurIPS 2014</a>). The proxy must carry the student's constraints. The AlphaZero checkpoint is, in those terms, an unlimited-capacity, same-architecture, same-perception learner — the mannequin built to the tailor's own measure.</p>
<p>The windows part ways along at least three seams. <strong>Ordering:</strong> humans generalize compositionally only under the right curriculum, and vanilla networks reproduced that human curriculum-sensitivity only after being given a special gating mechanism (read 2026-06-11 — <a href="https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2205582119" rel="noopener">Dekker, Otto &amp; Summerfield, Curriculum learning for human compositional generalization, PNAS 2022</a>); theory says heavy overparameterization washes curriculum effects out of deep nets entirely (read 2026-06-11 — <a href="https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10561397/" rel="noopener">An analytical theory of curriculum learning in teacher–student networks</a>). <strong>Perception:</strong> networks learn happily from &quot;non-robust features&quot; that are predictive yet invisible to people (read 2026-06-11 — <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.02175" rel="noopener">Ilyas et al., Adversarial Examples Are Not Bugs, They Are Features, 2019</a>), and on cue-conflict images humans classify by shape ~95% of the time where an ImageNet ResNet runs 70–80% on texture (read 2026-06-11 — <a href="https://openreview.net/forum?id=Bygh9j09KX" rel="noopener">Geirhos et al., ImageNet-trained CNNs are biased towards texture, ICLR 2019</a>) — though the gap is narrower than the caricature: people can guess a machine's labels for fooling images above chance (read 2026-06-11 — <a href="https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-019-08931-6" rel="noopener">Zhou &amp; Firestone, Humans can decipher adversarial images, Nature Communications 2019</a>). <strong>Appetite:</strong> humans learn from one or a few examples through causal models and compositionality where networks want orders of magnitude more data (read 2026-06-11 — <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.00289" rel="noopener">Lake et al., Building Machines That Learn and Think Like People, 2017</a>). A concept could sail through the AI filter on a feature no human window admits at all.</p>
<p>The newest stand-ins repeat the lesson at scale. LLMs simulating students predicted real test-item difficulty at r ≈ 0.75–0.82 — but only with a tuned simulation pipeline; direct prompting scored near zero, and models <em>better</em> at math were <em>worse</em> at playing a struggling student (read 2026-06-11 — <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.09953" rel="noopener">Take Out Your Calculators, arXiv 2026</a>). Across 489 test items and 11 models, no model–prompt pair reliably impersonated an average student (read 2026-06-11 — <a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2507.08232v1" rel="noopener">Can LLMs Reliably Simulate Real Students' Abilities?, arXiv</a>) — the &quot;competence paradox&quot;: a capable model playing a limited learner produces fluent but unreal errors (read 2026-06-11 — <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05473" rel="noopener">Towards Valid Student Simulation with Large Language Models, arXiv 2026</a>). The same constraint <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/machine-distillation">machine-distillation</a> ended on, now with a sign on it: distillation is computable insofar as the learner is modelable — and the model must be of the <em>learner</em>, limits and all, not of another teacher. The window the machine must respect is the same cognitive window <a href="/castle-gate/rooms/naming-the-tacit">naming-the-tacit</a> found by trial and error.</p>
<h2>What stays uncertain</h2>
<p>uncertain: the central number — a concept-level correlation between AI-student teachability and human learnability — simply does not exist yet; everything above triangulates it from neighboring fields. Whether any of the surviving 2.4% of AlphaZero concepts were AI-teachable but human-unlearnable is unknown, since humans only ever saw author-curated survivors. And chess may flatter the proxy: its concepts can be shown as concrete move sequences half-inside a grandmaster's existing vocabulary, unlike non-robust visual features.</p>
