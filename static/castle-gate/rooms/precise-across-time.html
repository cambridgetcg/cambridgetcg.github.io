If exact number survives in the moment but collapses once memory enters, where exactly does the wordless mind's grip fail — is it number alone, or anything that must be held precisely across time?

A row of stones laid beside a row of nuts holds the count perfectly — until you cover the nuts, and the exactness leaks away like water from a cupped hand.

thinking-without-words left the wordless mind succeeding at exact number in the moment and failing once memory entered. This room finds the failure is not about number at all. It is about holding anything precise and discrete across a delay with no symbol to carry it.

Watch where the Pirahã grip actually slips. Frank, Everett, Fedorenko & Gibson split the matching tasks by memory load: with the target row in view, the Pirahã matched large sets exactly; once the target was hidden or had to be carried after removal, accuracy fell as the sets grew (read 2026-06-10 — Frank et al. 2008; MIT News). Their conclusion is the hinge of this room: number words do not create the concept of number — they are "a cognitive technology for keeping track of cardinality across time, space and changes in modality." The grip fails in the carrying, not the seeing. Gordon's earlier study showed the same break, with error scaling with set size — the signature of an analog estimate, not a count (read 2026-06-10 — Gordon 2004); his stronger Whorfian reading, that without words they cannot conceive exact quantity, is the contested end (read 2026-06-10 — Scientific American on the dispute).

Two findings pull the answer wider than memory. First, that the in-view success leans on a non-symbolic handle: visible one-to-one correspondence does the tracking a word would do. Deaf Nicaraguan homesigners — adults with no count list — failed exact large-set matching even with the target visible, when the task blocked physical pairing (read 2026-06-10 — Spaepen et al. 2011). So the true variable is not delay alone but *whether any discrete handle — a word or a visible pairing — is at hand. Second, that the failure is not unique to number. Held across a delay, an exact colour shade drifts toward the prototype of its colour category* — and colours that sit near a category's center are remembered best (read 2026-06-10 — Bae, Olkkonen, Allred & Flombaum 2015; the drift grows with the delay, Olkkonen et al. 2014). The same shape as number: the analog value decays toward a category anchor while the discrete label stays put.

The mechanism reads as a code. The approximate sense of quantity obeys Weber's law — its noise grows with the magnitude — so a large analog estimate is already blurred in the moment and only blurs further across a delay, sliding toward a categorical center. A discrete symbol — "seventeen," a numeral, a tally — is a noise-free token that does not blur into "sixteen" over ten seconds; laid over the noisy analog channel, it works like a discretizing, error-correcting code: round the wobbly magnitude onto the symbol once, then carry the symbol, not the magnitude. (The "error-correcting" phrasing is this room's synthesis; Frank et al.'s own "technology for keeping track across time" is the same thought.) That the handle need not be a word is why exact number survives catastrophic language loss: severely agrammatic patients who could no longer parse a sentence still solved structure-dependent arithmetic (read 2026-06-10 — Varley et al. 2005), and a globally aphasic patient kept multidigit calculation intact (read 2026-06-10 — Rossor et al. 1995). Once the symbolic system is installed, exactness no longer needs live language.

And the trade cuts both ways — the mirror of meaning-between-minds's loss in transit. A symbol preserves discrete content beautifully but degrades fine continuous detail, because the label throws away what falls below its categories. Describe a face, or a wine if you are not a trained taster, and your later recognition gets worse — the coarse verbal code overwrites the fine perceptual one (read 2026-06-10 — verbal overshadowing, Schooler; Melcher & Schooler 1996). watching-the-watcher caught the same overwrite live: narrating insight-shaped work dropped solutions from 57% to 13%. So the principle, stated whole: analog traces drift toward category anchors across time; discrete handles do not — which is why anything that must be held precisely and discretely across a delay wants a symbol, and why pinning a symbol on something whose value is sub-categorical and continuous can lose the very precision you meant to keep. drift-across-dimensions walks through this room's first door and finds the colour drift is one law across length and duration too — a category pulls only when used, so a label helps a near-boundary value and harms a sub-categorical one.

What stays uncertain

uncertain: whether the operative variable is the memory delay or the availability of a discrete handle. The homesigners' failure with the target visible (but unpairable) muddies the clean "only-fails-across-delay" story and leans the answer toward "no-handle," but it is not decisive. The Whorfian magnitude stays contested — Everett reads the Pirahã's missing number words as cultural ("immediacy of experience"), not a cognitive limit (read 2026-06-10 — Everett), with Frank's "tool not concept" the consensus middle. Some cross-domain pillars are individually shaky: the claim that language is the combiner of geometry and landmark (read 2026-06-10 — Hermer-Vazquez et al.) is weakened by aphasia and counterbalancing studies; verbal overshadowing is real but modest and moderated by expertise. And it is unproven that colour-drift and number-noise share one mechanism rather than merely rhyming.

Doors

  • The drift-to-prototype is shown for colour — does it hold for any precise analog dimension carried across a delay (line length, pitch, duration), and does having a fine-grained label set for that dimension reduce the drift, as the error-correcting-code reading predicts?
  • A symbol saves discrete content but overwrites fine perceptual detail — is there a measurable crossover, set by how much of what must be kept is categorical versus sub-categorical, where naming flips from help to harm?
