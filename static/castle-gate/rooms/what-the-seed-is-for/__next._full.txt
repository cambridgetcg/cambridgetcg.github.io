10:T14f6,<p><a href="/castle-gate/rooms/seeded-fingerprint">seeded-fingerprint</a> ended on the courts' warning, and the cases confirm it cleanly. In <em>Feist v. Rural Telephone</em> (1991), four fictitious phone listings proved the copying beyond dispute — and Rural lost anyway, because facts belong to no one (<a href="https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/499/340/" rel="noopener">Feist, 499 U.S. 340, Justia</a>, read 2026-06-12). Fred Worth's fake &quot;Columbo's first name: Philip&quot; surfaced in Trivial Pursuit; his $300M suit was dismissed — fabricated facts presented as facts are still facts (<a href="https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F2/827/569/3179/" rel="noopener">Worth v. Selchow &amp; Righter, 9th Cir. 1987</a>, read 2026-06-12). The purest modern case is Genius v. Google: Genius watermarked its lyrics with curly-and-straight apostrophes spelling <strong>REDHANDED</strong> in Morse code, proved Google's partner copied them — and lost at every level, because Genius never owned the lyrics (<a href="https://www.loeb.com/en/insights/publications/2022/03/ml-genius-holdings-llc-v-google-llc" rel="noopener">Loeb &amp; Loeb case note</a>, read 2026-06-12). Perfect detection, zero <a href="/castle-gate/words/entitlement">entitlement</a>.</p>
<p>So what is the seed for? The answer the cases give: <strong>everything except the lawsuit it cannot win — and the lawsuit too, wherever some other law holds.</strong></p>
<ul><li><strong>Evidence, routed to whatever claim exists.</strong> The seed converts the   unprovable (&quot;did they copy or compile independently?&quot;) into the provable, and   that proof spends wherever <em>any</em> cause of action stands: a license contract   (<em>ProCD v. Zeidenberg</em> let an unenforceable-by-copyright phone database be   fenced by shrinkwrap contract — <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ProCD,_Inc._v._Zeidenberg" rel="noopener">Wikipedia</a>,   read 2026-06-12), the EU's database right, which protects investment with no   originality needed (<a href="https://europa.eu/youreurope/business/running-business/intellectual-property/database-protection/index_en.htm" rel="noopener">Directive 96/9/EC</a>,   read 2026-06-12), or plain copyright where the bar sits lower: the UK's   Ordnance Survey proved copying by stylistic fingerprints across 500+ AA   publications and took ~£20M at the courthouse door   (<a href="https://www.motortrader.com/general-news/aa-and-ordnance-survey-agree-a-20m-settlement-12-03-2001" rel="noopener">Motor Trader, 2001</a>,   read 2026-06-12).</li><li><strong>Intelligence.</strong> Knowing <em>who</em> copies, even when you do nothing legal about   it. Google seeded ~100 nonsense queries like &quot;hiybbprqag,&quot; watched them   surface in Bing, and never sued — it leaked the story and let the   embarrassment work (<a href="https://www.cnn.com/2011/TECH/web/02/02/google.bing.sting/index.html" rel="noopener">CNN, 2011</a>,   read 2026-06-12). A plus-addressed email names which company leaked you.</li><li><strong>Alarm.</strong> Security canarytokens — a fake key or document no honest hand   would ever touch — make detection itself the whole product: a near-zero   false-positive intrusion bell, no pretense of suing anyone   (<a href="https://canary.tools/" rel="noopener">Thinkst Canary</a>, read 2026-06-12).</li><li><strong>Deterrence and reputation.</strong> A careful competitor who cannot tell seed from   fact must verify everything independently — which is exactly what the seeder   wants. And public proof of copying imposes costs courts won't: Genius lost   the case and won the headline.</li></ul>
<p>The newest planting is for machines: unique trap sequences inserted in text before publication can later reveal, by membership inference, that a language model trained on it — detectable in a 1.3B-parameter model only when a long sequence repeated ~1,000 times (AUC=0.75) (<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09363" rel="noopener">Meeus et al., Copyright Traps for LLMs, ICML 2024</a>, read 2026-06-12). Same old structure: the seed proves the copying; whether the copying is a wrong remains the separate, contested question.</p>
<p>So the room's answer: detection and entitlement come apart <em>by design</em>. The seed was never the right itself — it is a fact-maker, and a fact spends in many markets: court where the law cooperates, the settlement table, the newsroom, the security pager, the quiet ledger of who can be trusted.</p>
<h2>What stays uncertain</h2>
<p>uncertain: And the seed can rot into truth: the paper town Agloe acquired a real general store, making the trap entry accurate and useless (<a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fictitious_entry" rel="noopener">Fictitious entry, Wikipedia</a>, read 2026-06-12).</p>
