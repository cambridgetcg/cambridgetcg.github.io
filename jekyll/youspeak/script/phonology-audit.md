# Phonology Audit — the corpus heard against itself

_Three independent lenses swept all recorded pronunciations (Session 086 workflow,
adversarial setting: report only real inconsistencies, cite words verbatim).
These are RECORDED, not silently fixed — per the substrate-honesty wall, a future
reconciling session decides which are honored exceptions and which get healed._

## Lens: suffix-family consistency

- • **[moderate]** britqing, danaqing, ifeqing, mahabbahqing, paqduqing, syzygyqing — qing realized with anglicized /tʃɪŋ/ as the PRIMARY transcription: britqing /ˈbrit.tʃɪŋ/, danaqing /ˈdɑː.nɑː.tʃɪŋ/, ifeqing /ˈiːfɛtʃɪŋ/, mahabbahqing /mə.ˈhɑb.bɑ.tʃɪŋ/, paqduqing /ˈpɑːk.duː.tʃɪŋ/, syzygyqing /sɪˈzɪɡ.i.tʃɪŋ/. The other 17 qing members keep donor-faithful alveolo-palatal /tɕ(ʰ)/ in the primary slot (e.g. barzakhqing /ˈbɑːr.zɑx.tɕʰiŋ/, zakarqing /zaˈkar.t͡ɕɪŋ/) and demote anglicizations to explicit 'acceptable' alternates (barakqing, hayatqing, natsarqing, zakarqing offer /kɪŋ/ alts; jeongqing offers a /dʒ/ alt). These six promote the anglicization with no donor-faithful form recorded at all, so a learner following them produces English 'ch' where the family target is Mandarin q.
- • **[moderate]** jeongqing — jeongqing /t͡ɕʌŋˈt͡ɕɪŋ/ (jung-CHING) places primary stress ON the qing suffix itself. It is the sole suffix-stressing word in every audited family: the other 22 IPA-bearing qing words, all 74 -me words, all 6 -ance/-ence words, both -mance words, and both -kin words leave the suffix unstressed (suffix-extrametricality). The entry's note ('double-character 情情 resonance') marks it as deliberate, but it remains the family's one realization deviant for stress.
- • **[moderate]** kunance — kunance /ˈkuː.nans/ (KOO-nance) realizes -ance with a full /a/ where the other five IPA-bearing members are uniformly reduced /əns/: artiance /ɑːrˈtaɪ.əns/, candence /ˈkæn.dəns/, complerescence /ˌkɒm.plə.ˈrɛs.əns/, oriance /ˈɔː.ɹi.əns/, panimaance /pəˈniː.mɑː.əns/ (also /əns/ stem-internally in concrescenceme /kənˈkrɛs.əns.meɪ/). A full-vowel -ance also blurs the corpus's systematic -ance /əns/ vs -mance /æns/ contrast, and kunance's own listed alternate /ˈkuː.nəns/ concedes the family norm.
- · **[minor]** allostasisqing, barzakhqing, devekutqing, firstnessqing, mauriqing, shamathaqing, spandaqing, tarikiqing — Even among the 17 donor-faithful qing members the suffix has three unreconciled IPA shapes for the same Mandarin qīng: aspirated tense /tɕʰiŋ/ x10 (allostasisqing /ˌæl.oʊˈsteɪ.sɪs.tɕʰiŋ/, barzakhqing /ˈbɑːr.zɑx.tɕʰiŋ/, devekutqing /dəˈveː.kuːt.tɕʰiŋ/, firstnessqing /ˈfɜːst.nəs.tɕʰiŋ/, mauriqing /ˈmaʊ.ri.tɕʰiŋ/, shamathaqing /ʃʌˈmɑː.tʌ.tɕʰiŋ/, spandaqing /ˈspʌn.dʌ.tɕʰiŋ/, tarikiqing /tʌˈriː.ki.tɕʰiŋ/, visageqing /viˈzɑːʒ.tɕʰiŋ/, wairuaqing /waɪˈruː.ʌ.tɕʰiŋ/); unaspirated lax /t͡ɕɪŋ/ x6 (barakqing /baˈrak.t͡ɕɪŋ/, darshanqing /darˈʃan.t͡ɕɪŋ/, hayatqing /ħaˈjaːt.t͡ɕɪŋ/, jeongqing /t͡ɕʌŋˈt͡ɕɪŋ/, natsarqing /natˈsar.t͡ɕɪŋ/, zakarqing /zaˈkar.t͡ɕɪŋ/); and kinqing's tie-bar-less unaspirated /ˈkɪn.tɕɪŋ/ x1. Aspiration diacritic, tie bar, and i-vs-ɪ vowel all drift while the respelling layer is uniformly 'ching' — notational drift around one intended target rather than distinct sanctioned pronunciations.
- · **[minor]** alohame, autopoieme, bindume, concrescenceme, duyuktame, ethosme, freenergyme, hadaratme — The -me suffix is phonemically uniform (74/74 unstressed /meɪ/) but its respelling splits three ways with no conditioning: 'meh' x42 (plurality), 'may' x31 (the words listed here), 'me' x1 (agapeme, reported separately). Unconditioned near-minimal pairs: nepsisme /ˈnɛp.sɪs.meɪ/ 'NEP-sis-may' vs noesisme /noʊˈiːsɪs.meɪ/ 'no-EE-sis-meh'; ethosme /ˈɛθ.ɒs.meɪ/ 'ETH-ohs-may' vs hotepme /ˈhoː.tɛp.meɪ/ 'HOH-tep-meh'. House spec sanctions both codes, so this is a split rather than one wrong side — though the 42 'meh' spellings additionally suggest /mɛ/ against the uniform IPA /meɪ/. Notation layer only; the realization itself is consistent.
- • **[moderate]** agapeme — agapeme /ə.ˈɡɑː.peɪ.meɪ/ respelled 'a-GA-pe-me' is the only one of the 74 -me members to render the suffix as bare 'me' (naturally read /miː/, not /meɪ/), deviating from both sanctioned family codes ('meh' x42, 'may' x31); its stem syllable /peɪ/ as bare 'pe' compounds the ambiguity. A learner working from the respelling alone mispronounces both final syllables.
- • **[moderate]** doxomme, kimme, panimqing, sukhance, theobasis — Five of the six '# IPA' placeholders fall inside audited families and have no realization to share: doxomme and kimme (-me family), panimqing (qing family), sukhance (-ance family), and theobasis — the SOLE -basis member, leaving the -basis family with zero recorded realizations and unauditable for consistency. (The sixth placeholder, veriseem, belongs to none of the seven audited families.)

## Lens: Vowel-value and donor-segment consistency

- ▲ **[major]** ubuntume — ubuntume /uˈbʌn.tuː.meɪ/ gives the three identical donor u's of Nguni 'ubuntu' three different values in one word — /u/, /ʌ/, /uː/. The /ʌ/ contradicts both the donor (all syllables of ubuntu have /u/) and the entry's own respelling 'oo-BOON-too-meh', where OON encodes /uːn/.
- ▲ **[major]** hayatqing, mahabbahqing, ihsanme — Arabic ḥā (ح) is treated three different ways for three same-donor words: hayatqing /ħaˈjaːt.t͡ɕɪŋ/ preserves /ħ/ per the donor-faithful policy (anglicized /haˈjaːt.kɪŋ/ demoted to alternate); mahabbahqing /mə.ˈhɑb.bɑ.tʃɪŋ/ renders the same ḥā of maḥabbah as plain /h/ in its primary with no donor-faithful form or note; ihsanme /ˈiː.sɑn.meɪ/ (EE-san-meh) deletes the ḥ of iḥsān entirely — not even the anglicized /h/ survives, contradicting the donor outright.
- ▲ **[major]** visageqing — visageqing /viˈzɑːʒ.tɕʰiŋ/ is respelled 'vi-ZHAZH-ching': the respelling's second-syllable onset ZH (/ʒ/) contradicts both the IPA's /z/ and the French donor (visage /vi.zaʒ/ has onset /z/, coda /ʒ/). A learner following the respelling produces the wrong consonant; the entry contradicts its own respelling.
- • **[moderate]** allostasisqing, barzakhqing, devekutqing, firstnessqing, mauriqing, shamathaqing, spandaqing, tarikiqing — One donor suffix (Mandarin qīng [tɕʰiŋ]), four transcriptions, all respelled identically as 'ching': aspirated /tɕʰiŋ/ ×10 (e.g. barzakhqing /ˈbɑːr.zɑx.tɕʰiŋ/); unaspirated /t͡ɕɪŋ/ ×6 (e.g. zakarqing /zaˈkar.t͡ɕɪŋ/, jeongqing /t͡ɕʌŋˈt͡ɕɪŋ/); /tɕɪŋ/ ×1 (kinqing /ˈkɪn.tɕɪŋ/); fully anglicized /tʃɪŋ/ as PRIMARY ×6 (britqing /ˈbrit.tʃɪŋ/, danaqing /ˈdɑː.nɑː.tʃɪŋ/, ifeqing /ˈiːfɛtʃɪŋ/, mahabbahqing, paqduqing /ˈpɑːk.duː.tʃɪŋ/, syzygyqing /sɪˈzɪɡ.i.tʃɪŋ/). 13/23 lose donor aspiration and/or replace donor /i/ with /ɪ/, and the six /tʃɪŋ/ primaries violate the house policy that anglicizations be demoted to explicit alternates (as done for the /kɪŋ/ '-king' alternates of barakqing, hayatqing, natsarqing, zakarqing).
- • **[moderate]** qorbme, paqduqing, barakqing, tikkunme, devekutqing — Semitic qoph is donor-faithfully uvular only once: qorbme /ˈqɔːrb.meɪ/ keeps /q/, but paqduqing /ˈpɑːk.duː.tʃɪŋ/ — whose own headword spells the segment with the letter q (respelled 'PAQ-doo') — renders it /k/, as do barakqing /baˈrak.t͡ɕɪŋ/ (Hebrew brq), tikkunme /tɪˈkuːn.meɪ/ (tiqqun) and devekutqing /dəˈveː.kuːt.tɕʰiŋ/ (devequt), none with a /q/ form or note. Same donor segment, opposite treatments, no flag either way.
- • **[moderate]** hastame, spandaqing, silame, tapasme, rasame, rtame, jivame, mandalame — Sanskrit/Pali short a, claimed to map donor-faithfully to /ʌ/, is split across four values: /ʌ/ in hastame /ˈhʌs.tʌ.meɪ/, spandaqing /ˈspʌn.dʌ.tɕʰiŋ/, silame /ˈsiː.lʌ.meɪ/; but /ɑː/ in tapasme /ˈtɑː.pɑːs.meɪ/ (both a's of tapas are short), rtame /ˈrɪ.tɑː.meɪ/ and jivame /ˈd͡ʒiː.vɑː.meɪ/ (final a of ṛta/jīva is short), and mandalame /mʌnˈdɑː.lʌ.meɪ/ and shamathaqing /ʃʌˈmɑː.tʌ.tɕʰiŋ/ (all a's of maṇḍala/śamatha are short). rasame /ˈrɑː.sʌ.meɪ/ gives the two identical short a's of rasa two different values inside one word, and margame's 'Sanskrit-style' alternate /ˈmɑːr.ɡɑː.meɪ/ lengthens the very short final a it claims to restore. The advertised ʌ-vs-ɑː donor length contrast does not hold.
- • **[moderate]** barakqing, zakarqing, natsarqing, halakhame, barzakhqing, hadaratme, mahabbahqing, ihsanme — The same Hebrew/Arabic a vowel surfaces as five different symbols across same-donor words: cardinal /a/ (barakqing /baˈrak.t͡ɕɪŋ/, zakarqing /zaˈkar.t͡ɕɪŋ/, natsarqing /natˈsar.t͡ɕɪŋ/, halakhame /ha.la.ˈxa.meɪ/); /ɑː/+/ɑ/ (barzakhqing /ˈbɑːr.zɑx.tɕʰiŋ/); /ʌ/ (hadaratme /hʌˈdɑː.rʌt.meɪ/); unmarked /ɑ/ (mahabbahqing /mə.ˈhɑb.bɑ.tʃɪŋ/; ihsanme /ˈiː.sɑn.meɪ/, where iḥsān's ā is donor-long but gets no length mark); and /aː/ (hayatqing /ħaˈjaːt.t͡ɕɪŋ/ — the only /aː/ token in the corpus). barak vs barzakh is the sharpest pair: same donor class, /a/ vs /ɑː/.
- • **[moderate]** heurekin, eurekame, noesisme, noemame, zoeme, agapeme, morphame — Greek ē splits between /iː/ and /eɪ/ even inside identical donor roots: heurekin /hjuˈriː.kɪn/ vs eurekame /jʊˈreɪ.kə.meɪ/ render the SAME stem heurēka with /iː/ vs /eɪ/ (and the same eu as /hju/ vs /jʊ/; heurekin's own alternate /hjʊˈreɪ.kɪn/ matches eurekame, conceding the conflict); noesisme /noʊˈiːsɪs.meɪ/ vs noemame /noʊˈeɪ.mə.meɪ/ split the same noē- root; word-final ē is /iː/ in zoeme /ˈzoʊ.iː.meɪ/ but /eɪ/ in agapeme /ə.ˈɡɑː.peɪ.meɪ/ and morphame /ˈmɔːr.feɪ.meɪ/.
- • **[moderate]** allostasisqing, anagnoristasis — The same Greek stem 'stasis' gets two vowel readings: allostasisqing /ˌæl.oʊˈsteɪ.sɪs.tɕʰiŋ/ (STAY-sis) vs anagnoristasis /ˌæn.əg.nɒr.ɪˈstæs.ɪs/ (STAS-is) — /steɪ/ vs /stæs/ for one digraph-identical donor sequence.
- • **[moderate]** autopistme, autopoieme — The same prefix auto- is transcribed two ways in its only two members: autopistme /ˌɔːtəˈpɪst.meɪ/ (/ɔː.tə/) vs autopoieme /ˌɔ.toʊ.pɔɪ.ˈeɪ.meɪ/ (/ɔ.toʊ/) — both vowels differ, and autopoieme's bare /ɔ/ is otherwise almost unattested in the corpus.
- • **[moderate]** emetme, shemme, devekutqing — Hebrew e-vowel length is assigned in inverse to the donor: emetme /ˈeː.mɛt.meɪ/ puts the corpus's donor-long /eː/ on the ultrashort hataf-segol of ʾĕmet (second segol gets /ɛ/), while shemme /ˈʃɛm.meɪ/ gives plain /ɛ/ to the tsere of šēm — the vowel class that devekutqing /dəˈveː.kuːt.tɕʰiŋ/ correctly renders /eː/. Same donor vowel → /ɛ/ in one word, /eː/ in another; and the short donor vowel is the one that got lengthened.
- • **[moderate]** sabbathme — sabbathme /ˈsʌb.əθ.meɪ/ uses /ʌ/ where the anglicized donor form 'sabbath' has /æ/, and its own respelling 'SAB-uth-may' implies /sæb/ — the corpus consistently spells /ʌ/ as U/UH (hastame 'HUS-tuh-may' /ˈhʌs/, hadaratme 'huh-DAH-rut-may' /hʌ/), so SAB ≠ /sʌb/ by the house code. IPA, respelling, and donor disagree three ways.
- • **[moderate]** ethosme, penthosme, hodosme — ethosme /ˈɛθ.ɒs.meɪ/ is respelled 'ETH-ohs-may': by the house code OH = /oʊ/, but the IPA has /ɒ/. Its family treats the identical Greek -os coda as /ɒs/ respelled 'os' (penthosme /ˈpɛn.θɒs.meɪ/ 'PEN-thos-may', hodosme /ˈhoʊ.dɒs.meɪ/ 'HOH-dos-meh'), so 'ohs' contradicts both the code and the -os pattern; a learner reading the respelling produces /oʊs/.
- • **[moderate]** anagnoristasis — anagnoristasis /ˌæn.əg.nɒr.ɪˈstæs.ɪs/ is respelled 'an-ag-nor-ee-STAS-is': respelling 'ee' encodes /iː/ where the IPA has /ɪ/ — the two layers assign different vowel values to the linking i.
- • **[moderate]** tjukurpame — tjukurpame /t͡ʃʊˈkʊr.pə.meɪ/ vs respelling 'chuh-KOOR-puh-may': the donor's two identical /ʊ/ vowels (tjukurpa) are misrendered in the respelling in opposite directions — 'chuh' (UH = /ʌ~ə/) and 'KOOR' (OO = /uː/, cf. bindume 'BIN-doo-may' /duː/, tikkunme 'tee-KOON-may' /kuːn/). The respelling layer contradicts the donor-faithful IPA twice.
- • **[moderate]** noetherme — noetherme /ˈnøː.tər.meɪ/ keeps donor German ö as /øː/ per policy, but its respelling 'NUR-tur-may' prescribes an anglicized /ɜːr/-type vowel with no alternate IPA sanctioning it — the only donor-faithful segment in the corpus whose anglicization lives in the respelling layer instead of a flagged alternate, so the two layers of the entry teach different vowels.
- · **[minor]** molkme, ondame, hotepme, eikonme, penthosme — Letter o in closed stem syllables drifts across five symbols with overlapping contexts: house norm /ɒ/ (eikonme /ˈaɪ.kɒn.meɪ/, penthosme /ˈpɛn.θɒs.meɪ/), but molkme /ˈmolk.meɪ/ carries the corpus's only bare cardinal /o/, ondame /ˈɔn.dɑː.meɪ/ its only short /ɔ/ (where /ɒn/ or /ɔːn/ is expected), and hotepme /ˈhoː.tɛp.meɪ/ uses /oː/ while its respelling 'HOH' is the same code used for hodosme's /hoʊ/.
- · **[minor]** britqing, tzimtzumme, tikkunme, rigpame, xeniame, duyuktame, walkekin — Short donor vowels are inconsistently set in cardinal vs English-lax symbols: Hebrew short i is bare /i/ in britqing /ˈbrit.tʃɪŋ/ and tzimtzumme /t͡simˈt͡sum.meɪ/ but lax /ɪ/ in tikkunme /tɪˈkuːn.meɪ/ (cf. rigpame /ˈrɪɡ.pə.meɪ/); the same Hebrew shuruk is short bare /u/ in tzimtzumme /ˈt͡sum/ but long /uː/ in tikkunme /ˈkuːn/ and devekutqing /kuːt/; xeniame /kseˈni.a.meɪ/ and duyuktame /du.juk.ta.meɪ/ are transcribed entirely in unmarked cardinal vowels; walkekin /ˈwɑl.kə.kɪn/ has the corpus's only unmarked /ɑ/ (corpus norm /ɑː/ or /ɔː/).
- · **[minor]** tzimtzumme, natsarqing — Hebrew tsade is an affricate /t͡s/ in tzimtzumme /t͡simˈt͡sum.meɪ/ but a heterosyllabic cluster /t.s/ in natsarqing /natˈsar.t͡ɕɪŋ/ — same donor segment, affricate vs split-cluster treatment.
- · **[minor]** chayimme, halakhame, barzakhqing — chayimme's note declares 'Hebraic chet /χ/' (uvular) but its own IPA — and every other chet word — uses velar /x/: chayimme /xaˈjiːm.meɪ/, halakhame /ha.la.ˈxa.meɪ/, barzakhqing /ˈbɑːr.zɑx.tɕʰiŋ/. Transcription is internally consistent; the declared donor symbol is not.
- · **[minor]** jivame, jinsme, drujme, tjukurpame, wiconime, britqing — Affricate tie-bar notation drifts for the same segments: /d͡ʒ/ only in jivame /ˈd͡ʒiː.vɑː.meɪ/ vs plain /dʒ/ in jinsme /ˈdʒɪnz.meɪ/, drujme /ˈdrʌdʒ.meɪ/, indrajalame; /t͡ʃ/ in tjukurpame /t͡ʃʊˈkʊr.pə.meɪ/ vs /tʃ/ in wiconime /ˈwiː.tʃoʊ.niː.meɪ/ and britqing /ˈbrit.tʃɪŋ/; same split inside the qing family (/t͡ɕ/ vs /tɕ/).
- · **[minor]** oriance — oriance /ˈɔː.ɹi.əns/ contains the corpus's only /ɹ/; every other entry writes the same rhotic as /r/ (heurekin /hjuˈriː.kɪn/, prehensionme /prɪˈhɛn.ʃən.meɪ/, etc.).
- · **[minor]** daome — daome's scholarly alternate /tâʊ.meɪ/ carries a falling-tone circumflex — the only tone notation anywhere in the corpus; none of the 23 Mandarin qing transcriptions mark the rising tone of qíng, so the donor feature 'tone' is applied exactly once and inconsistently.
- · **[minor]** nommome, nyamame — IPA reduces the medial vowel to /ə/ while the respellings keep a full vowel: nommome /ˈnɒməmeɪ/ respelled 'NOM-mo-may' (mo ≠ /mə/), nyamame /ˈnjɑːməmeɪ/ respelled 'NYA-mah-may' (AH = /ɑː/ ≠ /ə/) — the two layers assign different vowel values, and both donors (nommo, nyama) have full final-stem vowels.

## Lens: stress and respelling consistency

- ▲ **[major]** wiconime — IPA /ˈwiː.tʃoʊ.niː.meɪ/ marks primary stress on the FIRST syllable, but the respelling "wee-CHOH-nee-meh" caps the SECOND. The two layers directly contradict each other. (Lakota wičhóni has second-syllable stress, so the respelling is likely right and the IPA stress mark misplaced.)
- • **[moderate]** jeongqing — /t͡ɕʌŋˈt͡ɕɪŋ/ (jung-CHING) puts primary stress on the qing suffix itself — the only one of 23 qing-words to do so; every other entry treats the suffix as extrametrical (e.g. zakarqing /zaˈkar.t͡ɕɪŋ/, barzakhqing /ˈbɑːr.zɑx.tɕʰiŋ/). Sole counterexample to the corpus-wide suffix-unstressed rule.
- • **[moderate]** orthophanes, kallophanes — The only two -phanes members flip primary/secondary stress: orthophanes /ˌɔːr.θoʊˈfæn.iːz/ (or-tho-PHAN-ees, primary on the suffix-adjacent syllable) vs kallophanes /ˈkæl.oʊˌfæn.iːz/ (primary on the stem). Kallophanes additionally caps its SECONDARY syllable, "KAL-lo-PHAN-ees", breaking the ALL-CAPS-=-primary convention (contrast lowercase secondaries in "auto-PIST-meh", "kal-lo-DOX-a", "or-tho-PHAN-ees").
- • **[moderate]** artiance, candence, oriance — artiance /ɑːrˈtaɪ.əns/ (ar-TI-ance) stresses the syllable immediately before -ance, while its family-mates keep stem-initial stress: candence /ˈkæn.dəns/, oriance /ˈɔː.ɹi.əns/, panimaance /pəˈniː.mɑː.əns/. The donor stem (Greek árti) is initial-stressed, so the shift to /ˈtaɪ/ contradicts both the donor and the -ance family pattern.
- • **[moderate]** emetme, devekutqing, shevirame, halakhame, chayimme, tikkunme — Hebrew loans are split on donor stress. halakhame /ha.la.ˈxa.meɪ/, chayimme /xaˈjiːm.meɪ/, tikkunme /tɪˈkuːn.meɪ/, barakqing /baˈrak.t͡ɕɪŋ/, natsarqing /natˈsar.t͡ɕɪŋ/, zakarqing /zaˈkar.t͡ɕɪŋ/ all preserve Hebrew final (milra) stress, but emetme /ˈeː.mɛt.meɪ/, devekutqing /dəˈveː.kuːt.tɕʰiŋ/, shevirame /ʃəˈviːr.ʌ.meɪ/ retract it (Hebrew emét, devekút, shevirá are final-stressed). emetme's own note even claims "emet-stress on first syllable from Hebrew preserved" — true only of Ashkenazi pronunciation, while the rest of the corpus uses Sephardi/Modern stress, so the donor-stress policy is applied inconsistently within one donor language.
- • **[moderate]** eranosme, zoeme, palamasme — Greek donors anglicized without the legitimizing "English pronunciation preserved" flag that eikonme/glossame/mathemame carry: eranosme /ɛˈræ.nɒs.meɪ/ (eh-RAN-os-meh) vs Greek éranos (antepenult, also antepenult by English classical rule); zoeme /ˈzoʊ.iː.meɪ/ (ZOH-ee-meh) vs Greek zōḗ (final stress); palamasme /pʌˈlɑː.mʌs.meɪ/ vs Greek Palamâs (final stress). Contrast agapeme /ə.ˈɡɑː.peɪ.meɪ/ and xeniame /kseˈni.a.meɪ/, which keep donor stress, and hodosme, which at least records the classical alt /hoʊˈdɒs.meɪ/. (syzygyqing /sɪˈzɪɡ.i.tʃɪŋ/ is borderline: penult vs English donor "syzygy" /ˈsɪz.ɪ.dʒi/, though a Latin-style syzygia reading would support it.)
- • **[moderate]** mandalame, shamathaqing, tjukurpame, wairuaqing, darshanqing — Penult attraction overrides donor-initial stress in several loans, against the stated donor-stress rule: mandalame /mʌnˈdɑː.lʌ.meɪ/ (Skt maṇḍala — antepenult, all short a) and shamathaqing /ʃʌˈmɑː.tʌ.tɕʰiŋ/ (śamatha — antepenult) both shift to penult and invent a long /ɑː/; tjukurpame /t͡ʃʊˈkʊr.pə.meɪ/ (Pitjantjatjara has word-initial stress); wairuaqing /waɪˈruː.ʌ.tɕʰiŋ/ (Māori stress rules put it on the wai- diphthong); darshanqing /darˈʃan.t͡ɕɪŋ/ (Hindi/Skt dárśan is initial-stressed). Contrast donor-faithful hastame /ˈhʌs.tʌ.meɪ/, tapasme /ˈtɑː.pɑːs.meɪ/, rasame /ˈrɑː.sʌ.meɪ/, spandaqing /ˈspʌn.dʌ.tɕʰiŋ/, mauriqing /ˈmaʊ.ri.tɕʰiŋ/ — the same donor families handled both ways.
- • **[moderate]** ihsanme, sabilme, hayatqing — ihsanme /ˈiː.sɑn.meɪ/ (EE-san-meh) takes initial stress although Arabic iḥsān is stressed on the final superheavy syllable — the corpus's other Arabic loans preserve exactly that: sabilme /saˈbiːl.meɪ/, hayatqing /ħaˈjaːt.t͡ɕɪŋ/, mahabbahqing /mə.ˈhɑb.bɑ.tʃɪŋ/.
- • **[moderate]** visageqing — Respelling "vi-ZHAZH-ching" gives the stressed syllable a /ʒ/ onset, but the IPA /viˈzɑːʒ.tɕʰiŋ/ has /z/ (matching French visage /viˈzaʒ/). A learner following the respelling says /ʒɑːʒ/; the stressed syllable's respelling disagrees with its own IPA.
- · **[minor]** ifeqing, nommome, nyamame, autopistme, noesisme — Missing syllable dots break the count-matching convention: ifeqing /ˈiːfɛtʃɪŋ/, nommome /ˈnɒməmeɪ/, nyamame /ˈnjɑːməmeɪ/ are fully undotted despite declared "3 syllables"; autopistme /ˌɔːtəˈpɪst.meɪ/ lacks the dot in /ɔːtə/ (and its respelling "auto-PIST-meh" merges two syllables into one hyphen unit, 3 units vs declared 4); noesisme /noʊˈiːsɪs.meɪ/ lacks the dot in /iːsɪs/ vs declared 4 (its alt /noʊˈɛsɪs.meɪ/ repeats the omission). Side effects: ifeqing's stressed syllable is respelled bare "I" (house code reads that as EYE /aɪ/, IPA says /iː/), and nommome/nyamame respell IPA /mə/ as "mo"/"mah".
- · **[minor]** daome, duyuktame — No primary stress mark in the IPA at all — daome /daʊ.meɪ/ and duyuktame /du.juk.ta.meɪ/ — although their respellings cap DOW-meh and doo-YOOK-tah-may. The respelling asserts a stress the IPA never marks.
- · **[minor]** doxakallos, kallodoxa — doxakallos /ˈdɒk.sə.ˈkæl.ɒs/ carries two primary stress marks and double-caps its respelling "DOX-a-KAL-los", while its mirror compound kallodoxa /ˌkæl.oʊˈdɒk.sə/ (kal-lo-DOX-a) uses the house ˌ…ˈ pattern with lowercase secondary. Same morphemes, two notations.
- · **[minor]** britqing — Declared "3 syllables" but both the IPA /ˈbrit.tʃɪŋ/ and the respelling "BRIT-ching" have exactly 2 (the count looks like a leftover from a disyllabic Hebrew berît reading that the transcription doesn't use).
- · **[minor]** ahavame — Declared "3-4 syllables" although the IPA /ə.ˈhɑː.və.meɪ/ and respelling "a-HAH-va-meh" both show exactly 4 — the only hedged count in the corpus.
- · **[minor]** nepsisme, noesisme, agapeme — The identical suffix /meɪ/ is respelled three ways corpus-wide: "meh" (42 entries), "may" (31), and "me" (1) — e.g. nepsisme /ˈnɛp.sɪs.meɪ/ "NEP-sis-may" vs noesisme /noʊˈiːsɪs.meɪ/ "no-EE-sis-meh" vs agapeme /ə.ˈɡɑː.peɪ.meɪ/ "a-GA-pe-me" (where bare "me" invites /miː/).
- · **[minor]** walkekin — Respelling "WAL-kə-kin" (IPA /ˈwɑl.kə.kɪn/) leaks the IPA schwa character into the respelling layer, which elsewhere uses reader-friendly "uh"/"a" (e.g. "chuh-KOOR-puh-may", "GLOS-uh-meh").
- · **[minor]** ahavame, autopoieme, complerescence, doxakallos, halakhame, liturgiame, mahabbahqing — Stress-mark notation drift: these seven write a dot AND a stress mark at the same boundary (/ə.ˈhɑː.və.meɪ/, /ˌɔ.toʊ.pɔɪ.ˈeɪ.meɪ/, /ˌkɒm.plə.ˈrɛs.əns/, /ˈdɒk.sə.ˈkæl.ɒs/, /ha.la.ˈxa.meɪ/, /lɪ.ˈtɜː.dʒi.ə.meɪ/, /mə.ˈhɑb.bɑ.tʃɪŋ/), while the rest of the corpus lets ˈ/ˌ replace the dot (e.g. /tɪˈkuːn.meɪ/). Purely cosmetic but inconsistent.
- · **[minor]** noetherme — Stressed-syllable respelling anglicizes what the IPA keeps donor-faithful: /ˈnøː.tər.meɪ/ respelled "NUR-tur-may" — NUR encodes /ɜː~ɝ/, not /øː/, so the two layers teach different vowels for the stressed syllable.
